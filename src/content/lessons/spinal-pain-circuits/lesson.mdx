techniques:
  - name: "Intersectional Cre/Flp toxin ablation"
    blurb: >-
      Two genetic drivers restrict a diphtheria-toxin or toxin-receptor allele to cells that
      expressed both, so one genetically captured dorsal-horn population can be removed and the
      animal's behaviour measured weeks later. It tests necessity for the assay used, and the
      label is a lineage: cells that expressed the driver at some time, not cells expressing the
      marker now.
  - name: "Tetanus toxin light chain silencing"
    blurb: >-
      A viral vector delivers tetanus toxin light chain, which prevents vesicular transmitter
      release from the targeted neurons. The cell survives, which separates loss of output from
      loss of the cell, but the block is persistent rather than a rapid reversible interruption
      of firing.
  - name: "Chemogenetic activation and inhibition"
    blurb: >-
      An engineered receptor such as hM3Dq or hM4D is expressed in a defined population and
      driven by a systemic ligand, usually clozapine-N-oxide. Because CNO is converted to
      clozapine, which occupies these receptors with higher affinity in rodent experiments, a
      receptor-free ligand-treated control is part of the method rather than an extra.
  - name: "Gramicidin-perforated-patch recording"
    blurb: >-
      A pore-forming antibiotic gives electrical access to a neuron while leaving its own
      chloride concentration intact, so the anion reversal potential measured is the cell's and
      not the pipette's. It is the endpoint that turns "inhibition failed" into a number.

The first film walked through the touch-gate circuit in mice. Here we map the dorsal-horn layers, test what each tool can prove, and follow inhibition into the target cell.

**Allodynia** is pain from a normally innocuous stimulus such as light touch; animal studies report mechanical hypersensitivity or **allodynia-like behaviour**. **Nocifensive behaviour** is a defensive response to a noxious stimulus. **Disinhibition** means weakened inhibitory control.

## Map the dorsal horn before testing its gates

In 1965, Melzack and Wall proposed that cutaneous input is modulated in the spinal cord before pain perception and response, without naming cell types. Picture three dorsal-horn layers:

- **Lamina I (superficial):** contains sparse **projection neurons** that send output to the brain. In adult mouse lumbar L4, about 5 percent of lamina I neurons were estimated to project to the lateral parabrachial area.
- **Lamina II:** local excitatory and inhibitory interneurons—the relays and brakes between touch and pain.
- **Deeper dorsal horn:** many large, myelinated **A-beta low-threshold touch afferents** terminate in laminae III–IV. Some subtypes also send branches to neighbouring laminae.

In one route studied in mice, inhibitory interneurons limit how strongly A-beta touch input recruits excitatory relays that can drive lamina I nociceptive output. Modern genetics can remove, silence, or activate defined populations, but every claim stays bounded by its tool.

> **The guiding idea.** A spinal gate can fail when inhibitory output falls or the target's anion response shifts; behaviour alone cannot separate them.
>
> *Scope:* interpreting matched behavioural, anatomical, and electrophysiological endpoints in the rodent dorsal-horn preparations tested here; mechanisms can coexist; an open anion conductance can shunt concurrent input even when it depolarizes the cell, and its net effect on firing depends on reversal potential, timing and the other inputs; and pathways are not assumed obligatory across injuries, species, or sexes.

[Touch and pain](/lessons/touch-and-pain/) develops the 1965 proposal, spinothalamic crossing and central sensitization. [Dorsal horn touch](/lessons/dorsal-horn-touch/) covers where low-threshold afferents end.

## Build one circuit, then test the tool

The first film showed the somatostatin-relay and dynorphin-gate results from Duan and colleagues (Cell, 2014); its 5 to 40 percent spiking jump came from dorsal-root-attached lumbar slices of young mice in neurons sampled by location near the lamina II–III border, whereas in vivo brush-evoked c-Fos showed increased recruitment of somatostatin-positive neurons after dynorphin-lineage ablation.

Duan and colleagues used **intersectional Cre/Flp mouse genetics**, pairing a marker-specific Cre driver with Lbx1-Flp so doubly recombined neurons could express the diphtheria toxin receptor and be ablated by toxin; reporter counts four weeks later confirmed depletion. A Cre **lineage** records expression history, not current expression: detectable dynorphin messenger RNA appeared in about three-quarters of labelled cells, and the authors cautioned expression was probably transient in a minority. Killing captured cells tests the population's contribution, not the peptide's action or only current dynorphin-expressing cells.

Reversible tools in freely moving mice sharpen that claim (Christensen and colleagues, Cell Reports, 2016): the first film showed that activating spinal somatostatin neurons elicited nocifensive behaviour and suppressing them raised mechanical withdrawal thresholds and lengthened thermal withdrawal latency in naive mice. The study used **optogenetic activation** (light-driven channelrhodopsin) for activation and **chemogenetic inhibition** (an inhibitory engineered receptor with a systemic ligand) for suppression; slice spike-train checks validated the optical stimulation (*Going deeper*).

### Worked problem: how complete was the ablation?

Duan and colleagues counted labelled somatostatin-lineage neurons per sampled lumbar hemisection four weeks after toxin: 105 ± 4 in controls and 17 ± 2 after ablation (means ± SEM, 15 to 17 hemisections, three mice per group).

**Predict.** What depletion do the two means give, and what is the independent biological unit?

<details>
<summary>Check your prediction</summary>

Depletion = 100 × (105 − 17) / 105 = 100 × 88 / 105, about 84 percent (the paper reports 82 percent; extra decimals overclaim precision from rounded means).

The independent biological unit is the mouse (three per group), with hemisections nested within each animal; section-level SEMs cannot supply animal-level uncertainty without per-mouse values. Seventeen cells remain—roughly one-sixth of controls—measuring labelled cells removed, not pain removed.

## More than one excitatory route

Peirs and colleagues (Neuron, 2015) found another excitatory bridge in dorsal-horn neurons transiently expressing **VGLUT3** (vesicular glutamate transporter 3, which loads glutamate into vesicles). Developmental deletion of *Vglut3* in the dorsal horn reduced responses to acute noxious pressure and reduced mechanical hypersensitivity after paw **carrageenan** (an **inflammatory** paw model) or spared nerve injury (**neuropathic** pain) in mice. Chemogenetic activation of either the transient-VGLUT3 lineage or adult lamina II **calretinin** interneurons produced allodynia-like behaviour. Keep those claims separate: deleting a transient transporter tests a developmental requirement, whereas activating adult calretinin neurons is the cleaner adult sufficiency test (postnatal timing in *Going deeper*).

The same group then showed in male and female mice that, in the mouse models and mechanical assays tested, different injuries recruited overlapping sets of excitatory interneurons (Neuron, 2021):

| Excitatory population | Location | Mechanical allodynia role |
|---|---|---|
| **Calretinin** | Lamina II inner | Inflammatory allodynia |
| **PKC-gamma** (protein kinase C gamma) | Dorsal horn | Neuropathic allodynia |
| **CCK** (cholecystokinin) | Deeper laminae III–IV | Both inflammatory and neuropathic |

There is no invariant allodynia chain.

## Inhibitory populations are not interchangeable

As the first film showed, parvalbumin activation changed mechanical without thermal sensitivity, whereas glycinergic manipulations moved mechanical, heat and cold together. Here is the circuit and tool logic:

- **Parvalbumin interneurons (mechanical filter):** In male mice, parvalbumin ablation produced mechanical allodynia-like behaviour, and pharmacological evidence implicated PKC-gamma interneurons, while activation relieved nerve-injury mechanical hypersensitivity (Petitjean and colleagues, Cell Reports, 2015). Boyle and colleagues (Cell Reports, 2019) showed they act largely by presynaptic inhibition of myelinated low-threshold afferent terminals and inhibition of lamina II excitatory interneurons feeding lamina I.
- **Broad glycinergic interneurons (multimodal brake):** In male **GlyT2-Cre** mice (glycine transporter 2), Foster and colleagues (Neuron, 2015) found that local ablation or **tetanus-toxin-light-chain silencing** (blocking vesicular release in surviving cells) caused mechanical, heat and cold hypersensitivity, while chemogenetic activation ameliorated neuropathic hyperalgesia after chronic constriction injury.

**Predict.** Tetanus toxin light chain leaves the targeted neuron alive, but its block is persistent. Can this experiment show that the neuron's output is required at the precise moment of testing?

<details>
<summary>Check your prediction</summary>

No. It shows that losing transmitter output across the persistent block changes behaviour; because it is not a rapid reversible interruption of firing, it cannot isolate the exact moment output is needed.

</details>

| Tool | What it changes | Key boundary |
|---|---|---|
| **Cre/Flp toxin ablation** | Kills lineage-captured cells | Includes transient expression; irreversible |
| **Tetanus toxin light chain** | Blocks vesicular release in live cells | Persistent, not rapidly reversible |
| **Opto- or chemogenetics** | Alters activity on a timescale set by the tool | Verify the activity change and include appropriate light or ligand controls |
| **Developmental gene deletion** | Disrupts a gene during development | Can alter later circuitry and cannot by itself isolate acute adult function |

Now shift to injury mechanism. As the first film noted, permanent parvalbumin reporter counts did not fall significantly at 3, 5 or 8 weeks after spared nerve injury. Petitjean and colleagues found fewer parvalbumin terminal appositions on PKC-gamma cell bodies after spared nerve injury (noting detachment's contribution to allodynia was unknown), whereas Boyle and colleagues detected no significant change in their measurements of somatic and dendritic parvalbumin inputs to PKC-gamma cells and found reduced excitability of surviving parvalbumin cells.

## When inhibition changes sign

Stable gatekeepers raise a second question: can the target neuron change what an intact inhibitory synapse does?

<LessonFilm film={frontmatter.films[0]} />

The film introduced the rat lamina I result and the microglial pathway. The studies add details about the nerve-injury model, the interventions used to test sufficiency and reversal, and the findings in female mice.

Coull and colleagues (Nature, 2003) recorded from acute parasagittal lamina I slices in adult male Sprague-Dawley rats with a sciatic nerve cuff. Nerve injury reduced dorsal-horn expression of **KCC2**, a cotransporter that extrudes potassium and chloride in these neurons. The altered anion gradient shifted the anion reversal potential (`E_anion`) in the depolarizing direction. Lamina I recordings showed that the shift could make normally inhibitory synaptic input increase firing. Opening anion channels lowers input resistance and can **shunt** concurrent inputs even if it depolarizes the cell at rest; with sustained input in a passive membrane, it pulls the voltage below the excitation-only level if `E_anion` lies below that level, while the net effect on firing depends on the circuit and timing ([Synaptic integration](/lessons/synaptic-integration/)).

Next came sufficiency.

**Predict.** If lowering KCC2 is sufficient, what should spinal KCC2 blockade or knockdown do in otherwise intact rats?

<details>
<summary>Check your prediction</summary>

It should lower nociceptive thresholds without requiring nerve injury. That is what the experiments found, making the chloride change sufficient here rather than merely correlated with injury.

</details>

In Coull and colleagues (Nature, 2005), intrathecal transfer of ATP-stimulated rat microglia into uninjured adult male rats reproduced both the depolarizing `E_anion` shift and behavioural change, and pretreating those cells with **BDNF**-directed (brain-derived neurotrophic factor) interfering RNA inhibited both. Blocking BDNF–**TrkB** signalling *reversed* nerve-injury allodynia and the anion-potential shift in injured rats, tying the pathway to injury rather than transfer alone ([Trk and p75](/lessons/trk-and-p75/); [microglia and complement pruning](/lessons/microglia-complement-pruning/)).

Two limits bound this pathway. It was established in adult male rats, whereas Sorge and colleagues (Nature Neuroscience, 2015) found microglia were not required for mechanical hypersensitivity in immunocompetent female mice in the models tested, implicating adaptive immune cells—likely T lymphocytes—instead. Whether this sex difference is general remains debated because other studies find blocking microglial signalling relieves nerve-injury pain in both sexes. A mechanism can be real in one preparation without being obligatory across species or sexes.

## The output neurons and the denominator trap

Dorsal-horn circuits converge on sparse lamina I **projection neurons**. Cameron and colleagues (Pain, 2015) injected a **retrograde tracer** into the lateral parabrachial area of adult mice of either sex and examined labelled spinal cells after fixation. They estimated that about 5 percent of lamina I neurons in lumbar L4 on the side opposite the injection projected to that area. About 90 percent of traced lamina I **spinoparabrachial** neurons expressed **NK1r**, the substance-P receptor encoded by *Tacr1*.

**Predict.** Does that 90 percent mean that most NK1r-positive lamina I cells project to the parabrachial nucleus?

<details>
<summary>Check your prediction</summary>

No. Ninety percent is the fraction of *traced projection neurons* expressing NK1r, not the fraction of *NK1r-positive cells* that project. In an illustrative 100 lamina I neurons, 5 project and 90 percent of those is 4.5 cells; if 15 of the 95 interneurons also express NK1r, projection neurons are under a quarter (4.5 of 19.5) of NK1r-positive cells. Sheahan and colleagues (Journal of Neuroscience, 2020) found that most genetically targeted superficial *Tacr1-CreER* neurons were interneurons. Different denominators make both results compatible.

</details>

Roome and colleagues (Cell Reports, 2020) added a developmental handle: embryonic *Phox2a* expression captured adult anterolateral-system projection neurons. In adults of both sexes aged 6 to 19 weeks, intersectional tracing showed *Phox2a*-lineage neurons reaching parabrachial and thalamic targets. Human fetal spinal cords at gestational weeks 7.3 to 8.4 showed Phox2a molecular identity by immunohistochemistry—fixed-tissue correspondence, not adult human physiology.

**Predict.** Developmental *Phox2a* loss reduces supraspinal nocifensive responses while measured spinal nociceptive reflexes remain. Does that establish an acute adult function?

<details>
<summary>Check your prediction</summary>

No. The pattern fits a thinned ascending limb with local reflexes intact, but developmental deletion cannot measure what acute adult interruption would do.

</details>

Do not turn parabrachial and thalamic targets into exclusive affect and sensation channels: tracing shows the lineage reaches both without assigning exclusive roles. Lee and colleagues (Nature Communications, 2025) found *Calcrl*-positive projection neurons carrying cool-specific signals to the lateral parabrachial nucleus in mice, showing that route is not pain-only ([Chronic pain](/lessons/chronic-pain/); [itch](/lessons/itch/) uses partly separate populations in the same circuitry).

### Design problem: where did inhibition fail?

Remember the rule: behaviour measures hypersensitivity, and matched circuit measurements can test for cell loss, weak inhibitory output and altered anion reversal in a candidate pathway. Using the second film's three idealized patterns, design a nerve-injury test that probes them.

<details>
<summary>One way to do it</summary>

| Idealized change in the tested connection | Viable permanently labelled cells | Evoked transmission at matched driving force | Gramicidin reversal |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Cell loss alone** | Fewer | Often reduced | Similar in recorded targets |
| **Weaker recruitment or release with survival** | Similar | Reduced under stimulation that probes the affected step | Similar |
| **Anion reversal shift alone** | Similar | Conductance still detectable away from reversal | More positive |

Compare matched injury and sham groups. More than one change can occur in the same preparation.

*Survival.* Permanently label the inhibitory population before injury so counting does not depend on continued expression of its original marker. Compare counts of viable labelled neurons in matched injury and sham tissue.

*Output.* Start with a verified parvalbumin (PV) connection onto identified PKC-gamma or lamina II vertical cells, and measure evoked anionic transmission at several holding potentials in matched injury and sham preparations. Measure how PV neurons respond to afferent input as well as how much transmission follows matched PV activation. Use an independent measure of release or contacts before attributing a transmission deficit to either.

*Postsynaptic effect.* Measure `E_anion` for the relevant receptor-mediated response in the verified target with a **gramicidin-perforated patch** to preserve native chloride. Whole-cell recording can dialyse intracellular chloride and mask an injury-related shift. Compare reversal with resting potential and test whether the evoked conductance increases or decreases firing under matched input.

Compare matched injured and sham animals. Use acute reversible silencing in adults to test the population's contribution during behaviour, and check motor function independently. Test causal contributions to the injury phenotype by restoring the altered circuit property and checking the circuit and behavioural readouts. Collect all three endpoints in matched preparations because mechanisms can coexist, and never read smaller current amplitude at one holding potential alone as lost release, since amplitude also drops when driving force shrinks.

</details>

## Going deeper

### Developmental timing, stimulation, dosing and control details

In Peirs and colleagues (2015), dorsal-horn VGLUT3 immunoreactivity appeared around postnatal day 5, peaked around days 10 to 12, and was much reduced by about day 20. Their transient-VGLUT3 vector was injected at postnatal day 10 and behaviour tested three weeks later, making adult lamina II calretinin activation the cleaner adult sufficiency test.

In Christensen and colleagues (2016), channelrhodopsin-expressing somatostatin neurons followed 1 to 10 Hz light trains (10 ms pulses) in slices, with spike probability falling at higher frequencies. Petitjean and colleagues (2015) used male mice aged 8 to 14 weeks; Foster and colleagues (2015) used GlyT2-Cre mice aged 7 to 8 weeks at lumbar injection and clozapine-N-oxide at 1 mg/kg intraperitoneally.

For chemogenetics, include receptor-free animals given the same ligand: clozapine-N-oxide converts to clozapine, which occupies these receptors with higher affinity in rodents, and saline in receptor-expressing animals misses off-target drug effects.

Light-driven silencing has its own hazard: chloride-pumping silencers can leave an after-effect on reversal potential (reported in rat hippocampal slices and absent with a proton pump), altering the endpoint this design measures.
