## Explain: particles spread from more to less

<WordBank terms={['concentration', 'diffusion', 'passive transport', 'cell membrane', 'selectively permeable', 'osmosis', 'homeostasis']} />

Living things must move materials into, out of and within their cells to support life processes. Put one drop of food colouring in a glass of still water. At first the colour sits in one spot. Slowly it spreads until the whole glass is the same colour.

The dye spreads because its particles are always moving at random. **Concentration** is how much of a substance is in a given space. Where the drop landed, the concentration of dye is high. Far away, it is low. More particles happen to move out of the crowded spot than move into it.

**Diffusion** is the movement of a substance from where it is more concentrated to where it is less concentrated. In a cell, diffusion means a substance other than water crossing the cell membrane.

When the colour is even, the particles do not stop. They keep moving, but just as many move one way as the other. So the colour stops changing.

Your cells use oxygen and give off carbon dioxide (more in a later lesson). Oxygen is more concentrated in the blood than inside a cell, so it diffuses in. Carbon dioxide is more concentrated inside, so it diffuses out.

**Passive transport** is movement across a cell membrane that needs no energy from the cell. Diffusion and osmosis are both passive transport.

<Support min={2}>**Key idea:** Particles spread on their own from more concentrated to less concentrated, and that is diffusion.</Support>

## Explain: water crosses a choosy membrane

The **cell membrane** is the thin layer around every cell ([that lesson](/lessons/inside-a-cell/)). It is **selectively permeable**: it lets some substances through and holds back others. Water, oxygen and carbon dioxide cross it easily. Many dissolved particles, such as salt and sugar, cross much less easily.

**Osmosis** is the movement of water across a cell membrane. Here is how to predict its direction.

- Dissolve salt or sugar in water, and the water there is less concentrated.
- Water moves toward the side with more dissolved salt or sugar.
- Many of the dissolved particles cannot cross the membrane, so the water keeps moving that way.

|---|---|---|
| Diffusion | A substance other than water | Oxygen into a cell |
| Osmosis | Water | Water out of a salted cucumber |

Now the cucumber makes sense. Salt on the outside means water outside is less concentrated, so water leaves the cells. In the lettuce, the inside of each cell has more dissolved particles than plain water. Water moves in. In plants, the stiff cell wall and the large central vacuole help keep a well-watered plant firm ([that lesson](/lessons/plant-and-animal-cells/)).

**Homeostasis** is keeping conditions inside a living thing steady. The membrane helps. By letting some things pass and not others, it helps keep the right amounts of water and other substances inside the cell.

A model is never the real thing. Our model membrane holds back all the salt. Real membranes let some salt through, but much more slowly than water.

<Support min={2}>**Key idea:** In osmosis, water crosses the membrane toward the side with more dissolved particles, and the cell spends no energy on it.</Support>

### Design problem

Plan an investigation: how does the amount of salt in water change the mass of potato pieces? Name your variables, constants, control and number of trials, and say how you will stay safe.

<details>
<summary>One way to do it</summary>

- **Question:** How does the amount of salt change the percent change in mass of potato pieces?
- **Independent variable:** grams of salt in each 100 mL of water, for example 0, 1, 2, 4 and 6 g.
- **Dependent variable:** the percent change in mass of each piece after 30 minutes.
- **Constants:** one potato, same-size pieces, 100 mL of liquid per cup, the same time, temperature and patting method.
- **Control:** the cup with 0 g of salt, which is plain water.
- **Trials:** three pieces in each cup. Find each piece's percent change, then the mean.
- **Measure:** mass in grams on a balance, before and after, patted dry each time.
- **Safety:** an adult cuts the potato. Label every cup, wipe up spills, and never taste anything.

If pieces gain mass in plain water and lose more as the salt goes up, that fits osmosis.

</details>
