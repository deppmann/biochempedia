    label: "Like I'm 5"
    text: "When you eat more food than your body needs right now, your body doesn't throw the extra away. It saves it as fat, like packing snacks for later.   - level: "highschool"
    label: "High school"
    text: "Fatty acid synthesis is how a well-fed cell turns extra food energy into fat. It happens in the cytoplasm and is not just the breakdown pathway run backward. First the cell ships acetyl CoA out of the mitochondria disguised as citrate. Then a key enzyme, ACC, adds CO2 to make malonyl CoA, which is the committed step: ACC1's malonyl CoA is destined for fat. A big multi-tool enzyme called fatty acid synthase then runs a four-step cycle (condense, reduce, dehydrate, reduce) seven times, adding two carbons each round, until it builds a sixteen-carbon fatty acid called palmitate. It uses NADPH as the power source, not the NADH the breakdown pathway makes."
  - level: "college"
    label: "College"
    text: "Fatty acid synthesis builds palmitate from acetyl CoA in the cytoplasm and differs from beta-oxidation in three ways: location (cytoplasm vs mitochondrion), carrier (ACP-phosphopantetheine vs CoA), and reducing equivalent (NADPH consumed vs NADH/FADH2 produced), which permits independent regulation. Stage 1: the citrate shuttle exports acetyl CoA (ATP-citrate lyase) and, via cytoplasmic malate dehydrogenase (MDH) plus malic enzyme, can generate up to 8 of the 14 required NADPH; the pentose phosphate pathway supplies the rest. Stage 2: acetyl CoA carboxylase, a biotin enzyme, carboxylates acetyl CoA to malonyl CoA at the cost of one ATP, the committed step. Stage 3: fatty acid synthase runs the CRDR cycle (condensation driven by decarboxylation of the malonyl unit, NADPH reduction, dehydration, NADPH reduction) seven times to reach C16. Net: 8 acetyl CoA + 7 ATP + 14 NADPH + 7 HCO3⁻ yield palmitate + 7 CO2 + 8 CoA + 14 NADP⁺ + 7 ADP + 7 Pᵢ + 6 H2O. ACC is regulated by phosphorylation (AMPK off, PP2A on), allostery (citrate on, palmitoyl CoA off), and hormones (insulin on, glucagon off), and its product malonyl CoA inhibits CPT-I to block simultaneous oxidation."
  - level: "grad"
    label: "Graduate"
    text: "The architecture of fatty acid synthesis is a study in compartmentalized metabolic control. The cytoplasm/mitochondrion split, the ACP/CoA carrier distinction, and the NADPH/NADH currency separation together insulate anabolic flux from catabolic flux, eliminating a thermodynamically wasteful futile cycle and permitting opposed regulation. The committed enzyme, ACC, is the integration node: its biotin-dependent carboxylation is reversibly inactivated by AMPK phosphorylation and reactivated by PP2A, allosterically polymerized into active filaments by citrate and depolymerized by long-chain acyl-CoA feedback, and hormonally set by the insulin/glucagon axis through these same effectors. Chain elongation runs on the malonyl decarboxylation. Direct Claisen condensation of two acetyl-CoA is endergonic, so the cell invests ATP in a carboxylation whose CO2 is then lost during condensation, coupling an exergonic decarboxylation to C-C bond formation. ACP's phosphopantetheine tether enforces substrate channeling across the FAS dimer throughout. The reciprocal CPT-I inhibition by malonyl CoA (notably via ACC2) hardwires the synthesis/oxidation switch, and the AMPK node accounts for part of metformin's action: Complex I inhibition can raise AMP:ATP, activating AMPK to phosphorylate ACC, suppress lipogenesis and derepress fatty acid oxidation, while metformin's acute suppression of hepatic glucose output appears to work largely through AMPK-independent routes that are still debated. The stereochemical inversion (D-3-hydroxyacyl in synthesis vs L in mitochondrial oxidation) and the NADPH dependence are mechanistic fingerprints showing that synthesis uses its own enzymes, carrier, and intermediates at every step."

**One cookie. One slice of pizza. If you don't burn it, your body stores it.** Most of the fat in it is stored as it is. The sugar and starch refill glycogen first, and some of the surplus is turned into new fat by this pathway, running mainly in your liver and fat cells. That carbon becomes acetyl CoA, and in the fed state acetyl CoA is stitched into long fatty acid chains two carbons at a time.

This lesson answers two questions. What does it cost to build one fatty acid? And how does a cell that is building fat avoid burning it at the same time? The film above gives the route in three stages, transport, activation and elongation; the sections below use the same names and add the bill.

> **The one idea to carry through this lesson.** After the first acetyl group, every two carbons are added as one malonyl CoA, and each one has the same price: one ATP, spent at acetyl CoA carboxylase to attach a carboxyl group that will leave again as CO₂, and two NADPH, spent on fatty acid synthase to reduce the new C=O all the way to CH₂. Because malonyl CoA is the only way carbon gets onto the growing chain, the enzyme that makes it is where the cell decides whether to build fat, and malonyl CoA itself is the signal that shuts the door on burning fat: it blocks CPT-I, the gate long-chain fat must pass to be burned in the mitochondrion.

The rule covers saturated chains built from acetyl CoA already in the cytoplasm. Delivering the acetyl groups and finding the NADPH are separate bills, paid later. Odd-chain fats start from a different primer (Going deeper, "Where the rules bend"), and double bonds are added after the chain is built.

## Why it is not beta-oxidation in reverse

You have met beta-oxidation, the pathway that *takes fat apart*. It is tempting to assume synthesis runs it backward, but three differences set the two apart.

**Location.** Degradation happens mainly in the mitochondrion. Synthesis happens mainly in the cytoplasm. The cell physically separates the factory from the demolition crew.

**Carrier.** Beta-oxidation carries its intermediates on coenzyme A. Synthesis carries them on a dedicated acyl carrier protein, ACP, using a long phosphopantetheine arm that swings the growing chain from one active site to the next without ever letting it float free.

**Reducing power.** Degradation *produces* NADH and FADH2. Synthesis *consumes* NADPH. The extra phosphate on NADPH is a tag that enzymes read, so NAD and NADP form two separate pools kept in opposite states. In the cytoplasm NAD is kept mostly oxidized, as NAD⁺, ready to accept electrons from burning fuel; NADP is kept mostly reduced, as NADPH, ready to hand electrons to building. The two pools do not mix on their own: moving electrons from NADH to NADPH costs the cell energy (Paying the bill).

## Stage 1: Transport. Citrate carries the acetyl group out

Acetyl CoA is made in the mitochondrion, but it cannot cross the inner mitochondrial membrane. So the cell sends the acetyl group across in disguise.

Citrate is the disguise. The first step of the TCA cycle joins acetyl CoA to oxaloacetate to make citrate, and when fuel is plentiful, citrate builds up and is exported to the cytoplasm. There, **ATP-citrate lyase** spends an ATP to cleave it back into acetyl CoA and oxaloacetate. Think of citrate as an Uber for acetyl groups.

The leftover oxaloacetate helps pay for the build; we return to it once we know the bill.

## Stage 2: Activation. The committed step

In the cytoplasm, **acetyl CoA carboxylase (ACC)** spends one ATP to attach a carboxyl group, supplied as bicarbonate, to acetyl CoA. The product is **malonyl CoA**.

ACC carries biotin, a vitamin tethered to a lysine. Biotin is a swinging arm, a CO₂ taxi in the informal picture: it picks up the carboxyl group at one active site and drops it onto acetyl CoA at another. If you met pyruvate carboxylase in gluconeogenesis, this is the same trick.

Why call this the committed step? The malonyl CoA made by ACC1, the form of the enzyme that feeds synthesis, has essentially one fate: to be added to a fatty acid. So ACC1 is the first step that serves fat synthesis and nothing else, which is what "committed step" means and why the cell's fast controls act on this enzyme rather than on the later ones.

That leaves a puzzle: the carboxyl group ACC just attached, for an ATP, is about to leave again.

## Stage 3: Elongation, one turn at a time

The chain is built by **fatty acid synthase (FAS)**. In mammals it is one enormous protein, a pair of identical chains (a homodimer), and each chain carries every tool the cycle needs. The job names spell the cycle: **C**ondensation, **R**eduction, **D**ehydration, **R**eduction, or **CRDR**.

| FAS domain | What it does |
| --- | --- |
| Malonyl/acetyl transferase (MAT) | loads the acetyl primer and each malonyl unit |
| Ketoacyl synthase (KS) | **C**: joins the chain to the malonyl unit |
| Ketoreductase (KR) | **R**: C=O to CH–OH, using NADPH |
| Dehydratase (DH) | **D**: removes water, leaving a C=C |
| Enoyl reductase (ER) | **R**: C=C to CH₂–CH₂, using NADPH |
| Thioesterase (TE) | releases the finished chain |

ACP holds the chain by a thioester, the same sulfur link CoA uses.

### Turn one, drawn out

Start with the two pieces loaded. The **primer** is an acetyl group, parked on a cysteine of the KS domain. The **extender** is a malonyl group on ACP: an acetyl group carrying the extra carboxyl that ACC added.

> CH₃–CO–S–KS (acetyl, the primer)
>
> ⁻OOC–CH₂–CO–S–ACP (malonyl, the extender)

**Condensation.** KS joins them. The malonyl unit loses its extra carboxyl as CO₂, and the carbon that was next to it attacks the primer's C=O. The result is a four-carbon chain on ACP, **acetoacetyl-ACP**:

> CH₃–CO–CH₂–CO–S–ACP + CO₂

Why pay for a carbon that leaves one step later? Because joining two acetyl groups directly is uphill. The enzyme thiolase can do it (cholesterol synthesis starts that way), but the equilibrium lies far toward the separate pieces, so each join would be easily undone and the chain would grow only as fast as later steps could pull it along. Losing CO₂ is strongly downhill, and coupling that loss to the new carbon–carbon bond makes each join effectively one-way. The carboxyl group is a quarter in a vending machine: it goes in and does not come back, but it is what makes the snack come out. The ATP is what the quarter cost.

Now look at what condensation left: a C=O on the **β-carbon**, two carbons along from the thioester carbon. A fatty acid's chain is all CH₂, so the other three steps share one job: turn that C=O into CH₂. That takes four electrons (the carbon's oxidation state falls from +2 to −2). Each NADPH delivers two, as a hydride, so it takes two NADPH, added in two steps with a dehydration between them.

> **Reduction (KR, 1 NADPH):** CH₃–CH(OH)–CH₂–CO–S–ACP. The C=O becomes an alcohol.
>
> **Dehydration (DH):** CH₃–CH=CH–CO–S–ACP + H₂O. Water leaves, leaving a double bond.
>
> **Reduction (ER, 1 NADPH):** CH₃–CH₂–CH₂–CO–S–ACP. The double bond is saturated.

That is butyryl-ACP, four carbons, fully saturated, for **one malonyl CoA (so one ATP, spent at ACC) and two NADPH**. The butyryl chain moves back to the KS cysteine, a new malonyl unit loads onto ACP, and the same four steps run again. Each turn adds two carbons at the end nearest the sulfur, so the primer's two carbons end up at the far, methyl end of the finished fatty acid.

## Paying the bill: NADPH and delivery

The oxaloacetate left over from Stage 1 supplies part of the 14 NADPH. It is reduced to malate, which spends an NADH, and **malic enzyme** decarboxylates the malate to pyruvate, making NADPH. The pyruvate returns to the mitochondrion, where pyruvate carboxylase spends an ATP to make oxaloacetate again. In effect the shuttle turns an NADH into an NADPH for the price of an ATP. It runs once per acetyl group delivered, so it can supply up to 8 of the 14 NADPH. The pentose phosphate pathway and other routes supply the rest, and the real split varies by tissue.

## Regulation: malonyl CoA's second job

Back to the rule: ACC is where the cell decides whether to build. It is controlled three ways.

| Layer | Turns ACC on | Turns ACC off |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Phosphorylation | PP2A removes the phosphate | AMPK, the cell's low-fuel sensor, phosphorylates ACC |
| Allostery | citrate (abundant fuel) makes ACC polymerize into active filaments | palmitoyl CoA (the end product) depolymerizes them, a clean feedback brake |
| Hormones | insulin | glucagon and epinephrine |

Over hours to days, insulin and glucose also switch on the ACC and FAS genes, through the transcription factors SREBP-1c and ChREBP.

Exercise switches AMPK on, and so, in experiments, may the diabetes drug metformin; the mechanism is debated (Going deeper).

The second job is the elegant part: malonyl CoA blocks CPT-I. Much of the malonyl CoA that does this blocking is made by a second form of the enzyme, ACC2, which sits on the mitochondrion; ACC1 mostly feeds synthesis. So when the cell is building fat, it also tends to close the door on oxidation. (The film's phrase "locking the cell into building fat" describes ACC1's malonyl CoA. ACC2's is a signal.)

## After palmitate

FAS mostly stops at sixteen carbons. Longer chains and double bonds are added afterward, mainly on the endoplasmic reticulum. Elongases extend the chain from malonyl CoA at the same per-turn price, as in the stearate example. Desaturases install double bonds by a different chemistry, using O₂, NADH and cytochrome b5. Mammals cannot put a double bond near the methyl end of the chain, so **linoleate and alpha-linolenate are essential fatty acids** you must eat. Downstream, arachidonate becomes the eicosanoids, the signals that aspirin blocks.

Next, in **chapter 29**, acetyl CoA and the fatty acids you just built go on to become storage fat, membrane phospholipids, and cholesterol, and cholesterol gets its own committed enzyme and its own famous drug.

## Going deeper

The main line does not depend on these details, except that the metformin section fills in the hedge on a drug named in the objectives.

### Biotin and the 1927 egg-white rats

Biotin, ACC's CO₂ taxi, was found the hard way. In 1927, Margaret Boas in London fed rats raw egg white and watched them sicken. The culprit was avidin, an egg-white protein that binds biotin so tightly (a dissociation constant near 10 to the minus 15 molar) that it sequesters the vitamin and starves the enzymes that need it. That observation started the hunt that later identified biotin as the missing vitamin.

### Why two fats are essential

Counting from the carboxyl carbon, mammalian desaturases can place a double bond as far out as carbon 9, and no farther. The omega-6 and omega-3 positions are counted from the other, methyl end; in an 18-carbon chain they sit at carbons 12 and 15 from the carboxyl end, beyond carbon 9, so we cannot put double bonds there. Burr and Burr showed the consequence between 1929 and 1932: rats raised on fat-free diets sickened, linoleate cured them, and alpha-linolenate proved essential too.

### Aspirin and the platelet

Downstream of the essential fats, arachidonate becomes the eicosanoids: cyclooxygenase turns it into prostaglandins and thromboxanes. Aspirin works by permanently acetylating that enzyme. Platelets have no nucleus, so they make little or no replacement enzyme. That is why the effect of a single dose fades only gradually, over about a week, as new platelets replace the old ones.

### Metformin and AMPK

This is one proposed way metformin helps in diabetes: in experiments it inhibits Complex I, the AMP-to-ATP ratio climbs, and AMPK switches on and phosphorylates ACC. Malonyl CoA falls, so synthesis slows and CPT-I opens, and oxidation rises. How metformin lowers blood sugar is still debated, and much of that effect does not need AMPK. (Metformin descends from French lilac, also called goat's rue, a folk remedy for high blood sugar.)

### One more tell: D and L

Beyond location, carrier and reducing power, a fourth difference: the first reduction (KR) makes D-3-hydroxyacyl intermediates, while mitochondrial beta-oxidation runs through the L-isomers. Same atoms, opposite stereochemistry: one more sign that synthesis and degradation are different machines.

### The ledger's protons

The ledger is printed with protons omitted. Every carbon, nitrogen, oxygen, phosphorus and sulfur in it balances, and so does the water, but the protons are left out. Textbooks print different proton counts because they use different conventions for how charged each molecule is. The water count works because the oxygen that ends up on the phosphate comes from bicarbonate, not from water.

### Where the rules bend

- "Mainly" in the location rule matters. Very-long-chain fatty acids start their oxidation in peroxisomes, and a separate set of small enzymes inside the mitochondrion (mitochondrial fatty acid synthesis) makes an octanoyl group, the starting point for lipoic acid.
- Odd-chain fatty acids start from a three-carbon primer, propionyl CoA, instead of acetyl CoA.
- Not all malonyl CoA becomes fat: besides ACC2's signalling pool, malonyl CoA decarboxylase can break it down.
