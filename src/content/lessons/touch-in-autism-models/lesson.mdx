techniques:
  - name: "Vibrotactile psychophysics"
    blurb: >-
      A controlled vibrating probe on the finger, with a staircase or ramp, estimates a detection
      threshold in micrometres and discrimination performance. It measures what a participant can
      report in a laboratory task, which is a different quantity from how a stimulus feels in
      daily life.
  - name: "Conditional deletion and selective restoration"
    blurb: >-
      A floxed allele plus a driver such as Advillin-Cre removes a gene in targeted peripheral
      neurons (including primary somatosensory and sympathetic ganglion neurons); a
      tamoxifen-inducible CreERT2 version controls when recombination is induced. The reverse
      design restores a gene mainly in that targeted population on an otherwise null background,
      which tests whether that population can rescue a given outcome.
  - name: "In vivo dorsal root ganglion recording"
    blurb: >-
      Multi-unit recording from a ganglion in an anaesthetised mouse reports how many spikes
      mechanical stimulation of the skin evokes in peripheral afferents. Combined with a
      conditional receptor deletion, it tests whether a drug's effect runs through the receptor in
      those neurons.
  - name: "Ex vivo spinal cord electrophysiology"
    blurb: >-
      Dorsal root potentials in an isolated cord measure back-propagating primary afferent
      depolarisation, and whole-cell recordings in slices measure miniature inhibitory currents at
      a chosen age. Both report synaptic function in tissue, not perception.

## First separate the readouts

Sensory features belong to the diagnostic criteria for autism. DSM-5 criterion B4 covers hyperreactivity, hyporeactivity and unusual sensory interest, and is one of four restricted and repetitive behaviour criteria of which at least two must be met. Sensory differences therefore run in both directions, no single sensory feature is required, and not every autistic person is over-reactive to touch.

Three measurements often grouped under "touch sensitivity" hold different things fixed:

| Readout | What is held fixed? | What is measured? | What it cannot report by itself |
|---|---|---|---|
| Detection threshold | Stimulus protocol and response rule | Stimulus amplitude estimated from the participant's performance under that protocol | Everyday comfort or distress |
| Air puff alone in the 2016 comparison | A 0.9-psi, 50-ms puff on back hairy skin | Movement evoked by the puff, expressed relative to acoustic startle | Detection threshold or experience |
| Daily-life sensory report | Neither stimulus nor response | A person's response in context | A single receptor or circuit locus |

Puts and colleagues (2014) tested 32 autistic and 67 typically developing children aged 8 to 12 on static finger vibration. The autistic cohort's mean detection threshold was 9.13 micrometres against 6.59 micrometres—a 2.54-micrometre gap, or 38.5 per cent higher relative to 6.59.

With the readout boundary set, one rule organises the mouse experiments that follow:

> **The one idea.** In the mouse models and assays discussed here, similar tactile outcomes can arise through different mechanisms. Use targeted manipulations to test locus, physiology to test mechanism, and age comparisons or timed manipulations to test timing.
>
> *Scope:* this rule applies to the mouse models and behavioural assays here, spanning peripheral sensory neurons and the spinal dorsal horn. It does not identify a single cause of autism, turn a mouse movement into human perception, or establish human developmental windows.

## Locate a contribution with conditional genetics

The first film traced how conditional deletion and selective restoration bound a peripheral contribution; here are the four mutant lines that prompted that test, how the genetic driver works, and the timing control the first film did not show.

Orefice and colleagues (2016, *Cell*, Ginty laboratory) tested four mouse lines carrying mutations in autism-associated genes—*Mecp2*-null, *Mecp2*R306C, *Shank3B* heterozygous and *Fmr1*-null—and found stronger air-puff responses in all four at 6 to 8 weeks.

A **primary somatosensory neuron** spans the periphery and central nervous system, with its cell body in a dorsal-root or trigeminal ganglion and its central terminal inside the spinal cord or brainstem. In the crosses described here, crossing a **floxed allele** (a gene flanked by *loxP* sites) to **Advillin-Cre** expresses **Cre recombinase** and drives excision of the floxed DNA segment in the nuclei of targeted neurons, including dorsal-root and trigeminal sensory neurons—not one mechanoreceptor subtype. The deletion can affect these neurons' central terminals. Advillin drivers can also reach sympathetic ganglion neurons, with coverage depending on the driver, age and allele. Recombination can persist after Cre expression stops, and the investigators reported sparing resident CNS tissue in their validation.

Here are the specific assays behind the first film's two genetic arms, where **tactile prepulse inhibition** measures how strongly a gentle air puff before a loud tone suppresses acoustic startle:

## One touch response, several cellular routes

Conditional deletion locates a peripheral contribution, but a shared air-puff response cannot name the cellular defect; the second film maps how models diverge across sensory terminals, afferent excitability and spinal circuits.

<LessonFilm film={frontmatter.films[0]} />

The second film previews the physiological findings. Here is how the researchers obtained and interpreted them, starting with how depolarisation can reduce release in the classical terminal model.

Orefice and colleagues (2016) tested **low-threshold mechanoreceptors (LTMRs)**, afferents tuned to innocuous mechanical touch. First came anatomy: GABRB3-containing GABA-A receptors sit on presynaptic compartments of A-beta- and A-delta-LTMR central terminals in the mouse dorsal horn. In sensory-neuron *Mecp2* mutants, GABRB3 **puncta** (immunolabelled clusters in fixed tissue) near terminals marked by **vGlut1** (vesicular glutamate transporter 1, a marker of myelinated LTMR endings) fell by about 70 per cent. That supports receptor-marker loss near terminals; it does not measure ionic current or show that every terminal lost every receptor.

**Predict.** Does about 70 per cent fewer receptor-associated puncta establish about 70 per cent lower GABA-A conductance?

<details>
<summary>Check your prediction</summary>

No. Puncta are counted marker clusters in fixed tissue, whereas conductance is an electrical measurement; imaging supplies no conversion between them.

</details>

Two physiological tests followed. First, in P14-to-P19 spinal slices with strontium (Sr²⁺) replacing extracellular calcium to desynchronise vesicle fusion, dorsal-root stimulation evoked single-vesicle currents—**asynchronous quantal EPSCs**—in **postsynaptic dorsal column (PSDC) neurons** (dorsal-horn neurons receiving LTMR input). Following dorsal-root stimulation after sensory-neuron *Mecp2* deletion or heterozygous *Gabrb3* deletion, more detectable quantal events arrived without a detected change in their mean size. This supports enhanced presynaptic transmission under the recording conditions; the recording alone does not distinguish release probability per site from the number of active sites.

Second, they probed the GABA-A pathway itself, which raises a textbook paradox: primary afferents hold chloride high, so GABA-A activation can **depolarise** them ([synaptic integration](/lessons/synaptic-integration/) explains why the chloride gradient sets the sign). How can depolarisation inhibit release? In the classical terminal-inhibition model cited by the authors, when the GABA-A reversal potential lies above the resting potential, receptor activation can depolarise an afferent while increasing its membrane conductance. Near a terminal, the extra conductance can **shunt** spike current, and sufficient depolarisation can inactivate some sodium channels; either effect can reduce spike-driven glutamate release, depending on receptor location and timing. That depolarisation can spread backward into the dorsal root as a **dorsal root potential (DRP)**, which reports afferent depolarisation (**primary afferent depolarisation**, or **PAD**) in the root. In isolated cords from sensory-neuron *Mecp2* or *Gabrb3* mutants, evoked DRPs were greatly diminished.

Together—fewer puncta, higher quantal release frequency and smaller DRPs—the authors attributed the touch phenotype to deficient GABA-A-mediated presynaptic inhibition.

Similar fields of vGlut1-positive LTMR central-terminal profiles have gold GABRB3 immunolabelled puncta associated with them; fewer puncta remain in the mutant field. The puncta are marker clusters rather than individual receptor molecules, and the drawing does not depict conductance or transmitter release." caption="In mouse sensory-neuron Mecp2 mutants, GABRB3 puncta near vGlut1-positive LTMR terminals fell by about 70 per cent—a fixed-tissue marker count, not a conductance or release readout. Separate synaptic and dorsal-root-potential recordings support the authors' presynaptic-inhibition account, which is not settled physiology for all sensory afferents." />

Now qualify that first model. Depolarising is not identical to exciting, nor is every axonal GABA-A receptor an inhibitory terminal brake. Later work in proprioceptive afferents placed axonal GABA-A receptors mainly near nodes of Ranvier and branch points—where depolarisation aids spike propagation—and assigned release inhibition to GABA-B receptors (Hari and colleagues, 2022). Keep the GABA-A presynaptic-inhibition account tied to these mouse LTMR experiments rather than every sensory afferent.

The 2019 follow-up (Orefice and colleagues, *Cell*) found no detectable change in the terminal GABRB3 marker in the tested *Shank3* model and identified a different physiological defect. Large-diameter dorsal-root-ganglion neurons cultured for one to two days from 6- to 8-week-old *Shank3* mutants showed reduced **Ih** (the hyperpolarisation-activated mixed-cation current carried by HCN channels) and greater excitability. When some HCN channels are active near the starting voltage, their conductance can limit small voltage changes. Reducing that conductance can raise **input resistance**, so the same small current step can produce a larger voltage change from the same starting voltage. HCN channels also influence resting potential, so their net effect on firing depends on the cell and conditions. In the cultured large *Shank3*-mutant neurons, the authors measured higher input resistance and a lower current threshold for firing. The models shared an increased air-puff response, and their physiological results support different peripheral contributions to that response.

## Testing the peripheral target: the pharmacological control

The second film did not cover pharmacology. Orefice and colleagues (2019) tested **isoguvacine**, a GABA-A agonist detected in blood and liver—but not brain or cerebrospinal fluid—by mass spectrometry at the assayed high dose and sampling conditions (details in Going deeper). That is non-detection at stated limits, not proof that no molecule ever crosses.

Acute low doses reduced tactile over-reactivity in five genetic models and one **maternal immune activation** model (prenatal immune stimulation in pregnant dams), without the sedation reported for the brain-penetrating comparator midazolam in those assays.

The decisive control was genetic: in anaesthetised mice, subcutaneous isoguvacine reduced stimulus-evoked A-beta-LTMR firing recorded from the L4 dorsal root ganglion.

**Predict.** If isoguvacine suppresses firing through β3-containing GABA-A receptors in the targeted sensory neurons, what should happen to that firing effect after homozygous *Gabrb3* deletion in those cells?

<details>
<summary>Check your prediction</summary>

The firing suppression should disappear. It did: isoguvacine no longer altered touch-evoked spiking, supporting a requirement for *Gabrb3* in those neurons under these recording conditions.

</details>

Chronic dosing drew a second boundary. Litters from *Mecp2*R306C or *Shank3B* heterozygous dams received 2 mg/kg daily from P1 to P42, with testing from P42; each litter held mutant and control pups given the same treatment. Body condition, some brain abnormalities, anxiety-like behaviours and some social impairments improved, whereas texture discrimination, memory deficits, *Mecp2* motor impairment and *Shank3* overgrooming persisted—selective effects on mouse endpoints, not a general rescue.

## Timing requires two different designs

Whereas P28 CreERT2 intervened on *when* gene loss began, Tasnim and colleagues (2024, *Nature Neuroscience*, Ginty corresponding author, Orefice coauthor) asked which models already show increased air-puff movement at P4. The second film introduces the P4 and adult results and the spinal *Nlgn2* locus: at P4, *Gabrb3* and *Mecp2* mutants moved more in response to air puffs, whereas the study detected no P4 increase in the tested *Nlgn2* and *Rorb* mutants, although both showed adult tactile abnormalities (adult *Rorb* mutants showed enhanced tactile PPI, with no detectable increase in puff-alone reactivity). The film calls this an onset comparison; more strictly, the different assays—neonatal body displacement versus adult puff-alone reactivity (relative to acoustic startle) and tactile PPI—establish when effects were detected at the sampled ages. Here are the assay details, genetic drivers and maturation recordings the second film did not show.

Neonatal reactivity was measured as maximal body displacement within 500 milliseconds of an air puff. The age comparison distinguishes effects detected at P4 from abnormalities detected in adult tests; it does not identify the onset of any one phenotype, and the neonatal displacement and adult PPI assays measure different outcomes (a separate E18.5 optogenetic experiment is in Going deeper).

Deleting *Nlgn2* in **Lbx1-Cre**-targeted dorsal-horn neurons was sufficient to alter the tested adult tactile behaviours. To probe inhibitory synapses in the dorsal horn, the authors recorded **miniature inhibitory postsynaptic currents (mIPSCs)**—spontaneous single-vesicle inhibitory currents isolated with action potentials blocked by tetrodotoxin—in a separate, broader **Cdx2-Cre** conditional line.

**Predict.** If *Nlgn2* loss weakens dorsal-horn synaptic inhibition, what could mIPSCs show at P19 to P21?

<details>
<summary>Check your prediction</summary>

Weaker inhibition could produce smaller or fewer detectable miniature events, depending on what changed (synapse number, spontaneous release, receptor function, or small events falling below detection). In the P19-to-P21 *Cdx2-Cre* *Nlgn2* slices (deleted in all cells below cervical level 2), both event frequency and amplitude were lower than in controls.

</details>

In the main adult comparisons (Fig. 1e,g), the *Gabrb3* and *Mecp2* groups with increased P4 air-puff movement spent less time in the open-field centre and showed reduced three-chamber sociability, whereas the tested adult *Nlgn2* and *Rorb* groups showed no detectable differences from controls on those two comparisons—the authors' central dissociation (a separate open-field comparison found a difference between *Nlgn2* homozygotes and wild-type controls, Extended Data Fig. 1d). That remains an association across genotypes, not proof that early tactile state causes later behaviours, and does not establish human developmental windows.

Maturation recordings fit this split without proving why each model starts when it does: GABRB3 immunoreactivity appeared near vGlut1-positive terminals by P0 to P1, and *Gabrb3* deletion virtually abolished GABA-evoked depolarisations in medium- and large-diameter DRG neurons at P4 and P18 to P30. By contrast, deep dorsal-horn mIPSCs were smaller and rarer at P4 than at P19 to P21, and by P19 **feedforward inhibition** (inhibition driven by touch afferents via local spinal interneurons) in the LTMR-recipient zone was predominantly glycinergic.

Why timing can matter is developed in [sensitive periods in behaviour](/lessons/sensitive-periods-behavior/) and [critical period control](/lessons/critical-period-control/).

## What this does and does not mean for people

The short reminder is: **test locus, mechanism and timing separately.** In these mouse models, part of a tactile phenotype and selected accompanying behaviours can begin outside the brain. That does not make autism a peripheral condition: the manipulations are mouse sufficiency tests, the assays measure behaviour rather than subjective experience, and rescues left major features unchanged.

Human tactile differences are task-specific, run in both directions and vary across individuals far more than a group mean conveys. Human peripheral findings exist but do not bridge this mechanism: in skin biopsies from 32 autistic men, intraepidermal nerve-fibre density was 5.53 against 11.13 fibres per millimetre in controls, and about half met a skin-denervation criterion (Chien and colleagues, 2020). Those are small unmyelinated epidermal fibres, not the myelinated A-beta- and A-delta-LTMRs central to the mouse studies.

No peripheral GABA-A therapy for tactile over-reactivity in autism is approved. Isoguvacine remains experimental: a 2025 review found no documented human administration, and a registry search returned no registered studies (which cannot exclude other registries or unregistered work). The 2019 authors described peripheral GABA-A intervention as requiring patient tests and disclosed pending patents on peripherally restricted GABA-A drugs for that use.

Moving forward requires human measurements with some of the cellular specificity of mouse genetics; [Chronic pain](/lessons/chronic-pain/) and [spinal pain circuits](/lessons/spinal-pain-circuits/) face the same gap from the other side.

### Design problem

Test whether early tactile over-reactivity causes later anxiety-like behaviour in mice, or merely predicts it.

<details>
<summary>One way to do it</summary>

In separate parallel cohorts, schedule tamoxifen induction with an inducible sensory-neuron driver before P4, beginning at P28, or in adulthood, using a floxed *Gabrb3* allele for loss and a Cre-activatable rescue allele on a mutant background for restoration. Verify when and how much GABRB3 protein changes, including whether the earliest manipulation takes effect before the P4 test. Standard Cre excision is permanent, so use a reversible manipulation to isolate a brief interval of loss. Include tamoxifen-treated Cre-negative and untreated Cre-positive littermates, because inducible drivers can recombine without ligand depending on driver, tissue and age ([Tools for development](/lessons/tools-for-development/) covers these controls).

Measure P4 air-puff reactivity and adult anxiety-like and social assays in the same animals so each supplies both endpoints (which tracks within-animal association, though not mediation on its own). Use dorsal-horn *Nlgn2* deletion as a comparison with a different gene, cell population and physiology; the models also differ in the ages at which tactile effects were detected. To test whether early tactile signalling contributes to adult behaviour, assign animals of the same mutant genotype to transient, selective normalisation of early tactile signalling or a matched control, verify the change in tactile-evoked neural activity, and compare adult behaviour after the intervention has ended.

Use the unit independently assigned to treatment. For whole-litter assignment (as in chronic isoguvacine studies, even when each pup is injected), model pups as clustered within litters and account for repeated dams when relevant. For independently assigned pups, analyse animal outcomes with litter clustering where appropriate. Report cells and mice separately for physiology, account for recordings nested within mice, and state that this mouse design does not establish human windows.

</details>

## Going deeper

### Human psychophysics beyond the mean

Puts and colleagues (2014) vibrated the left second and third digits, using a 25-hertz dynamic ramp rising at 2 micrometres per second, and also tested amplitude and frequency discrimination. Baseline amplitude discrimination was worse in the autistic cohort, while frequency discrimination showed no significant diagnosis effect.

Static thresholds were 6.59 ± 2.81 micrometres (typically developing) and 9.13 ± 4.81 micrometres (autistic), as mean ± standard deviation—large SDs beside the 2.54-micrometre gap. Dividing that 2.54-micrometre difference by the control mean gives 38.5 per cent, whereas dividing by the autistic cohort's mean gives 27.8 per cent; neither equals the fraction of children beyond a cutoff:

> difference = 9.13 − 6.59 = 2.54 micrometres
>
> ratio = 9.13 ÷ 6.59 = 1.385
>
> relative to control mean = (2.54 ÷ 6.59) × 100 = 38.5 per cent
>
> relative to autistic cohort mean = (2.54 ÷ 9.13) × 100 = 27.8 per cent

Other studies diverge: lower thresholds at 200 hertz but not 30 hertz in adults with Asperger syndrome (Blakemore and colleagues, 2006), and heightened forearm vibration and thermal-pain sensitivity with normal light-touch thresholds (Cascio and colleagues, 2008). Thresholds vary with frequency, body site and age, so Puts is one result among inconsistent ones.

### Recording details behind the timing map

GABA-evoked depolarisations at P4 and P18 to P30 used a high-chloride internal solution, setting the driving force the experimenter wanted to see. The P19-to-P21 dorsal-horn mIPSC comparison recorded seven cells per genotype from two mice per genotype; because cells from one mouse share genotype, litter, preparation and day, seven cells are not seven independent animals.

### Isoguvacine pharmacology and dosing

Half-maximal effective concentrations of isoguvacine were 19.7 micromolar at recombinant human alpha-one beta-three gamma-two receptors and 18.7 micromolar at alpha-two beta-three gamma-two receptors in HEK293T cells—in-vitro receptor values, not tissue exposure targets.

After 20 mg/kg intraperitoneally in 8- to 12-week-old male mice (ten times the 2 mg/kg acute behavioural dose), liquid chromatography–mass spectrometry detected isoguvacine in blood and liver but not brain or cerebrospinal fluid, with limits of 640 picomolar in fluids and 16 nanomolar in tissue. A ClinicalTrials.gov search for isoguvacine on 27 October 2026 returned no registered studies.

### The embryo preparation

In E18.5 exteriorised embryos, optogenetic activation of forepaw Ret-positive A-beta-LTMRs evoked greater movement in *Gabrb3* heterozygotes than in controls (11 control and 9 heterozygous embryos)—evoked movement in an embryo preparation, not a prenatal test of anything else.
