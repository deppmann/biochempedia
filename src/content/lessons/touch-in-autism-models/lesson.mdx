techniques:
  - name: "Vibrotactile psychophysics"
    blurb: >-
      A controlled vibrating probe on the finger, with a staircase or ramp, estimates a detection
      threshold in micrometres and discrimination performance. It measures what a participant can
      report in a laboratory task, which is a different quantity from how a stimulus feels in
      daily life.
  - name: "Conditional deletion and selective restoration"
    blurb: >-
      A floxed allele plus a sensory-neuron Cre driver removes a gene from primary somatosensory
      neurons; a tamoxifen-inducible CreERT2 version sets when. The reverse design restores a gene
      only in those cells on an otherwise null background, which tests sufficiency of that cell
      population for a given outcome.
  - name: "In vivo dorsal root ganglion recording"
    blurb: >-
      Multi-unit recording from a ganglion in an anaesthetised mouse reports how many spikes
      mechanical stimulation of the skin evokes in peripheral afferents. Combined with a
      conditional receptor deletion, it tests whether a drug's effect runs through the receptor in
      those neurons.
  - name: "Ex vivo spinal cord electrophysiology"
    blurb: >-
      Dorsal root potentials in an isolated cord measure back-propagating primary afferent
      depolarisation, and whole-cell recordings in slices measure miniature inhibitory currents at
      a chosen age. Both report synaptic function in tissue, not perception.

**Deleting a gene from peripheral sensory neurons, while sparing the brain and spinal cord, was enough to change how a mouse reacted to a gentle puff of air, and to shift its scores in anxiety-like and social assays.** The brain in those animals carried the gene throughout. Across the models tested, tactile over-reactivity that was already present in newborn pups was associated with these adult behavioural differences, and over-reactivity that emerged later was not; this is an association across mouse models, taken up under Timing. The deletion experiment is the reason this lesson exists, and most of the work of reading it is keeping straight what it does and does not claim.

## What "over-reactive to touch" means in people, and what it does not

Sensory features are in the diagnostic criteria. Criterion B4 of the DSM-5 covers hyperreactivity, hyporeactivity and unusual interest in sensory aspects of the environment, and it is one of four restricted and repetitive behaviour criteria of which at least two must be met. Two things follow. Sensory difference runs in both directions, and a sensory feature is not required for a diagnosis. Not every autistic person is over-reactive to touch.

The laboratory version of the question is narrower still. Puts and colleagues (2014) tested 32 autistic and 67 typically developing children aged 8 to 12 with vibration delivered to the fingers, and measured static detection thresholds, a dynamic ramp task, and amplitude and frequency discrimination. The autistic cohort needed a stimulus about 38.5 per cent larger to detect it (mean threshold 9.13 µm against 6.59 µm). A higher threshold is poorer detection, so whatever everyday over-reactivity is, it is not a lower detection threshold in this task. Other studies point the other way: lower thresholds at 200 Hz but not 30 Hz in adults with Asperger syndrome (Blakemore and colleagues, 2006), and heightened forearm vibration and thermal pain sensitivity with normal light-touch thresholds (Cascio and colleagues, 2008). Threshold results in autism vary with frequency, body site and age, so treat this as one result among inconsistent ones. Baseline amplitude discrimination was also worse in that cohort, while frequency discrimination showed no significant diagnosis effect. The standard deviations are large next to the difference: the distributions overlap heavily, and a group mean cannot fix any one child's threshold or say how a touch feels to that child.

Hold that beside the mouse readout you are about to meet. The mouse measure is the size of a startle-like movement to a fixed stimulus, a 0.9 psi puff lasting 50 ms on back hairy skin. That is reactivity at one stimulus level, not a threshold, and not a report of what the animal perceives. Human psychophysics and mouse reactivity are two different quantities, and the lesson keeps them apart.

## A peripheral locus: conditional deletion and selective restoration

Orefice and colleagues (2016, Cell, Ginty laboratory) began with four mouse lines carrying mutations in genes associated with autism in people: Mecp2-null, Mecp2R306C, Shank3B heterozygous and Fmr1-null. All four reacted more strongly than controls to the air puff, tested from 6 to 8 weeks of age.

Then came the restriction. Advillin-Cre supplies Cre recombinase to primary somatosensory neurons, so crossing it to a floxed allele removes that gene from those cells and leaves the brain's copy intact. (A Cre driver labels the cells that expressed it at some time, which here is the whole population of dorsal root and trigeminal neurons, plus sympathetic ganglion neurons, not one subtype.) Deleting Mecp2 or Gabrb3 this way was sufficient for the tactile phenotype: stronger air-puff responses, altered tactile prepulse inhibition, and impaired texture discrimination in a textured novel-object task. It was also sufficient for anxiety-like and social-interaction differences in the assays tested.

Two controls make that result readable rather than merely striking. First, timing. An inducible Advillin-CreERT2 version let the same deletion begin at P28 instead of during development. The tactile deficits still appeared. The anxiety-like phenotype did not. The paper's word for that condition is "adult deletion", but the induction age is P28, and that is the number to keep. Second, direction. Restoring Mecp2 only in the Advillin-targeted neurons (mostly sensory, with the sympathetic coverage noted above), on an otherwise null background, rescued tactile sensitivity and selected anxiety-like and social measures. Lethality, memory deficits and motor deficits were not rescued. A peripheral contribution to part of the phenotype, then, and an explicit failure to account for the rest.

What is wrong in the mutant terminals? GABRB3-containing GABA-A receptors localise to the presynaptic compartments of Aβ- and Aδ-LTMR central terminals in mouse dorsal horn, that is, to the endings of peripheral neurons inside the spinal cord. (An LTMR is a low-threshold mechanoreceptor afferent.) In sensory-neuron Mecp2 mutants, GABRB3 puncta associated with vGlut1-positive terminals were reduced by about 70 per cent. That figure counts immunolabelled puncta in fixed tissue; it is not a measured conductance. Two physiological results point the same way: in P14 to P19 slices, dorsal-root-evoked quantal EPSC frequency onto postsynaptic dorsal column neurons went up, and in isolated cords, evoked dorsal root potentials were greatly diminished after sensory-neuron deletion of Mecp2 or Gabrb3. Dorsal root potentials are the back-propagating signature of primary afferent depolarisation. The authors attributed the touch phenotype to deficient GABA-A-mediated presynaptic inhibition.

Mind the sign. GABA acting on an afferent terminal depolarises it, because those terminals hold chloride high ([synaptic integration](/lessons/synaptic-integration/) sets out why the sign follows the gradient, not the transmitter). The 2016 study interprets that depolarisation as reducing action-potential-evoked release. Depolarising is not the same as exciting. Later work in proprioceptive afferents places axonal GABA-A receptors mainly near nodes and branch points, where they aid spike propagation, and assigns presynaptic inhibition of release to GABA-B (Hari and colleagues, 2022), so read the presynaptic-inhibition account here as the authors' interpretation rather than settled physiology.

## Not one mechanism: a second model and a drug that stays outside

The 2019 follow-up (Orefice and colleagues, Cell) broke the single story. Large-diameter dorsal root ganglion neurons cultured one to two days from 6- to 8-week-old Shank3 mutants had reduced Ih, the hyperpolarisation-activated current, and increased excitability. No altered GABRB3 expression was detected at Shank3 LTMR central terminals. Same behavioural phenotype, different cellular defect.

The same paper asked whether a drug acting only outside the brain could change the behaviour. Isoguvacine is a GABA-A agonist. After injection in male mice at ten times the behavioural dose (dose and detection limits under Going deeper), it was detected in blood and liver but not in brain or cerebrospinal fluid. Low acute doses reduced tactile over-reactivity in five genetic models and one maternal-immune-activation model, without the sedation reported for the brain-penetrating comparator midazolam in those assays.

The control that makes this a mechanism claim rather than a drug effect is genetic. In anaesthetised mice, isoguvacine reduced stimulus-evoked Aβ-LTMR firing recorded from the L4 ganglion. In mice with homozygous sensory-neuron Gabrb3 deletion, it did not. Remove the receptor subunit from the neurons and the drug stops working on them.

Chronic dosing drew the limits again. Litters from Mecp2R306C or Shank3B heterozygous dams received 2 mg/kg daily from P1 to P42, with testing from P42; each litter held mutant and control pups and the whole litter got the same treatment. Body condition, some brain abnormalities, anxiety-like behaviours and some social impairments improved. Texture discrimination and memory deficits persisted, as did Mecp2 motor impairment and Shank3 overgrooming. This is a selective effect on some mouse endpoints. Isoguvacine remains an experimental compound: a 2025 review reports no documented human administration, and a registry search found no registered studies, though that cannot exclude other registries or unregistered work. The 2019 authors present peripheral GABA-A intervention as a strategy that would require testing in patients, and they disclose pending patents on peripherally restricted GABA-A drugs for that use.

## Timing: early afferent receptors, later spinal inhibition

Tasnim and colleagues (2024, Nature Neuroscience, Ginty corresponding author, Orefice a coauthor) asked when each model's phenotype starts. Across the models tested, neonatal over-reactivity was associated with adult anxiety-like and social differences, while over-reactivity that emerged later in life was not; the authors treat that dissociation as their central result. Association across genotypes is not a demonstrated causal sequence, and these mouse studies do not establish corresponding human developmental windows. Why timing should matter at all is the subject of [sensitive periods in behaviour](/lessons/sensitive-periods-behavior/) and [critical period control](/lessons/critical-period-control/).

Gabrb3 and Mecp2 loss-of-function pups were already more reactive to an air puff at P4, measured as maximal body displacement within 500 ms. Nlgn2 and Rorb mutants were not, despite tactile abnormalities as adults. Sampling two ages does not pin the onset day; it does separate early-onset from later-onset models.

The machinery matches that split. GABRB3 immunoreactivity was detectable near VGLUT1-positive sensory terminals at P0 to P1, and Gabrb3 deletion virtually abolished GABA-evoked depolarisations in medium- and large-diameter dorsal root ganglion neurons at both P4 and P18 to P30 (recorded with a high-chloride internal solution, which sets the driving force the experimenter wanted to see). Deep dorsal-horn miniature inhibitory currents, by contrast, were smaller and rarer at P4 than at P19 to P21, and feedforward inhibition in the LTMR-recipient zone was predominantly glycinergic by P19. Nlgn2 sits in that later system: Lbx1-Cre deletion from dorsal-horn neurons was sufficient for adult tactile alterations, and P19 to P21 Cdx2-Cre mutants (deleted in all cells below cervical level 2) had reduced dorsal-horn mIPSC frequency and amplitude.

## What this does and does not mean for people

The mouse work establishes that in these models part of a tactile phenotype, and some behavioural measures that go with it, can begin in neurons outside the brain. It does not make autism a peripheral condition. The genetic manipulations are sufficiency tests in one species, the behaviours are operational assays rather than experiences, and the rescue experiments left major features untouched. In people, the measurable differences are task-specific, go in both directions, and vary between individuals far more than any group mean conveys. No peripheral GABA-A therapy for tactile over-reactivity in autism has been approved.

Human peripheral data are not entirely absent. In skin biopsies from 32 autistic men, intraepidermal nerve fibre density was lower than in controls, 5.53 against 11.13 fibres per mm, and about half met a criterion for skin denervation (Chien and colleagues, 2020). Those are small fibres, not the Ab- and Ad-LTMRs the mouse work turns on. What would settle the next question is a human measurement with the specificity of the mouse genetics behind it. [Chronic pain](/lessons/chronic-pain/) and [spinal pain circuits](/lessons/spinal-pain-circuits/) face the same gap from the other side.

### Design problem

Test whether early tactile over-reactivity causes later anxiety-like behaviour in mice, or merely predicts it.

<details>
<summary>One way to do it</summary>

Use a tamoxifen-inducible sensory-neuron driver to delete Gabrb3 in defined windows (before P4, from P28, and in adulthood), and in a parallel arm restore it in the same windows on a mutant background. Include tamoxifen-treated Cre-negative and untreated Cre-positive littermates, because inducible drivers can recombine without ligand, to an extent that varies with driver, tissue and age ([tools for development](/lessons/tools-for-development/) covers the controls Cre-based manipulations need).

Measure air-puff reactivity at P4 and the anxiety-like and social assays later in the same animals, so each animal supplies both ends of the association. Add the dorsal-horn Nlgn2 deletion as a later-locus contrast: if the adult outcome follows the early tactile state rather than the gene, that arm should separate them.

Define the unit of assignment for this design: genotype and induction window are assigned per animal, so analyse per animal with litter as a clustering factor, and keep litter as the treatment unit only where whole litters are dosed, as in the chronic isoguvacine experiments. Report cells and animals separately wherever a physiological measure enters. State in the discussion that mouse studies of this kind do not establish human counterparts of these windows.

## Going deeper

### The Puts cohort in detail

Vibration was delivered to the second and third digits of the left hand. The dynamic ramp task used 25 Hz rising at 2 µm per second. Table 1 of the paper gives a mean static detection threshold of 6.59 ± 2.81 µm in the typically developing cohort and 9.13 ± 4.81 µm in the autistic cohort (mean ± SD, human children, in vivo psychophysics).

> difference = 9.13 µm − 6.59 µm = 2.54 µm
> ratio = 9.13 / 6.59 = 1.385
> relative increase = (2.54 / 6.59) × 100 = 38.5 per cent

The standard deviations were 2.81 and 4.81 µm, against a 2.54 µm difference in means.

### Isoguvacine pharmacology and dosing

The EC50 of isoguvacine was 19.7 µM at recombinant human α1β3γ2 and 18.7 µM at α2β3γ2 receptors expressed in HEK293T cells, an in vitro receptor measurement and not a tissue exposure target. After 20 mg/kg intraperitoneally in male mice aged 8 to 12 weeks, LC-MS detected the compound in blood and liver but not in brain or cerebrospinal fluid, with detection limits of 640 pM in fluids and 16 nM in tissue. The acute behavioural dose was 2 mg/kg. A ClinicalTrials.gov search for isoguvacine on 27 October 2026 returned no registered studies.

### The embryo preparation

In E18.5 exteriorised embryos, optogenetic activation of forepaw Ret-positive Aβ-LTMRs evoked greater movement in Gabrb3 heterozygotes than in controls (11 control and 9 heterozygous embryos). That is evoked movement in an embryo preparation, not a prenatal test of anything.
