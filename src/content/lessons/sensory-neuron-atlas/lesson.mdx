    story: >-
      If identity is a transcriptional program, can it be undone? William Renthal and colleagues
      cut or crushed peripheral nerves in mice, mostly males aged 8 to 12 weeks, and then
      sequenced single nuclei from the DRG. After injury, injured neurons of many subtypes lost
      much of the expression that marked their subtype, while injury-induced genes rose.
      Over a time course associated with the axons regrowing to their targets, subtype identity
      re-emerged. Genetic perturbation of Atf3, a transcription factor induced by injury, showed
      that it was required for the full reprogramming. Four years later Renthal was the
      corresponding author, with first author Bhuiyan and colleagues, of an atlas that harmonized
      31 data sets from six mammalian species into one reference for the dorsal root and
      trigeminal ganglia. Together the two papers made the distinction between a cell type and a
      cell state something that can be measured, rather than argued about.

techniques:
  - name: "Single-cell and single-nucleus RNA sequencing"
    blurb: >-
      Dissociated cells, or nuclei isolated from frozen tissue, are each given a barcode so that
      their RNA can be sequenced and counted one cell at a time, then grouped by similarity.
      Whole cells and nuclei capture different RNA, so the method of preparation can change which
      groups appear.
  - name: "Cre, CreER and Cre/Flp intersectional genetics"
    blurb: >-
      A recombinase expressed under a marker gene's regulatory sequences switches on a reporter or an actuator in
      cells that expressed that marker at some time; CreER adds a tamoxifen-timed window, and
      requiring both Cre and Flp restricts the label to cells that expressed two markers. Each
      line is validated by its specificity and its efficiency.
  - name: "In vivo DRG calcium imaging"
    blurb: >-
      A genetically encoded calcium indicator such as GCaMP is expressed in sensory neurons, the
      ganglion is exposed in an anaesthetized mouse, and hundreds of cell bodies are imaged while
      the skin is stimulated. It reports which neurons respond, but the slow signal does not count
      spikes.
  - name: "Spatial transcriptomics with in situ hybridization"
    blurb: >-
      RNA from a tissue section is captured on an array of barcoded spots (55 µm across on the
      Visium platform), so expression keeps its position; single-molecule in situ hybridization
      then checks a marker in individual cells. A spot over a large neuron still mixes in RNA
      from neighbouring cells.

  - stem: "A CreER line is induced in mice and labels 150 DRG neurons in a set of sections. Of those, 120 express the target marker by in situ hybridization. The same sections contain 240 marker-positive neurons in all. What are the line's specificity and efficiency?"
    choices:
      - "Specificity 50%, efficiency 80%"
      - "Specificity 80%, efficiency 62.5%"
      - "Specificity 80%, efficiency 50%"
      - "Specificity 62.5%, efficiency 50%"
    answer: 2
    rationale: >-
      Specificity is labelled-and-marker-positive cells over all labelled cells, 120 / 150 = 80%.
      Efficiency is the same numerator over all marker-positive cells, 120 / 240 = 50%. Specificity
      50% with efficiency 80% swaps the two definitions. Efficiency 62.5% divides the 150 labelled
      cells by the 240 marker-positive cells, which counts off-target labelled cells as hits.
      Specificity 62.5% makes the same error in the other quantity.
  - stem: "Sharma et al. (2020) compared P0 DRGs from Ngf-null;Bax-null mice with DRGs from Bax-null littermates. Why was Bax deleted in both groups?"
    choices:
      - "To stop NGF from reaching the neurons' targets"
      - "To label NGF-dependent neurons for sequencing"
      - "To keep alive neurons that would die without NGF"
      - "To replace NGF signalling through the TrkA receptor"
    answer: 2
    rationale: >-
      Without NGF many DRG neurons die by Bax-dependent apoptosis, so an Ngf knockout alone would

  - stem: "After sciatic nerve crush in adult mice, single-nucleus sequencing of the DRG shows a cluster rich in Atf3 that is absent from uninjured ganglia and shrinks as axons regenerate. How is this cluster best classified?"
    choices:
      - "A new permanent subtype made by adult neurogenesis"
      - "A state that injured neurons enter and later leave"
      - "Contaminating satellite glia in the neuronal data"
      - "A human-specific class revealed by the mouse injury"
    answer: 1
    rationale: >-
      Renthal et al. (2020) found that injury suppressed subtype identity and that identity
      re-emerged with regeneration, so an Atf3-rich group is best read as a transient state of existing
      neurons. The harmonized atlas lists such a group among its eighteen, a reminder that atlases can mix types and states. Nothing here

The practical rule needs a scope before we use it.

> **Guiding idea.** For sensory-neuron atlases, treat a transcriptomic cluster as a candidate type: it earns a functional type claim when it recurs and an independent measurement anchors it, while its count and boundaries remain conditional on resolution, preparation and state.

This is a rule for interpreting sensory-neuron atlas claims, not a universal definition of cell type. An independent anchor can be an ending, physiology, target or behaviour after manipulation. A molecular class remains useful before that test; it simply supports a narrower claim.

[Touch and pain](/lessons/touch-and-pain/) introduced Aβ, Aδ and C fibres, and [sensory coding](/lessons/sensory-coding/) compared labelled lines with population codes. [Sequencing and genomes](/lessons/sequencing-and-genomes/) and [recording many neurons](/lessons/large-scale-neuroscience/) cover the sequencing and atlas methods. Here, a **cluster** is a group of cells with similar measured RNA profiles; a **state** is a condition an existing cell can enter and leave.

## What each atlas actually counted

The dorsal root ganglion (DRG) holds cell bodies of neurons innervating skin, muscle and viscera. Usoskin, Ernfors and colleagues (2015) dissociated mouse lumbar DRGs and let an unsupervised method group the single-neuron transcriptomes into eleven classes. NP3, one non-peptidergic class, was linked by the authors to itch. That expression pattern suggested a function; it did not prove one ([itch](/lessons/itch/) supplies the later evidence).

Zeisel and colleagues (2018) profiled a much larger census across the mouse nervous system. Within peripheral sensory neurons, their hierarchical taxonomy resolved eight peptidergic, six non-peptidergic and three neurofilament types: seventeen. Broad classes and fine subtypes occupy different levels of the same tree. More cells, deeper sequencing or a finer clustering setting can split a branch, whereas a preparation can hide RNA or cells rather than merely changing the zoom.

Bhuiyan, Renthal and colleagues (2024) integrated 31 DRG and trigeminal data sets from human, mouse, rat, guinea pig and two macaque species. Their reference contained eighteen DRG and fourteen trigeminal neuronal subtypes. The authors stressed that preparation, sequencing depth and analysis affect what is resolved. One DRG group was defined by injury-induced transcription factors: the Atf3 group. It warns that an atlas can contain a state alongside types. They also cautioned that one molecular subtype can behave differently at different innervation targets.

Do not turn fibre labels into transcriptomic clusters. Among low-threshold mechanoreceptors (LTMRs), neurons activated by innocuous touch, C, Aδ and Aβ mean unmyelinated, lightly myelinated and heavily myelinated axons (Li and colleagues, 2011). C does not mean nociceptor: adult mouse tyrosine-hydroxylase-positive neurons were identified as C-fibre LTMRs. [The LTMR lesson](/lessons/ltmr-classes/) builds the full class table.

## From a candidate cluster to a functional test

The usual bridge is genetic access. A **Cre driver** expresses Cre recombinase under a marker gene's regulatory sequences, either from the gene's locus or a transgene. A reporter then marks cells that expressed that marker. **CreER** adds a tamoxifen-controlled time window. An **intersectional** strategy requires Cre and Flp, restricting the label to cells that expressed both markers ([tools for development](/lessons/tools-for-development/) covers the genetics).

Every line has two separate quality numbers:

> **specificity = labelled and marker-positive cells / all labelled cells**
>
> **efficiency = labelled and marker-positive cells / all marker-positive cells**

**Predict.** A made-up line labels 200 neurons. Of those, 180 express the target marker, while the sections contain 300 marker-positive neurons altogether. Can the line be highly specific but only moderately efficient?

<details>
<summary>Check your prediction</summary>

Yes. Specificity is 180 / 200 = 90%, because most labelled cells are on target. Efficiency is 180 / 300 = 60%, because 40% of the target population was missed. The line can isolate the right cells for imaging, but a negative ablation result is weak: killing 60% and seeing no deficit does not show the population is unneeded.

Timing is part of the label. With TrkC-CreER intersected with a Ret reporter, Ret-f(CFP), Bai and colleagues (2015) used 3 mg tamoxifen at E12.5 and labelled Merkel-associated Aβ SA1 endings. With 0.1 mg at P5, the label favoured Aβ field-LTMR circumferential endings.

**Predict.** Can the difference be attributed to induction age alone when both age and tamoxifen dose changed?

<summary>Check your prediction</summary>

Qi, Iskols, Sharma, Ginty and colleagues (2024) turned such access into a functional test. One intersection, Smr2-Cre with Calca-FlpE, restricted labelling to an SMR2- and CGRP-positive population with free endings. The team exposed the L4 DRG in anaesthetized young mice and imaged calcium while indenting skin with forces from 1 to 75 mN, heating it or cooling it.

The authors reported that each genetically defined subtype had its own threshold and response range within the mechanical or thermal stimuli tested. Together the subtypes tiled those tested ranges, recruiting more subtypes as a stimulus grew stronger. Variation remained within a group: TRPM8 neurons showed three kinds of cooling response. The independent physiology therefore strengthened the candidate clusters' claim to be functional types without making every member identical.

Calcium imaging sets a limit on that inference. In adult mouse DRG imaged in vivo, GCaMP6 rises and decays slowly compared with an action potential (Chisholm and colleagues, 2018). It detected single action potentials in most cases, but nearby spikes summate, so one transient does not give an exact spike count and amplitude tracks firing rate only roughly. **Patch-seq** makes the bridge more direct: record a neuron with a patch electrode, then aspirate and sequence that same cell. Cadwell and colleagues (2016) demonstrated it in mouse neocortex; Parpaite and colleagues (2021) used it in mouse DRG, matching mechanically activated currents to transcriptomic populations.

## The causal tests behind changing identity

The chapter showed the trajectories; the experiments below supply the controls. DRG neurons arise from neural crest ([the neural crest](/lessons/neural-crest/)). Sharma, Ginty and colleagues (2020) sequenced mouse DRG neurons from embryonic stages to adulthood. The early cells were already post-mitotic sensory neurons. “Unspecialized” refers only to subtype identity: they coexpressed transcription factors that later became restricted to particular subtype combinations over weeks.

The target-derived signal NGF keeps TrkA-expressing neurons alive ([Trk and p75](/lessons/trk-and-p75/)). An Ngf knockout alone would remove cells, making altered identity impossible to separate from selective survival.

**Predict.** Which littermate control matches the block of apoptosis in Ngf-null;Bax-null mice: wild type or Bax-null?

<details>
<summary>Check your prediction</summary>

Bax-null is the matched control. Bax is needed for these neurons to die by apoptosis, so removing it in both groups keeps neurons available for profiling and lets the comparison ask about NGF-dependent identity rather than cell loss. At P0, the double mutants had fewer transcriptionally distinct populations. NGF therefore contributes to subtype specialization; the mouse experiment does not show that NGF alone sets every fate.

</details>

Renthal and colleagues (2020) cut or crushed peripheral nerves in adult mice and sequenced single DRG nuclei. Injury suppressed subtype-marker expression, and Atf3 was required for the full injury reprogramming. Subtype identity re-emerged over a time course associated with axons regrowing to their targets.

“Associated with” matters. Timing alone does not show that reinnervation restores identity, and apparent recovery could reflect which neurons survived or were sampled. The study therefore traced injured neurons: the share labelled by an Atf3 injury reporter was unchanged from one week to two months, arguing against selective death, and Mrgprd reporter tracing fit retained identity.

An injured, inflamed or diseased ganglion can therefore contain states. An Atf3-rich cluster is a condition existing neurons enter and leave, not a new permanent type, a distinction that matters in [chronic pain](/lessons/chronic-pain/).

## Human atlases: methods, markers and denominators

Keep the rule in view: each group must be read with its resolution, preparation and state. Human DRG atlases often use nuclei or tissue sections because donor tissue is often frozen and large human neurons are hard to dissociate intact.

Nguyen and colleagues (2021) sequenced 1,837 neuronal nuclei from lumbar DRGs of donors aged 34 to 55. Several classes lacked a clear mouse equivalent in their comparison. H9 coexpressed TRPM8 and PIEZO2 transcripts, suggesting sensitivity to cold and touch but not demonstrating it. MRGPRX1, although not strongly expressed, localized selectively to H10. Primate MRGPRX genes have no one-to-one mouse counterparts, so a mouse Mrgpr population should not simply be renamed with a human gene.

The 2024 atlas linked mouse groups to function using rodent genetics: Mrgprd neurons to nociception; Mrgpra3 and Trpv1 coexpressing neurons to itch; and Mrgpra3 and Mrgprb4 coexpressing neurons to C-LTMR physiology. Those links do not by themselves assign human function.

Spatial data preserve anatomy but change the denominator. Tavares-Ferreira and colleagues (2022) used Visium spots 55 µm across on lumbar DRG sections from eight organ donors, four women and four men, then validated markers by RNAscope in situ hybridization. They selected 4,356 spots overlapping single neurons, retained 3,952 after filtering and curated twelve neuronal clusters, five called C nociceptors.

**Predict.** A student divides five C-nociceptor cluster labels by twelve labels and reports that 41.67% of human DRG neurons are C nociceptors. What did that denominator count?

<details>
<summary>Check your prediction</summary>

It counted category labels, not neurons. The valid calculations are:

1. Retention = 3,952 / 4,356 = 0.9073, or 90.73%. Check: 4,356 × 0.9073 = 3,952.2, which rounds to 3,952.
2. Five / twelve = 0.4167, or 41.67% **of cluster labels**. Clusters need not be equally large.

Per-cluster spot counts come closer to an abundance estimate because each kept spot was selected to overlap one neuron. They still describe sampled sections, not all human DRG neurons. Spots also collect RNA from satellite glia and neighbours, affecting assignment, and a few neurons lay under more than one spot.

</details>

The same study found TAC1 and CALCA, encoding substance P and CGRP, broadly expressed across human nociceptor clusters. The authors therefore suggested that the mouse peptidergic/non-peptidergic split may not transfer as a clean binary. Jung and colleagues (2023) used one workflow across mouse, guinea pig, macaque and human DRG. Tafa4 was mainly in mouse C-LTMRs, whereas human TAFA4 was strongest in NTRK2- and SCN5A-positive Aδ-LTMRs, confirmed by in situ hybridization. A mouse mechanism tied to a marker needs a human check of which cells carry it. [Nociceptors and sodium channels](/lessons/nociceptors-and-sodium-channels/) meets the same drug-target problem.

### Design problem: is a proposed human-only class real?

Nguyen and colleagues (2021) reported human classes without clear mouse equivalents using single-nucleus sequencing. Yu and colleagues (2024), deeply sequencing single human DRG somata, also reported likely human-specific types. The 2024 integration found no human-specific neuronal clusters, but an integration is built to align data sets and can merge real differences.

**Predict.** Why should tissue validation test two or three defining markers in the same neurons rather than one marker alone?

<details>
<summary>Check your prediction</summary>

A cluster is defined by a combination of expression, and one marker may be shared with other groups. Coexpression of two or three defining markers in the same cells tests whether the proposed population exists in tissue rather than merely whether one gene is present. It should recur across donors.

Nguyen and colleagues noted a separate preparation warning: mouse whole-cell data contained a Trpa1-rich group that a compared mouse nuclear data set missed. If a proposed human-only group gains a mouse partner only after nuclei are compared with nuclei, a preparation difference becomes the leading explanation, provided the integration did not simply force unlike groups together.

To test the claim fully:

1. **Match preparation.** Compare nuclei with nuclei and, where possible, whole cells with whole cells, at similar depth and with the same pipeline and clustering resolution.
2. **Test robustness.** Require the class in several donors and across several clustering resolutions and integration methods. A class carried by one donor or setting is fragile.
3. **Validate in tissue.** Use multiplex in situ hybridization for two or three defining markers in human and orthologous mouse genes, counting coexpressing neurons and measuring soma size. A coexpressing population present in human sections but absent from mouse sections supports a real expression difference.
4. **Anchor function.** Record human DRG neurons in culture or by Patch-seq, or use microneurography where an afferent class can be matched, and ask whether the marker-defined cells share physiology.

If the class disappears when methods are matched, and integration did not force unlike groups together, it was probably a preparation artefact. If it survives matched methods and tissue validation but lacks distinct physiology, it is a molecular difference of unknown function. All four steps support a functional human-mouse difference; calling it human-specific also requires comparison with another species such as macaque.

</details>

## Going deeper

### Class lists, sample sizes and toolkit lines

Usoskin and colleagues sequenced 622 single neurons from dissociated lumbar DRGs. Their eleven classes were five neurofilament-rich groups (NF1 to NF5) with myelinated profiles, three non-peptidergic groups (NP1 to NP3), two peptidergic groups (PEP1 and PEP2), and one marked by tyrosine hydroxylase.

Zeisel and colleagues profiled roughly half a million cells across the mouse nervous system. That is the whole census, not a DRG-neuron count.

In adult mouse, TH-positive neurons were identified as C-fibre LTMRs, with mean conduction velocity 0.58 m/s in ex vivo skin-nerve recordings from five neurons.

The Qi toolkit included Th2A-CreER for C-LTMRs, TrkB-CreER for Aδ-LTMRs, Ret-CreER for Aβ rapidly adapting LTMRs and new knock-ins such as Trpm8-T2a-FlpO.

### Calcium-indicator constants

In adult mouse DRG imaged in vivo under urethane, GCaMP6 had a typical rise of about 200 ms and a decay time constant of about 700 ms (Chisholm and colleagues, 2018).

### Sampling schedule and cohort details

Sharma, Ginty and colleagues sampled mouse DRG neurons at E11.5, E12.5, E15.5, P0, P5 and P28 to 42. Renthal and colleagues injured peripheral nerves in mice, mostly males aged 8 to 12 weeks.
