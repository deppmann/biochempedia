## What the names mean

<WordBank terms={['symbiosis', 'lichen', 'mutualism', 'commensalism', 'parasitism', 'host', 'parasite']} />

**Symbiosis** is a close relationship between individuals of different species living together, often for a long time.

A **lichen** is a fungus and an alga or a cyanobacterium (a bacterium that can make its own food) living as one body. Beyond the trade the first film showed, the lichen body soaks up water from the air as well as from rain.

The three kinds:

- **Mutualism** is symbiosis in which all the partners benefit.
- **Commensalism** is symbiosis in which one partner benefits and the others are unaffected.
- **Parasitism** is symbiosis in which one partner benefits and the others are harmed.

## Why partnerships last

The first film showed that no partner chooses kindness or harm. So how does a partnership carry on? In mutualism, partners that lived together survived and reproduced better than ones that lived apart. So over many generations, the partnership carried on. A parasite can carry on the same way, although its host is harmed.

That is how mutualism supports the survival of both partners. A lichen can grow on bare rock. There, the alga alone might dry out, and the fungus alone would have no food, because no fungus can make its own.

These means come from a drought experiment by Asrar and Elhindi, published in 2011. They grew Jubilee marigolds in a greenhouse at King Saud University in Riyadh, Saudi Arabia. Seeds were sown in September 2009. Six weeks after the seedlings moved into pots, the researchers dried and weighed the shoots.

**Predict.** At 25% water, how much more dry mass did plants with fungus have? About what percent more is that than plants without fungus?

<details>
<summary>Work it through</summary>

Difference: 2.62 g minus 2.01 g equals 0.61 g. Percent more: 0.61 divided by 2.01, times 100, is about 30%. The mass without fungus is the comparison, so it belongs in the denominator.

</details>

Now repeat the steps.

1. At 75% water, find the difference and percent more with fungus.
2. The gap in grams is largest at 100% water. Work out the percent more at 100% water. Is the percent gap largest there too?

In the authors' statistics, the smallest gap, 0.17 g at 50% water, could be due to chance. The other three gaps are large enough to trust.

Plan a test of whether a root fungus helps bean plants grow. Name the variables, control, constants, number of plants, metric measurement and safety steps.

<details>
<summary>One way to do it</summary>

- Question: do bean plants grow more shoot mass with a root fungus than without one?
- Independent variable: living root fungus added (a garden-store root fungus powder), or the same powder after an adult has heated it in an oven to kill the fungus.
- Dependent variable: dry shoot mass in grams after six weeks. Cut the shoots and dry them all the same way in paper bags for about a week. Weigh them on a balance, let them dry two more days, and weigh again; use the mass once it stops changing.
- Control: pots with the same potting mix and the same amount of the heat-killed powder, so the only difference is living fungus. If you cannot kill the powder, say that you tested the product, not just the fungus.
- Constants: the same seeds, pot size, amount of potting mix, water, light and temperature. Use a potting mix with little or no added fertilizer, because lots of fertilizer can stop root fungi from helping.
- Trials: at least 10 pots per group, mixed on the shelf so neither group gets better light. Find each group's mean.
- Safety: wear gloves and avoid breathing the powder, follow the label, wash your hands afterward, and never taste anything.
- What results would mean: a higher mean mass with the fungus supports a benefit to the plant in these conditions. If a teacher has a microscope, check stained roots to see if the fungus grew.

</details>

## Going deeper: when the simple model needs refining

The fungus–alga picture can leave members out. In 2016, scientists found yeast cells, another kind of fungus, inside some lichens. They still debate what the yeast does there, how many lichens have it and whether it is a true partner.

The same kind of partnership can also turn out differently for different partners. In a 2022 study, 99 related wheat lines each grew with the same mix of five root fungi for five weeks. Shoot growth ranged from 34% less to 89% more. The fungi raised shoot growth by over 10% on average, but only 12 lines differed clearly from no change. The effect must be measured, not assumed.
