> **The one idea.** Scientists sort living things with several kinds of evidence: what eyes see, what a microscope shows, tests of feeding and cell chemistry, and DNA. New evidence can move a living thing to a new group. On the top rung, scientists compare the same gene or protein across living things and build family trees from many genes before they trust a grouping. Groups that many genes put together probably share a more recent **common ancestor**, an organism both groups descend from.
>
> *Scope (for every "match" or "closer" in this lesson):* the ladder orders kinds of evidence, not dates. One match count is a clue, not proof: one group's gene can change faster, so the closest match is not always the closest relative. A closer relative is not the same kind: fungi are closer to animals, but a mushroom is not an animal.

## Explain: six kingdoms inside three domains

<WordBank terms={['nucleus', 'cell wall', 'chloroplast', 'Bacteria', 'Archaea', 'Eukarya', 'protist', 'fungus', 'DNA sequence', 'common ancestor']} />

You met the levels of classification, from domain down to species, in [that lesson](/lessons/sorting-living-things/). A **nucleus** is the part of a cell that holds its DNA. A **cell wall** is a stiff layer outside the cell membrane. A **chloroplast** is a green part where a cell makes sugar (glucose) using light. **Bacteria** and **Archaea** are two domains of tiny, mostly single-celled organisms with no nucleus; they look alike but differ in cell chemistry and genes. **Eukarya** is the domain of all organisms whose cells have a nucleus.

Many US textbooks use six kingdoms: Bacteria and Archaea each get one, and four are inside Eukarya. A **protist** is an organism with a nucleus that is not a plant, animal or fungus, such as a paramecium; protists are a mixed group (see Going deeper). A **fungus** is an organism such as a mushroom, mold or yeast.

### Design problem

A fungus takes in food. Does baker’s yeast, a single-celled fungus, need sugar to stay active? Plan a test. Active yeast gives off a gas, carbon dioxide, which you can collect in a balloon.

<details>
<summary>One way to do it</summary>

- **Question:** Does yeast give off more gas when it has sugar to take in?
- **Independent variable:** sugar added (0 grams or 10 grams).
- **Dependent variable:** the distance around the balloon, in centimetres, after 30 minutes.
- **Constants:** 7 grams of yeast, 100 millilitres of warm tap water, the same size bottle and balloon, the same room.
- **Control:** the bottle with yeast and water but no sugar.
- **Trials:** three bottles of each kind; find the mean of each.
- **What results mean:** larger balloons with sugar show the yeast takes in sugar and breaks it down. Similar balloons would mean this test did not show a clear effect of sugar.
- **Safety:** use warm tap water, never a flame or stove. Wear goggles, do not taste anything, and wash your hands.

</details>

## Going deeper

DNA shows that protists come from many separate branches of Eukarya. Some look-alikes, such as water molds, share the fungus clues, but DNA shows they are not fungi. And for some genes, the archaea version is more like the version in your own cells than the bacteria version is.
