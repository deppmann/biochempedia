## Explain: four clues sort life into domains and kingdoms

<WordBank terms={['nucleus', 'cell wall', 'chloroplast', 'Bacteria', 'Archaea', 'Eukarya', 'protist', 'fungus', 'DNA sequence', 'common ancestor']} />

You met the levels of classification, from domain down to species, in [that lesson](/lessons/sorting-living-things/). Here you see how scientists choose an organism’s domain and kingdom. They ask four questions:
- Do its cells have a nucleus? A **nucleus** is the part of a cell that holds its DNA.
- Which cell parts does it have? A **cell wall** is a stiff layer outside the cell membrane. A **chloroplast** is a green part where a cell makes sugar (glucose) using light.
- Does it have one cell or many?
- Does it make its own food, or take food in?

The first question splits life into cells with a nucleus and cells without one. Genes and chemistry then split the cells without a nucleus into two domains, so life has three domains. **Bacteria** is a domain of tiny, mostly single-celled organisms with no nucleus. **Archaea** is a second domain of mostly single-celled organisms with no nucleus. **Eukarya** is the domain of all organisms whose cells have a nucleus.

Bacteria and Archaea look alike under a light microscope. But their chemistry and their genes show they are very different from each other, and from Eukarya.

Many US textbooks then use six kingdoms. Bacteria and Archaea each get one. The other four are inside Eukarya.

| Kingdom | Nucleus? | Cell wall? | Chloroplasts? | Cells | Food |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Bacteria | No | Most have one | No | Mostly one | Some make it, some take it in |
| Archaea | No | Most have one | No | Mostly one | Some make it, some take it in |
| Protists | Yes | Some | Some | Most one, some many | Some make it, some take it in |
| Fungi | Yes | Yes | No | Most many; yeast one | Take it in |
| Plants | Yes | Yes | Yes, in green parts | Many | Make it |
| Animals | Yes | No | No | Many | Take it in |

A **protist** is an organism with a nucleus that is not a plant, animal or fungus, such as a paramecium. A **fungus** is an organism with a nucleus and a cell wall but no chloroplasts, which takes in food from what it grows on.

## Explain: new tools bring new evidence

The groups have changed many times, each time after a new kind of evidence.

| Year | Scientist | Groups | New evidence behind it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1735 | Linnaeus | 2 kingdoms of living things | Body parts seen by eye |
| 1866 | Haeckel | 3 kingdoms | Tiny living things that were neither plant nor animal |
| 1938 | Copeland | 4 kingdoms | Cells with no nucleus put in their own kingdom |
| 1969 | Whittaker | 5 kingdoms | Fungi separated by how they get food |
| 1977 | Woese and Fox | 3 main lines of life | Comparing a molecule all cells have (ribosomal RNA) |
| 1990 | Woese, Kandler and Wheelis | 3 domains | Molecule comparisons; a new level above kingdom |

Mushrooms show how this works. Mushrooms stay in one place, like plants. But a closer look showed that fungal cells have no chloroplasts, so fungi cannot make sugar from light. Instead, a fungus releases digesting substances outside its cells and takes in the small pieces. A mushroom on a log feeds on the dead wood. Other fungi feed on living hosts or trade food with living plants.

<LessonFigure src="/figures/domains-kingdoms-and-dna/how-a-mushroom-feeds.webp" width={1200} height={676} alt="AI-generated illustration: mushrooms growing from a fallen, rotting log. An inset zooms in on a few fungal cells against the wood: in three steps the cells release digesting substances, the wood breaks into small pieces, and the cells take the pieces in." caption="A mushroom cannot make sugar from light. It releases digesting substances into the dead wood and takes in the small pieces." />

Then came DNA. A **DNA sequence** is the order of the chemical letters in a stretch of DNA. Each protein is built from a gene’s instructions, so comparing proteins is another way to compare genes. You will read about DNA in [that lesson](/lessons/dna-the-code-of-life/).

The more alike two organisms’ DNA sequences are, the more closely related they are. A **common ancestor** is an organism that two groups both descend from. These comparisons put fungi closer to animals than to plants. That does not make a mushroom an animal. It means fungi and animals share a more recent common ancestor than either shares with plants.

<Support min={2}>**Key idea:** New technology, from microscopes to DNA comparisons, gives new evidence, and new evidence can change how organisms are grouped.</Support>

### Design problem

A fungus takes in food. Does baker’s yeast, a single-celled fungus, need sugar to stay active? Plan a test. Active yeast gives off a gas, carbon dioxide, which you can collect in a balloon.

<details>
<summary>One way to do it</summary>

- **Question:** Does yeast give off more gas when it has sugar to take in?
- **Independent variable:** sugar added (0 grams or 10 grams).
- **Dependent variable:** the distance around the balloon, in centimetres, after 30 minutes.
- **Constants:** 7 grams of yeast, 100 millilitres of warm tap water, the same size bottle and balloon, the same room.
- **Control:** the bottle with yeast and water but no sugar.
- **Trials:** three bottles of each kind; find the mean of each.
- **What results mean:** larger balloons with sugar show the yeast takes in sugar and breaks it down. Similar balloons would mean sugar made no difference here.
- **Safety:** use warm tap water, never a flame or stove. Wear goggles, do not taste anything, and wash your hands.

</details>
