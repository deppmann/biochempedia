## Explain: one cell does every job

<WordBank terms={['unicellular', 'multicellular', 'cilia', 'contractile vacuole', 'specialized cell', 'tissue', 'organ', 'organ system', 'division of labor']} />

A **unicellular** organism is a living thing made of just one cell. A **multicellular** organism is made of many cells. Bacteria and Paramecium are unicellular. You and an oak tree are multicellular. You met the idea that all living things are made of cells in [that lesson](/lessons/cells-everywhere/).

A Paramecium has no tissues or organs. Its one cell still does every life job:

- **It moves.** Short, hair-like parts called **cilia** cover the cell and beat to push it along.
- **It eats.** Food, such as bacteria, is taken into small sacs called food vacuoles and digested.
- **It removes extra water.** Water keeps moving into the cell from the pond. A **contractile vacuole** is a sac that fills with this extra water and squeezes it out. [That lesson](/lessons/diffusion-and-osmosis/) shows why water moves in.
- **It removes wastes.** Bits of food it cannot digest leave through a small opening.
- **It reproduces.** One cell can divide to form two new cells.

So one cell is not simple. Its parts work as a system, as you saw in [that lesson](/lessons/inside-a-cell/).

Volvox, a green alga, sits in between. It is a ball of many cells living together, but with only a few kinds of cells.

<Support min={2}>**Key idea:** A unicellular organism carries out every life process inside one cell, using different parts of that cell for different jobs.</Support>

## Explain: cells, tissues, organs, systems

In a multicellular organism, cells share the work. A **specialized cell** is a cell whose shape and parts suit it to one kind of job. Sharing the work this way is called **division of labor**. Cells are grouped into levels:

- A **tissue** is a group of similar cells doing one job, such as muscle tissue.
- An **organ** is made of different tissues working together, such as the heart.
- An **organ system** is a group of organs working together, such as the heart and blood vessels.

> cell → tissue → organ → organ system → organism

{/* DIAGRAM: a zoom strip from small to large, with five numbered frames: 1 one heart muscle cell, 2 heart muscle tissue, 3 the heart, 4 the circulatory system in a body outline, 5 a whole person */}

Together, the levels help the whole organism grow, repair itself, reproduce, exchange gases, carry out metabolism and respond. The levels depend on each other. If heart tissue is damaged, the whole body can suffer.

A school play works a bit like this. One group builds sets, one runs lights, and one acts. The comparison breaks in one way: no cell chooses its job or plans the show.

Not every multicellular animal has all these levels. Trichoplax, a tiny ocean animal, has several kinds of cells but no organs.

<Support min={2}>**Key idea:** In a multicellular organism, cells form tissues, tissues form organs, and organs form organ systems, so each level does part of the work.</Support>

## Explain: a shape for every job

Specialized cells look very different. Their shapes and parts fit their jobs.

| Cell | Shape and parts | How it fits the job |
|---|---|---|
| Muscle cell (in your arm) | Long and thin, with many mitochondria | Shortens to pull on bones and move the body |
| Nerve cell | Long branches | Carries signals over long distances, in some cases about a metre |
| Red blood cell | Small, flexible disc, dented on both sides; no nucleus when mature (in humans) | Carries oxygen and bends through very narrow vessels |
| Root hair cell (plant) | Long, thin tube growing into the soil | More surface touches the soil, where water comes in |

Bigger organisms do not mostly get bigger cells. Most cells of one kind are about the same size in a mouse and an elephant. The elephant has far more of them.

Scientists have counted or estimated the cells in many organisms. They have also sorted those cells into kinds.

### Design problem

A Paramecium’s contractile vacuole pumps out extra water. Does adding a little salt to its water change how often the vacuole empties? Plan an investigation with a Paramecium culture from a science supply company.

<details>
<summary>One way to do it</summary>

- **Question:** Does the amount of salt in the water change how often the contractile vacuole empties?
- **Independent variable:** salt added, such as 0 g, 0.1 g and 0.2 g per 100 mL of spring water.
- **Dependent variable:** number of times the vacuole empties in 60 seconds.
- **Constants:** same culture, same temperature, same microscope, and the same wait (10 minutes) before counting.
- **Control:** spring water with no salt added.
- **Repeated trials:** count 5 paramecia in each kind of water, and find the mean.
- **Safety:** wash your hands, handle glass slides with care, never taste anything, and give the culture back to your teacher.
- **What it means:** if the counts differ, salt changed this one cell’s water job.

</details>
