## One parent or two sex cells

<WordBank terms={['asexual reproduction', 'sexual reproduction', 'offspring', 'sex cells', 'body cells', 'meiosis', 'fertilization', 'chromosome pair', 'allele']} />

A species lasts only if its members reproduce. Every living thing alive today came from living things before it. **Offspring** are the new living things made when reproduction passes on genetic information.

**Asexual reproduction** has one parent and makes offspring genetically the same, like the runner. Other examples: a potato grows from its eyes, a spider plant makes plantlets, and yeast forms a bud that breaks off. A bacterium splits in two by a simpler kind of division than mitosis. Rare DNA changes mean the copies are not always perfect.

<LessonFigure src="/figures/reproduction-and-meiosis/one-parent-copies.webp" width={1200} height={800} alt="AI-generated illustration: a strawberry plant sends a runner along the ground to a small new plant that has rooted in the soil. Small insets show a potato sprouting from its eyes and a yeast cell with a bud growing from one end." caption="One-parent reproduction usually passes the same genetic information to each new plant or yeast cell." />

**Sexual reproduction** joins two **sex cells**, usually one from each of two parents. Sex cells are the cells that carry genetic information to the next generation. Plants make egg cells and sperm cells carried inside pollen. Sex cells are made by special cells in animal ovaries and testes, and in plant flowers. **Body cells** are the other cells, such as skin, leaf or muscle cells.

## Halve, then restore: the human numbers

A **chromosome pair** is two chromosomes with the same kinds of genes in the same general places, one from the mother and one from the father. (One exception, the X and Y pair, is in Going deeper.) **Meiosis** is the cell division that makes sex cells with half the parent cell's chromosomes, one from each pair. **Fertilization** joins a sperm and an egg into one cell with full pairs again. A typical human body cell has 46 chromosomes in 23 pairs, so an egg or sperm normally has 23:

> 23 chromosomes (egg) + 23 chromosomes (sperm) → 46 chromosomes (fertilized egg)

## Count the mixes

The second film showed the sorting rule: each chromosome pair sorts by chance, independently of the other pairs in this model. An **allele** is one version of a gene. You carry two alleles of most genes, one on each chromosome of a pair. So if a parent has alleles B and b, each sex cell has a 50 percent chance of carrying B and a 50 percent chance of carrying b.

Because the pairs sort independently, the counts multiply. One pair gives 2 kinds of sex cells. Two pairs give 2 × 2 = 4. Three give 2 × 2 × 2 = 8.

**Predict:** What will a fourth pair do to the number of mixes?

It doubles the total again. A person has 23 pairs, so the count is 2 multiplied by itself 23 times: 8,388,608 mixes of whole chromosomes. (That count assumes the two chromosomes in every pair differ.) That is one reason siblings usually receive different mixes. Linking mixes to traits comes in [Predicting Traits](/lessons/predicting-traits/).

| | Mitosis | Meiosis |
|---|---|---|
| Makes | Body cells | Sex cells |
| Used for | Growth, repair, most asexual reproduction | Sexual reproduction |
| Chromosomes in new cells | Same as parent cell | Half, one from each pair |
| New cells | Match the parent cell | Can carry different mixes |

### Design problem

Do plants grown from runners vary less than plants grown from seeds? Plan an investigation with strawberry plants.

<details>
<summary>One way to do it</summary>

- **Question:** Do strawberry plants grown from runners of one parent vary less in leaf length than plants grown from seeds of the same kind of strawberry?
- **Independent variable:** how the plant was made, from a runner (asexual) or from a seed (sexual).
- **Dependent variable:** the length of the largest leaf in millimetres after 8 weeks. Find each group's range, the longest minus the shortest.
- **Constants:** the same soil, pot size, water, light and temperature.
- **Control:** seed-grown plants are the comparison group. The parent plant can be an extra reference.
- **Repeated trials:** at least 10 plants in each group.
- **Safety:** wear gloves for soil, wash your hands, and do not eat the plants.

A smaller range in the runner group would fit the idea that asexual offspring share the parent's genetic information. Some variation will still appear in both groups, because light, water and space also affect growth. Runner plants may start with food from the parent, another difference between the groups.

</details>

## Going deeper

In most males, X and Y form a pair even though they match only in small parts. In plants, meiosis first makes spores and sex cells form later, but they still end up with half.

Table sources: Human and chimpanzee: a 2020 review by Suntsova and Buzdin. Cat: fat stem cells from female cats, with more than 300 dividing cells checked (Algorta and others, 2024). Horse: 44 normal horses (Blue, 1981). Chicken: Fillon and others (1998). Fruit fly: female larvae (Cherezov and others, 2020). Pea: root tips of one line (Yurkevich and others, 2018). Edger and others (2019) pieced together the DNA of 28 chromosomes, half the body-cell number, for the Camarosa kind.
