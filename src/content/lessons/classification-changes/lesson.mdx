## Build the model: evidence tests a tree

<WordBank terms={['classification', 'kingdom', 'domain', 'phylogeny', 'embryology', 'protist', 'ribosomal RNA', 'Archaea', 'Bacteria', 'Eukarya']} />

**Classification** sorts living things into groups based on relationships. The groups nest inside one another. A **kingdom** is a large group, such as plants or animals. A **domain** is the largest group, above kingdom.

A **phylogeny** is the history of ancestors and descendants, often drawn as a branching tree. Classification groups are meant to follow that history. Biologists test a phylogeny with several kinds of evidence:

- **Structure:** body parts and cell parts, such as a nucleus or cell wall.
- **Embryology:** the study of early development, before birth, hatching or sprouting.
- **DNA and RNA:** the order of bases in genes and other molecules.

> **The one idea.** Classification changes when new evidence changes the best-supported picture of ancestry.
>
> *Scope:* New evidence includes a new reading of old evidence. The rule describes modern classification. Early schemes, such as Linnaeus’s, sorted by similarity. Some changes only rename a group. One result does not settle every branch.

## How ribosomal RNA exposed a hidden branch

The first film showed how lists of **ribosomal RNA** (rRNA) pieces compare distant cells. rRNA is the RNA that forms much of every ribosome. Each overlap score ran from 0, no overlap, to 1, the same. In 1977, Woese and Fox interpreted the film’s two-way pattern as evidence for three major lines of descent: eubacteria, archaebacteria and urkaryotes. The scores have no units, are not percentages of DNA and cannot date a split.

<LessonFigure src="/figures/classification-changes/rrna-comparison-method.webp" width={1200} height={676} alt="AI-generated illustration: four steps from left to right. Two methane-making cells and one typical bacterium each hold ribosomes; their rRNA is cut into short pieces, the pieces from each are listed in a column, and the two methane makers' lists share many pieces while a methane maker and the bacterium share few." caption="A repeated pattern of rRNA-piece overlap placed methane makers together and apart from typical bacteria." />

In 1990, Woese, Otto Kandler and Mark Wheelis proposed three domains:

- **Bacteria:** familiar single-celled prokaryotes, such as *E. coli*.
- **Archaea:** single-celled prokaryotes that differ from bacteria in their RNA and membranes.
- **Eukarya:** organisms whose cells have a nucleus, including animals, plants, fungi and protists. The paper spelled this name Eucarya.

## Other groups that moved

The second film gave the logic of each move. Here are the numbers, dates and limits.

- **Fungi and animals.** In 1993, trees built from four protein sequences grouped animals with fungi. Across 25 proteins, the sampled animals and fungi shared four insertions or deletions (added or missing stretches of amino acids). Plants, protists and bacteria lacked them.
- **Two African elephants.** Nuclear DNA support for two species, forest and savanna, began in 2001. A 2010 study added 39,763 DNA bases from 375 places in the nuclear genome. It sampled only one or two animals of each kind, so it could not settle every detail.
- **Whales.** With whales placed among the even-toed hoofed mammals, some biologists call the combined group Cetartiodactyla. Others widen Artiodactyla to include whales.

**Predict 3.** If two group names cover the same whales and hoofed mammals, must they show different phylogenies?

<details>
<summary>Check your prediction</summary>

No. Cetartiodactyla and a widened Artiodactyla can name the same branch. Evidence decides where whales sit on the tree. Which name to use is a separate choice.

</details>

A reclassification makes the groups fit the current evidence better, not perfectly, so they can be revised again.

### Design problem

You think a newly found pond microbe might be an archaeon, not a bacterium. Plan a sequence comparison.

**Predict 5.** Why would comparing it with one archaeon and one bacterium be weaker than using several of each?

<details>
<summary>One way to do it</summary>

- **Question:** Is the pond microbe’s rRNA more similar to known archaea or known bacteria?
- **Independent variable:** the comparison organism, using several known archaea and bacteria.
- **Dependent variable:** percent matching bases in the same rRNA gene.
- **Constants:** the same gene, sequence length and computer method for every pair.
- **Control:** compare two known bacteria and two known archaea to show within-group scores.
- **Repeated trials and sample size:** sequence the pond microbe more than once and use at least five organisms from each domain. One unusual comparison organism could mislead.
- **Second evidence line:** check another gene or membrane fats, since one marker can mislead.
- **Safety:** treat unknown microbes as possible germs and grow them only in a supervised lab.

If the microbe scores clearly higher with every archaeon than with every bacterium, and the second marker agrees, the evidence supports calling it an archaeon.

</details>

## Going deeper: systems change, and testing continues

Each date below marks publication of a scheme, not the year everyone agreed.

| Year | Scheme | What changed |
|---|---|---|
| 1735 | Linnaeus, *Systema naturae* | Living things sorted as plants or animals (minerals too) |
| 1866 | Haeckel | Protista added as a third living kingdom |
| 1938 | Copeland | A four-kingdom system, with bacteria apart |
| 1969 | Whittaker | Five kingdoms, with Fungi apart from plants |
| 1977 | Woese and Fox | RNA evidence for three major lines of descent |
| 1990 | Woese, Kandler and Wheelis | Three domains placed above the kingdoms |

Many US textbooks use six kingdoms: Archaebacteria, Eubacteria, Protista, Fungi, Plantae and Animalia. A **protist** is a eukaryote that is not a plant, animal or fungus. Virginia’s Biology standards keep Protista as a kingdom, so you will use it. Newer trees divide protists into separate groups because some are closer to animals or plants than to other protists.

Testing continues. Since 2015, genome studies have placed eukaryotes inside the Asgard archaea. If that model is right, eukaryotes grew from within Archaea, leaving two primary branches of cellular life rather than three. Studies from 2023 and 2025 disagree about the exact branch, and scientists still use the three domain names.
