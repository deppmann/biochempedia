techniques:
  - name: "Cell-type ablation and genetic rescue in mice"
    blurb: >-
      A defined group of neurons is removed with a Cre-dependent or ligand-targeted approach, or a missing channel is restored only in that group on a null background. Ablation tests necessity and rescue tests what the cells can do alone, each only for the assay, age and driver used.
  - name: "Cheek scratch-versus-wipe assay"
    blurb: >-
      A test substance is injected into the skin of the mouse cheek. At the same injection site, researchers score both hindpaw scratching as itch-related behaviour and forelimb wiping as pain-related behaviour for each stimulus and genotype.
  - name: "Ratiometric calcium imaging of cultured neurons and cells"
    blurb: >-
      A calcium-sensitive dye reports intracellular calcium in transfected HEK293 cells or in cultured dorsal root ganglion neurons as a ligand is applied. It gives responsiveness and potency in a dish, not firing in an animal or sensation in a person.
  - name: "Whole-cell recording in spinal slices"
    blurb: >-
      An optogenetically activated population is stimulated while synaptic currents or spikes are recorded from a neighbouring cell in an acute slice. It tests whether one population can drive another under a defined stimulus pattern.

**Itch is a sense of its own, not weak pain.** The first film separated a receptor route from the sensory neurons carrying it; here we build that experimental logic from skin triggers and species potency through spinal relays, mechanical itch and immune signals.

## Start at the skin: one sensation, several inputs

**Itch** is the unpleasant sensation associated with a desire to scratch. A **pruriceptor** is a primary sensory neuron that detects itch-producing stimuli; the term is functional, so a molecular marker is useful only insofar as an experiment shows what the marked cells do.

This lesson follows two kinds of skin input that can evoke itch:
- A **chemical pruritogen** (such as histamine, chloroquine or a cytokine) acts on receptors or channels at free nerve endings.
- Light touch can evoke scratching in dry or inflamed skin—**alloknesis**. Chemical and mechanical itch use partly different spinal circuits, though not mutually exclusive peripheral neurons.

Histamine comes mostly from skin mast cells and acts on histamine receptors; H1 antagonists block most histamine itch in people. Yet not all chemical itch is histaminergic, and the routes diverge downstream. As the first film introduced in mice, histamine itch involves PLC-beta-3 and TRPV1 (Imamachi and colleagues, *PNAS*, 2009), whereas chloroquine (via MrgprA3) and BAM8-22 (via MrgprC11) scratching falls markedly in *Trpa1*-deficient mice (Wilson and colleagues, *Nature Neuroscience*, 2011)—see [G protein signalling](/lessons/gpcrs-and-g-proteins/) and [PLC, IP3 and DAG](/lessons/plc-ip3-dag-pkc/). Deleting *Trpv1* does not remove chloroquine calcium responses in cultured mouse neurons: carrying a channel does not mean using it for every input.

Primates also have more than one chemical pruriceptor class, a split distinct from chemical versus mechanical itch. Human **microneurography** (recording individual afferents with a fine electrode inserted into a peripheral nerve) and complementary teased-fibre recordings in anaesthetized macaques find mechanically insensitive C fibres that can respond strongly to histamine and mechanosensitive **polymodal** afferents (fibres responding to several stimulus types) that respond to **cowhage** spicules, the itchy hairs of a tropical legume and a nonhistaminergic pruritogen. The response profiles overlap in some preparations (and myelinated afferents can also contribute to cowhage itch). In human psychophysics, the two stimuli can evoke itch of similar peak intensity, sometimes accompanied by pricking or burning, while their time courses and accompanying sensations depend on dose and delivery (LaMotte and colleagues, *Nature Reviews Neuroscience*, 2014). [Touch and pain](/lessons/touch-and-pain/) set itch aside; [spinal pain circuits](/lessons/spinal-pain-circuits/) supplies the dorsal-horn vocabulary used below.

## Testing a labelled line in mouse skin

A strict [labelled line](/lessons/sensory-coding/) is a pathway whose activity means one thing regardless of how it is activated. To test that idea in mice, researchers need an assay that separates itch-like from pain-like output at one site. In the mouse **cheek assay**, hindpaw scratching is scored as itch-related behaviour and forelimb wiping as pain-related behaviour—two motor actions, not a reported sensation. A cheek injection of an **algogen** (a pain-causing substance, such as capsaicin) typically elicits predominantly wiping in wild-type mice, and both actions are scored for each stimulus and genotype.

Those tests give the lesson its organizing rule:

> **Guiding idea.** Trigger, recruited population, spinal route and inhibition together shape scratching; each perturbation supports only the link it tests.
>
> *Scope:* this rule interprets the mouse chemical- and mechanical-itch experiments in this lesson. It does not turn scratching into a report of sensation, make any marked neuron itch-only under every stimulus or establish identical cell classes in people.

Apply that scope to the core model: even a clean TRPV1 rescue does not prove an "itch-only neuron." When Sharif and colleagues (*Neuron*, 2020) drove *MrgprA3* neurons with fast ionotropic stimulation (light-gated channels or P2X3), mice showed mostly **nocifensive** (pain-related defensive) behaviour, whereas Gq-linked stimulation evoked itch behaviour. Likewise, mouse MrgprA3 and human MRGPRX1 are different genes; sharing chloroquine as a ligand does not establish a human MrgprA3 lineage.

### Worked problem: potency is not identity

In transfected HEK293 calcium assays (Liu and colleagues, *Cell*, 2009), chloroquine's functional **EC50**—the concentration giving a half-maximal response—was 27.55 ± 2.03 µM at mouse MrgprA3 and 297.68 ± 2.10 µM at human MRGPRX1 (mean ± SEM). The central ratio is 297.68 / 27.55 = 10.81: chloroquine is about eleven times less potent at the human receptor here.

Impose a deliberately simple one-site model with unit Hill slope, zero baseline and maximum 1:

> **R(C) = C / (EC50 + C)**

**Predict.** The human EC50 is 10.81 times the mouse value. At C = 30 µM, will this model therefore predict 10.81 times less response at the human receptor?

<details>
<summary>Check your prediction</summary>

No. Because the curve is nonlinear and bounded at 1, an EC50 ratio does not equal the response ratio at every concentration.

</details>

At C = 30 µM:
- **Mouse MrgprA3:** 30 / (27.55 + 30) = 30 / 57.55 = 0.521
- **Human MRGPRX1:** 30 / (297.68 + 30) = 30 / 327.68 = 0.0916
- **Response ratio:** 0.521 / 0.0916 ≈ 5.69, not 10.81.

At 30 µM the mouse receptor is already past half-maximal while the human receptor sits under a tenth of maximum; only at very low concentrations (C ≪ EC50) does the ratio approach 10.81, and at very high concentrations both saturate toward 1. The assumed slope and matched maxima are ours, not fitted values, and functional EC50 in a transfected cell is not binding affinity, neuronal firing, perceived itch or proof that mouse and human neurons match.

## Into the spinal cord: the relay and its brakes

The peripheral tests identify receptors and sensory populations that contribute to scratching. The second film uses spinal rescue and bypass to support one functional relay order, then shows how removing inhibition can increase scratching.

<LessonFilm film={frontmatter.films[0]} />

The second film presented one supported functional order in mice—sensory **Nppb** acts via spinal **NPRA**-expressing cells, with **GRP** acting via **GRPR** farther downstream in this model (Mishra and Hoon, *Science*, 2013; with GRPR-mutant and GRPR-ablation support from Sun and Chen, *Nature*, 2007, and Sun and colleagues, *Science*, 2009)—while noting it does not prove direct synapses. Injected GRP still evoked scratching after partial NPRA-cell ablation, and blocking GRPR or ablating GRPR neurons reduced Nppb-evoked scratching in the primary study. Three extensions go beyond the film.

First, **Nppb marks a subset of pruriceptors.** In the Mishra and Hoon model, Nppb comes from a subset of TRPV1-positive primary sensory neurons rather than dorsal-horn cells. Transcriptomics enriches *Nppb* mainly in the **Nppb- and somatostatin (*Sst*)-marked** afferent class rather than the *MrgprA3* class. *MrgprA3* afferents may signal through other transmitters, and their downstream targets and any indirect recruitment of Nppb signalling require separate tests.

Second, **developmental loss of inhibitory cells can increase scratching.** Mutating *Bhlhb5*—expressed transiently in developing dorsal horn—eliminates a subset of inhibitory interneurons, and mutants tested mainly at four weeks scratch excessively, linking the mutant phenotype to developmental loss of dorsal-horn inhibition (Ross and colleagues, *Neuron*, 2010). Later experiments identified the kappa-opioid peptide **dynorphin** in a subset of Bhlhb5-dependent inhibitory interneurons, implicated kappa-opioid signalling at or downstream of GRPR neurons in suppressing scratching, and found that these interneurons receive input from sensory neurons responsive to menthol and other counter-stimuli (Kardon and colleagues, *Neuron*, 2014; reviewed by Dong and Dong, *Neuron*, 2018)—helping explain why counter-stimuli and kappa-opioid agonists ease itch while mu agonists can provoke it.

### The contested step: where does spinal GRP come from?

The functional order leaves open how much GRP reaches GRPR neurons from **primary sensory afferents**, **dorsal-horn interneurons**, or both. Mouse studies using different readouts and targeting strategies report differing results:

| Study | Approach | Key finding |
|---|---|---|
| Solorzano et al. (*J. Neurosci.*, 2015) | Expression & antiserum test | Abundant spinal *Grp*, not uninjured DRG neurons in their assays; antiserum needed high dilution |
| Albisetti et al. (*J. Neurosci.*, 2019) | Spinally restricted GRP-cell ablation | Reduced itch-related behaviour |
| Pagani et al. (*Neuron*, 2019) | Slice recordings (3–5-week mice) | Repeated burst firing of spinal GRP neurons progressively excited GRPR neurons |
| Barry et al. (*Nat. Commun.*, 2020) | Sensory *Grp* KO; intersectional spinal ablation | Sensory KO cut nonhistaminergic scratching (histamine spared); spinal ablation spared itch in tests used |

Null-validated pro-GRP antibody mapping adds further excitatory dorsal-horn candidates rather than settling the source (Gutierrez-Mecinas and colleagues, *Neuroscience*, 2023; percentages in Going deeper). Because targeting strategies, ages and reporter coverage differ, each result stands for what it tested.

**Predict.** Suppose sensory and spinal sources both release GRP and partly compensate for one another. Which comparison is most likely to expose that redundancy?

<summary>Check your prediction</summary>

Compare each source-specific deletion with the combined deletion, then add source-specific rescues. Either single deletion can look modest when both sources contribute.

## Mechanical itch: a partly separate route

**Reminder:** trigger, recruited population, spinal route and inhibition together shape scratching, and each perturbation supports only the link it tests.

The second film placed **neuropeptide Y (NPY)**-lineage dorsal-horn interneurons at a mechanical-itch gate whose removal raised touch-evoked scratching—surviving GRPR-cell ablation—without raising tested chemical itch or pain (Bourane and colleagues, *Science*, 2015). To ablate or silence NPY-lineage interneurons in the dorsal spinal cord and medulla, Bourane and colleagues used an **intersectional** strategy requiring both *NPY::Cre* activity and FlpO activity driven by *Lbx1*, a developmental marker of these interneurons. Together the two drivers define the targeted lineage. A separate Cre-only reporter cross recorded past *NPY::Cre* activity in spinal interneurons where recombination occurred; the P30 percentage below comes from that cross.

**Predict.** At postnatal day 30, only 35 percent of reporter-positive cells had detectable NPY. Does an *NPY::Cre* reporter identify current NPY expression or developmental history?

<details>
<summary>Check your prediction</summary>

Developmental history: the reporter records prior driver activity in cells where recombination occurred. At P30, 35 percent of the labelled spinal interneurons had detectable NPY, so most labelled cells were not currently NPY-positive.

</details>

What circuit does that NPY-lineage gate restrain? Several types of cutaneous low-threshold mechanoreceptors, including fast, myelinated **A-beta** afferents, provide input to spinal excitatory interneurons of the **neuropeptide Y1 receptor** (a receptor for NPY) lineage; ablating or silencing Y1-marked spinal neurons reduced mechanical itch, and Acton and colleagues also recorded monosynaptic inhibitory currents in Y1-expressing cells when they stimulated NPY-lineage interneurons (Acton and colleagues, *Cell Reports*, 2019). From the dorsal horn, ascending *Calcrl*- and *Lbx1*-marked spinoparabrachial neurons projecting to the brainstem's **parabrachial nucleus** were required for normal mechanical-itch scratching (Ren and colleagues, *Neuron*, 2023). GRPR's role remains model-dependent: mechanically evoked scratching survived GRPR ablation in Bourane's paradigm, whereas Chen and colleagues (*Nature Communications*, 2020) found that *Tac2-Cre* interneurons receive A-beta input and contact GRPR neurons, and GRPR-neuron ablation nearly abolished mechanically evoked scratching in their assay.

A **peripheral brake** also acts in skin via **Merkel cells** (see [Merkel cells](/lessons/merkel-cells/)) and Piezo2. Deleting *Piezo2* across skin epithelium (*K14-Cre*)—or in Merkel cells in a smaller group via tamoxifen-inducible *Atoh1-CreERT*—raised touch-evoked scratching, whereas **chemogenetic activation** (using an engineered receptor and designer ligand) of Merkel cells reduced alloknesis in a dry-skin model (Feng and colleagues, *Science*, 2018). In that 2018 study, aged mice also scratched to light **von Frey** filaments (calibrated nylon hairs) that did not evoke scratching in young mice.

**Predict.** Do those two directions of effect make Merkel-cell Piezo2 signalling a generator or a brake for touch-evoked scratching in these experiments?

<details>
<summary>Check your prediction</summary>

A brake: removing Piezo2 raised touch-evoked scratching, whereas activating Merkel cells lowered alloknesis. These experiments did not establish a direct Merkel-to-NPY synapse.

</details>

Keep that endpoint narrow: a later study showed Merkel-cell Piezo2 signalling can *promote* spontaneous itch in chronic dry skin (Feng and colleagues, *Science Translational Medicine*, 2022), so the brake applies to touch-evoked scratching, not every itch behaviour.

Nor is the peripheral split absolute. Deleting *Piezo1* in peripheral neurons reduced mechanically evoked scratching; *Piezo1* transcript appeared in most *Nppb*-positive DRG neurons (a molecularly overlapping pruriceptor class whose route for mechanically evoked scratching was not established by these experiments), and combined *Piezo1* and *Piezo2* knockdown left nearly all cultured *Sst*-marked neurons lacking mechanically activated responses (Hill and colleagues, *Nature*, 2022). A pruriceptor can therefore transduce touch; see [mechanotransduction channels](/lessons/mechanotransduction-channels/) and the [sensory neuron atlas](/lessons/sensory-neuron-atlas/).

Finally, scratching itself supplies state-dependent inhibition. The second film depicts lower histamine-evoked firing after scratching in primate lumbar spinothalamic tract recordings and shows baseline and capsaicin-evoked activity as unchanged. In the primary study, scratching before histamine produced no comparable suppression. Across the 11 neurons tested during a capsaicin response, mean firing increased in the first 10 seconds after scratching (Davidson and colleagues, *Nature Neuroscience*, 2009; histamine-cell counts in Going deeper).

## Immune molecules can trigger or change gain

Studies relevant to inflammatory skin disease show that some immune signalling proteins (**cytokines**) act directly on sensory neurons. In the mouse experiments below, TSLP and IL-31 can evoke scratching, and IL-4 can increase neuronal responses to other pruritogens.

Separate culture and mouse experiments support acute triggering:
- Epithelial **thymic stromal lymphopoietin (TSLP)** produced calcium responses in a subset of cultured TRPA1-positive mouse DRG neurons, and skin injection elicited rapid, TRPA1-dependent scratching in mice (Wilson and colleagues, *Cell*, 2013).
- **IL-31** acts via a receptor complex containing **IL-31 receptor alpha (IL-31RA)**. The study detected IL-31RA in human donor DRG neurons and showed co-expression with TRPV1 in mouse DRG. Mice deficient in either *Trpv1* or *Trpa1* scratched less in response to IL-31 (Cevikbas and colleagues, *Journal of Allergy and Clinical Immunology*, 2014).

**IL-4** and **IL-13** work differently. IL-4 evoked calcium responses in cultured mouse and human-donor DRG neurons, and IL-13 did so in mouse neurons (Oetjen and colleagues, *Cell*, 2017).

**Predict.** Does a calcium response in culture require either cytokine to produce acute scratching after skin injection?

<details>
<summary>Check your prediction</summary>

No. Cellular responsiveness in culture, acute behavioural output and sensitization are separate claims.

</details>

Under the tested conditions, high-dose IL-4 or IL-13 alone did not evoke acute scratching. Instead, IL-4 sensitized cultured neurons to subthreshold pruritogens, and deleting *Il4ra* or *Jak1* in sensory neurons reduced scratching in mouse chronic-itch models (see [JAK-STAT](/lessons/jak-stat/)). A cytokine can thus raise neuronal gain without acting as an acute trigger.

Two clinical antibody drugs target these routes: **nemolizumab** blocks IL-31RA, and **dupilumab** blocks IL-4R-alpha (shared by IL-4 and IL-13). Clinical chronic itch means itch lasting longer than six weeks. These studies do not partition a drug's effects between neurons and immune cells; for persistent pain, see [chronic pain](/lessons/chronic-pain/).

### Design problem: where does spinal GRP come from?

Design an experiment that tests whether sensory afferents, dorsal-horn interneurons, or both supply the GRP acting on GRPR, given the disagreements above.

<details>
<summary>One way to do it</summary>

First list the targeting strategy, reporter coverage and age behind every published result (Cre line, intersection, fraction of GRP cells reached). Then compare validated sensory-neuron and spinal-neuron *Grp* deletions alongside a double deletion, matched controls and source-specific rescues in one laboratory, one mouse background and one age, scoring the same histamine and nonhistaminergic assays.

Test peptide necessity with the *Grp* deletions and rescues. To test cell necessity, separately silence each candidate population with *Grp* intact and score the same behaviours. Pair these comparisons with measurements of endogenous peptide release. Do not infer localized release from bulk transcript or peptide alone: measure mature GRP release at the relevant spinal site (validating every antibody in *Grp*-null tissue) and GRPR-dependent responses in every group. In spinal slices, stimulate each source while suppressing or removing *Grp* from the other, with GRPR block as a control, so that indirect recruitment of local GRP interneurons is not mistaken for direct afferent release.

Remember that a larger double-deletion effect is consistent with redundancy but can also reflect incomplete single deletion or nonlinear summation. Slice physiology and localized release address source and mechanism, while matched behaviour tests relevance to each specific itch assay.

</details>

## Going deeper

### Ages, treatment schedules and rescue scope

Liu and colleagues (*Cell*, 2009) tested 2- to 3-month-old male mice carrying a germline deletion of the *Mrgpr* cluster and first showed in transfected HEK293 cells that chloroquine activates mouse MrgprA3 and human MRGPRX1. Han and colleagues (*Nature Neuroscience*, February 2013 issue, online 2012) tested male mice in the same age range; inducible diphtheria-toxin ablation of *MrgprA3*-positive neurons began at five weeks of age and behaviour was tested three weeks later. Each ablation or rescue supports conclusions only for the driver, age and assays used.

In Mishra and Hoon (*Science*, 2013), intrathecal Nppb (injected into the fluid around the spinal cord) evoked scratching in wild-type and *Nppb*-null mice, showing intact downstream responsiveness without proving direct synapses, and GRP still evoked scratching after ~70 percent of NPRA-expressing spinal cells were removed.

### GRP antiserum titration and pro-GRP co-expression

Solorzano and colleagues (*Journal of Neuroscience*, 2015) titrated a commonly used GRP antiserum against *Grp*-null tissue and found selectivity for GRP only at high dilutions. Using pro-GRP antibodies validated in *Grp*-null tissue, Gutierrez-Mecinas and colleagues (*Neuroscience*, 2023) scored pro-GRP immunoreactivity within sampled groups of VGLUT2-positive dorsal-horn axonal boutons. The frequencies were 56 percent for pro-NPFF-positive boutons, 15.7 percent for substance P-positive/CGRP-negative boutons and 14.7 percent for PPTB-positive boutons (PPTB is the NKB precursor). The corresponding frequencies were 7.3 percent for pro-CCK-positive boutons, 5.3 percent for neurotensin-positive boutons and 5 percent for GRPR-reporter-labelled boutons.

### Mechanical itch: ages, forces and counts

In Feng and colleagues (*Science*, 2018), mice older than 24 months scratched to von Frey forces of 0.02 to 0.16 gram-force that did not evoke scratching in 2-month-old mice.

In Hill and colleagues (*Nature*, 2022), deleting *Piezo1* in peripheral neurons of 2- to 6-month-old mice reduced mechanically evoked scratching; *Piezo1* transcript appeared in 92 percent of *Nppb*-positive DRG neurons, and after combined *Piezo1* and *Piezo2* knockdown, 32 of 33 cultured *Sst*-marked neurons lacked mechanically activated responses.

Davidson and colleagues (*Nature Neuroscience*, 2009) recorded extracellularly from lumbar spinothalamic neurons of a primate in vivo (species not specified here). For eight histamine-responsive cells, firing during the first 10 seconds after scratching was 62 ± 8 percent of the pre-scratch level (mean ± SEM)—a firing measurement, not a reported sensation.

### Culture and animal details in the immune experiments

Wilson and colleagues (*Cell*, 2013) used mouse DRG cultures from P18 to P30 animals; 4.1 ± 0.6 percent of cultured neurons responded to TSLP with calcium. Oetjen and colleagues (*Cell*, 2017) used cultured DRG neurons from 8- to 12-week-old mice.
