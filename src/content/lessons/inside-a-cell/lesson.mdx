>

## Explain: the parts and their jobs

<WordBank terms={['organelle', 'nucleus', 'cell membrane', 'cytoplasm', 'endoplasmic reticulum', 'mitochondria', 'vacuole', 'metabolism', 'photosynthesis', 'cellular respiration']} />

For reference, here are the first film's six parts, with details it left out. An **organelle** is a small part inside a cell that does a special job.

- **Nucleus:** holds the instructions that guide what the cell makes and does.
- **Cell membrane:** controls what enters and leaves, and lets the cell sense its surroundings.
- **Cytoplasm:** everything inside the membrane except the nucleus, fluid and organelles together. Many reactions happen in the fluid.
- **Endoplasmic reticulum** (ER): folded membranes that help make and move many proteins and fats.
- **Mitochondria:** release much of the energy stored in food such as sugar (glucose) and fats, in a form the cell can use.
- **Vacuole:** stores water, food and wastes. Many animal cells have only small sacs, and sacs called lysosomes break down wastes and worn-out parts.

The first film followed one job; the table maps the others.

| Job | Parts that help |
|---|---|
| Using energy | Mitochondria, cytoplasm |
| Making molecules | Nucleus (instructions), ER, cytoplasm |
| Removing wastes | Cell membrane, vacuoles and other small sacs |
| Responding to the environment | Cell membrane, nucleus |

Not every cell has every part. A mature human red blood cell has no nucleus and no mitochondria. Bacteria have no nucleus ([that lesson](/lessons/domains-kingdoms-and-dna/)). Plant cells share the first five parts and add a cell wall, a large central vacuole and, in green cells, chloroplasts ([that lesson](/lessons/plant-and-animal-cells/)).

## Explain: the parts work as a team

The first film's stalled repair job was a cartoon. Scientists saw a real case in 1968. They added chemicals that stopped energy release in the mitochondria to slices of guinea pig pancreas. New proteins made to be sent out of the cell got stuck in the ER and could not move on toward the cell's exit. The same teamwork runs every life process.

- **Growth and repair** use the path the first film followed: nucleus, ER and mitochondria.
- **Reproduction.** Before a cell divides, it grows and copies its instructions, so it needs the same team ([cell division](/lessons/cells-divide/)).
- **Gas exchange.** In a cell using oxygen, oxygen moves in across the cell membrane, and carbon dioxide moves out. Mitochondria use the oxygen. In bright light, a green leaf cell can give out more oxygen than it takes in. That happens when its photosynthesis runs faster than its respiration ([how gases cross](/lessons/diffusion-and-osmosis/)).
- **Response.** A signal reaches the cell, often at the cell membrane, and the cell changes what it makes or does.
- **Metabolism** means **all the chemical reactions inside the cell**, such as building molecules up and breaking them down. Two key examples: in **photosynthesis**, plant cells use radiant energy (light) to make sugar (glucose) ([photosynthesis](/lessons/photosynthesis-feeds-the-world/)). In **cellular respiration**, cells break down sugar and release its stored energy ([releasing energy](/lessons/releasing-energy-from-food/)).

<Support min={2}>**Key idea:** Organelles work as one system, so trouble in one part slows many life processes.</Support>

### Design problem

Does the spacing of a counting grid change your answer? Plan a test using a printed electron microscope picture of a cell and clear grids you draw yourself.

<LessonFigure src="/figures/inside-a-cell/counting-points-on-mitochondria.webp" width={1200} height={800} alt="AI-generated illustration: a grey picture of a thin slice of a cell with oval mitochondria, covered by an even grid of dots. Dots that land on mitochondria are filled gold and the rest are hollow. A zoom shows one mitochondrion with gold dots on it and a hollow dot beside it." caption="The counting rule your grids will use: the share of points that land on mitochondria estimates the share of the cell's volume they fill." />

<details>
<summary>One way to do it</summary>

- **Question:** Does grid spacing change the measured share of a cell filled by mitochondria?
- **Independent variable:** spacing between grid points, such as 10 mm, 5 mm and 2 mm.
- **Dependent variable:** percent of points that land on mitochondria.
- **Constants:** the same printed picture, the same rule for a point on an edge, and the same lighting.
- **Control:** the finest grid (2 mm) is the comparison for the others.
- **Repeated trials:** place each grid in three different spots on the picture and count each time. Find the mean for each spacing.
- **Safety:** use a ruler and pencil only; no sharp tools needed.
- **What it means:** if the coarse grid gives values far from the fine grid, it needs more points to be trusted.

</details>
