## Explore: build a tree one trait at a time

A **cladogram** is a branching diagram of proposed relationships. In the builder, choose a trait, and the animals that lack it branch off first. Each trait marks a group within the whole sample. Added from backbone to hair, the traits pick out smaller and smaller groups. Another order changes the steps, not the finished tree. A DNA panel then checks the tree with another kind of evidence.

## Explain: the evidence for shared ancestry

<WordBank terms={['classification', 'homologous structures', 'embryology', 'fossil record', 'common ancestor', 'genetic code', 'cladogram', 'shared derived characteristic', 'branch point']} />

**Classification** is the grouping of organisms by how they are related. The first film's limb pattern is a **homologous structure**: a body part built from the same basic parts because it came from a **common ancestor**. A common ancestor is an earlier population from which two or more groups descended. Name the bone sets yourself in the figure.

<ScaffoldFigure src="/figures/building-a-tree-of-life/forelimb-homology.webp" width={1200} height={800} keyItems={["upper arm bone", "two forearm bones", "wrist bones", "finger bones"]} given={[0, 3]} alt="AI-generated illustration: the forelimb bones of a human arm, a bat wing and a whale flipper side by side, with matching bones shaded the same colour in all three. Four numbered circles point to the bone sets on the human arm." caption="A hand, wing and flipper do different jobs but keep the same inherited bone pattern. Name the four numbered bone sets, then find each one in the bat and the whale." />

**Embryology** is the study of development before birth or hatching. Early fish, chick and human embryos have a tail and neck folds called pharyngeal arches. In fish, openings form between the arches, and several arches support gills. In humans the grooves between the arches never open into gill slits. The arches form parts of the jaw, ear and throat, and the tail shrinks. Human embryos do not turn into fish.

The **fossil record** is the collection of preserved remains and traces of past life, placed in time order by the rock layers they are found in, including groups that no longer exist.

Beyond the film's DNA insertions, scientists compare matching DNA and protein sequences. The more two species' sequences match, the more recently they usually shared an ancestor. At a deeper scale, almost every organism reads DNA with the same **genetic code**. The code matches each three-letter word in a gene's message to an amino acid or a stop signal. Some organisms and mitochondria read a few of these words differently, but these are small changes to the same code. Most organisms also share basic reactions for breaking down sugar. A shared code and shared chemistry are best explained by common ancestry. [The protein-synthesis lesson](/lessons/protein-synthesis/) shows how the code works.

<Support min={2}>**Evidence check:** A nearly universal genetic code and shared chemistry point to a common origin for life, while smaller differences help resolve later branches.</Support>

## Explain: read and build a cladogram

The second film defined a **shared derived characteristic**, a trait that first appeared in one ancestor and passed to its descendants, and a **branch point**, or node, the common ancestor of every branch that grows from it. It also showed that spinning branches or stretching unscaled lines changes only the drawing. It left out two points. A trait counts as derived only compared with the rest of the sample. And a node is an ancestral population that lived in the past, not a single individual; living species sit at the tips.

### Design problem

Plan a study to test whether whales are closer to hippos or to pigs, using inserted pieces of DNA. Say what you would measure and what result would support each answer.

<details>
<summary>One way to do it</summary>

- **What you measure:** at many matching DNA spots, whether an inserted piece of DNA is present or absent in whales, hippos, pigs, cows and an outgroup such as a horse.
- **Prediction if whales and hippos are closest:** several insertions found in whales and hippos but not in pigs.
- **Prediction if hippos and pigs are closest:** several insertions shared by hippos and pigs but not by whales.
- **Constants:** look at the same DNA spots in every species, and use the same lab method for each sample.
- **Checks:** sequence each spot to confirm the insertion is really there. Test more than one individual of each species, and use many spots, not one, so that a single odd result cannot decide the answer.
- **Ethics:** use DNA from museum tissue, stranded animals or zoo samples, so no animal is harmed for the study.

</details>

## Going deeper

### Classification ranks and embryo drawings

Classification is also a nested list from broad to specific: domain, kingdom, phylum, class, order, family, genus, species. Each level holds fewer organisms that share more traits. [The domains-and-kingdoms lesson](/lessons/domains-and-kingdoms/) covers the broadest levels.

Some old textbook drawings made vertebrate embryos look more alike than they really are. Biologists now compare photographs of real embryos. The shared structures are evidence; the embryos are not identical.
