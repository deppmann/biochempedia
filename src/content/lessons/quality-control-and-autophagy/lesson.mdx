      protein in the lumen has to come back out into the cytosol to meet the proteasome. Genetics

      ATPase Cdc48 completing extraction. Because the system was built from defined parts, each
      requirement could be tested by leaving one part out, a test of what a defined set of parts can do on its own,

techniques:
  - name: "Cycloheximide chase"
    blurb: >-
      Cycloheximide stops new protein synthesis, and samples taken over time are immunoblotted for
      one protein. The rate at which its band fades gives its degradation rate, separate from its
      rate of synthesis. A protein that stays stable in a mutant points to a block in its
      destruction.
  - name: "LC3-II turnover (flux) assay"
    blurb: >-
      LC3-II, the lipid-linked form of LC3 on autophagosome membranes, is measured by immunoblot
      with and without a lysosomal inhibitor such as bafilomycin A1. The difference between the two
      shows how much LC3-II the lysosome destroyed in that time, which a single steady-state
      measurement cannot.
  - name: "Mutant screen and complementation"
    blurb: >-
      Mutagenized cells are screened for a visible failure, such as no autophagic bodies in the
      vacuole. Crossing mutants and asking which pairs restore the normal phenotype sorts them into
      complementation groups, each normally corresponding to one gene.
  - name: "Proteoliposome reconstitution"
    blurb: >-
      Purified membrane proteins are inserted into synthetic lipid vesicles, and the reaction is
      run with defined parts. Leaving out one part at a time, or using an inactive version, shows
      what each is required for in that minimal system.

      UGGT inspects glycoproteins released from calnexin and calreticulin and re-glucosylates the

  - stem: "Which mammalian UPR sensor removes a 26-nucleotide intron from XBP1 mRNA?"
    choices:
      - "BiP, an ER lumen chaperone of the Hsp70 family"
      - "ATF6, an ER membrane protein cut by Golgi proteases"
      - "IRE1, an ER membrane kinase that is also an endoribonuclease"
      - "PERK, an ER membrane kinase that phosphorylates eIF2α"

    rationale: >-
      IRE1 has a cytosolic RNase domain that cuts XBP1 mRNA, removing the 26-nucleotide intron so
      the active factor XBP1s is made. PERK is a kinase whose target is eIF2α, not an mRNA. ATF6 is
      itself cut by Site-1 and Site-2 proteases; it splices nothing. BiP binds unfolded proteins in
      the lumen and has no RNase activity.
  - stem: "When PERK phosphorylates eIF2α during ER stress, what happens to protein synthesis?"
    choices:
      - "Most translation is unchanged, while ATF4 mRNA is spliced"
      - "Most translation slows, while ATF4 is translated preferentially"
      - "Translation of all mRNAs rises, and ATF4 rises along with it"
      - "Translation of all mRNAs stops, including the mRNA for ATF4"
    answer: 1
    rationale: >-
      Phosphorylated eIF2α slows initiation on most mRNAs, which lowers the load of new proteins
      entering the ER, while ATF4 mRNA is translated preferentially. Translation does not rise
      overall; slowing it is the point. It does not stop completely, and ATF4 is the exception that
      escapes. Splicing is the IRE1 branch acting on XBP1 mRNA, not something PERK does to ATF4.
  - stem: "In macroautophagy, how does cytoplasmic cargo reach lysosomal hydrolases?"
    choices:
      - "Cargo is enclosed in a double-membrane vesicle that fuses with a lysosome"
      - "Cargo is tagged with ubiquitin and unfolded by a barrel in the cytosol"
      - "Cargo is taken up at the cell surface and sorted into an endosome"
      - "Single proteins are threaded through a pore in the lysosome membrane"
    answer: 0
    rationale: >-
      A growing membrane closes around cytoplasm to form an autophagosome with two membranes, and
      its fusion with a lysosome delivers the inner vesicle and its contents for digestion.
      Threading single proteins across the lysosome membrane describes a different route, chaperone-mediated autophagy,
      and cannot take whole organelles. A ubiquitin-tagged protein unfolded in a cytosolic barrel

  - stem: "Mouse fibroblasts lacking IRE1α are treated with tunicamycin, which blocks N-glycosylation. Which result is expected?"
    choices:
      - "XBP1 mRNA is spliced normally by the PERK kinase"
      - "BiP is induced only once tunicamycin is removed"
      - "No UPR at all, since IRE1 is the only sensor in mammals"
      - "ATF6 is still cleaved and BiP mRNA is still induced"
    answer: 3
    rationale: >-
      Mammals have three UPR branches, and in IRE1α-null fibroblasts ATF6 was still cleaved and

In a strain lacking the ubiquitin ligase Hrd1, the band barely changes over 90 minutes. What fraction of the protein is left in wild-type cells at 60 minutes, and what does the mutant result show?"
    choices:
      - "One eighth is left; Hrd1 is needed to make the protein fold"
      - "One third is left; Hrd1 is needed to synthesize the protein"
      - "One eighth is left; Hrd1 is needed to degrade the protein"
      - "One half is left; Hrd1 is needed to degrade the protein"
    answer: 2
    rationale: >-
      Sixty minutes is three half-lives of 20 minutes, so one half times one half times one half
      leaves one eighth, or 12.5%. Cycloheximide stops synthesis, so a band that barely changes in
      the mutant means the protein is hardly being destroyed, which points to a block in degradation.
      One third divides the time by the half-life instead of halving three times, and synthesis is
      not measured in a chase. One half is the amount after a single half-life. The folding answer
      misreads a stable band as rescue; a chase measures disappearance, not shape.

    text: >-

      from leaving. Failed proteins are pushed back out into the cell fluid, tagged with a small
      protein called ubiquitin, and destroyed by the proteasome, a barrel-shaped protein shredder.

      of digestive enzymes. That process is called autophagy, meaning self-eating.

**Starve a yeast cell of nitrogen and it starts to digest pieces of its own cytoplasm. A mutant that cannot do this loses viability faster than normal yeast during the starvation. Why?**

The film showed one moving map: quality-control cargo arrives, takes a disposal route and leaves, so a pile alone cannot reveal its flow. The rest of the page first tests that rule on LC3-II data, then asks how the ER retries or rejects a client and how a damaged mitochondrion is selected; each case comes back to arrival versus clearance. The film's closing questions—how fast does material arrive, and how fast does it leave?—are answered first in the worked problem below.

In 1993, Miki Tsukada and Yoshinori Ohsumi found that the autophagy-defective yeast mutant *apg1* failed to induce protein degradation in the vacuole, the yeast counterpart of a lysosome, and lost viability faster during nitrogen starvation. The experiment supports a role in survival without establishing a sole cause.

> **The one idea to carry through this lesson.** For a defined cellular pool over a defined interval, its change equals arrival minus clearance. A high level measured at one time therefore cannot tell whether arrival increased or clearance slowed. Temporarily blocking a clearance route and measuring the additional accumulation can estimate flux through that route, if the block is complete and does not itself change arrival over that interval.

## Physical constraints on the two exits

The film's two routes have different physical jobs because they admit cargo differently. The **26S proteasome**, found in the cytosol and nucleus, has a hollow 20S core whose active sites face inward. Its regulatory caps recognise ubiquitin chains, remove ubiquitin for reuse, and use ATP to unfold and thread an engaged protein into the core. The tagging system is covered in [ubiquitin and proteolysis](/lessons/ubiquitin-and-proteolysis/), and the chemistry in [protein turnover](/lessons/protein-turnover-amino-acid-catabolism/).

In a **lysosome**, a V-ATPase—an ATP-powered proton pump—acidifies the lumen so digestive enzymes called hydrolases work well (see [organelles as environments](/lessons/organelles-as-environments/)). Material also arrives from outside by endocytosis. In macroautophagy, the main form of autophagy, a cup-shaped membrane grows around a portion of cytoplasm and closes into an autophagosome bounded by two membranes. The autophagosome fuses with a lysosome, and the inner membrane and its contents are digested; the building blocks return to the cytosol. Starvation can raise this through nutrient-sensing kinases such as mTOR (see [mTOR and AMPK](/lessons/mtor-and-ampk/)). Ohsumi's yeast genes, later renamed *ATG* genes, encode much of the machinery, including a system that attaches LC3 (Atg8 in yeast) to phosphatidylethanolamine; this lipid-linked **LC3-II** coats autophagosome membranes, and inner-membrane copies are destroyed in the lysosome.

The film's individual-protein-versus-bulk contrast follows from these physical constraints, but it is a scoped model rather than a rule that cargo size alone determines fate. Going deeper gives the overlap that qualifies this comparison.

## Read a flow, not a still picture

The film's arrival-minus-clearance rule becomes useful when an experiment turns one of those rates down. Counting LC3 dots or measuring one LC3-II band still cannot distinguish faster carrier formation from slower destruction.

**Worked problem: autophagic flux.** Cultured cells are immunoblotted for LC3-II, with each band normalized to actin and control set to 1.0. Bafilomycin A1 inhibits the V-ATPase, suppressing lysosomal degradation under these assay conditions. It is added for the last few hours. Assume a saturating dose, high enough for a complete block, plus linear LC3-II accumulation and no change in carrier formation over that interval.

| Condition | Without bafilomycin | With bafilomycin |
|---|---|---|
| Control | 1.0 | 2.0 |
| Starved | 1.5 | 4.5 |
| Drug D | 3.0 | 3.2 |

> flux over the interval = LC3-II with bafilomycin − LC3-II without bafilomycin

**Predict.** Drug D has the highest LC3-II level without bafilomycin. Does it also have the highest flux? Calculate all three differences before opening the answer.

## The ER: retry, remove, raise capacity

The same moving-pool logic applies upstream: the ER can retry a folding client, remove it or raise capacity. Its lumen is a folding compartment for secreted and membrane proteins (see [protein sorting](/lessons/protein-sorting/)). A new chain enters through the translocon, a protein-conducting channel. BiP, an Hsp70-family chaperone, binds exposed hydrophobic stretches and helps it fold.

The film's picture was the simple case; here is the fuller one: the sugar tag that runs the retry loop. Many entering proteins receive an **N-linked glycan**, a branched sugar tree attached to asparagine with three glucose residues at its tip. Glucosidases remove two. When one remains, the **lectin chaperones** calnexin, which is membrane-bound, and calreticulin, which is soluble, bind that sugar and hold the client for another folding attempt. Glucosidase II removes the last glucose and releases the protein.

Now comes the test. UGGT recognizes features of a still-non-native client and adds one glucose back. That glucose is not a permanent label for a bad protein; it is a ticket for another round with calnexin or calreticulin. A client that has reached its native state is not reglucosylated and can leave for the Golgi.

**Predict.** A client is released from calnexin but still exposes non-native features. What happens to its glycan, and where does the client go next?

<details>
<summary>Check your prediction</summary>

UGGT adds one glucose back. The client can bind calnexin or calreticulin again and remains in the ER for another folding attempt. The glucose does not send it directly to the proteasome.

</details>

A client that cycles too long can be diverted to **ER-associated degradation**, or **ERAD**. In the standard account, trimming mannose residues from its glycan helps mark it. ERAD must then move a failed protein from the ER lumen to a proteasome in the cytosol.

Moving back across the ER membrane is called **retrotranslocation**. An ER-membrane E3 ligase, an enzyme that transfers ubiquitin, adds a ubiquitin chain; Hrd1 is one such ligase in yeast. The ATP-powered extraction machine Cdc48—p97 or VCP in mammals—pulls the client out and passes it to the proteasome. The exact channel and the limits of the yeast reconstitution are in Going deeper.

When failures arrive faster than the ER can handle them, the **unfolded protein response**, or **UPR**, changes capacity. It raises folding and disposal machinery and, through PERK in mammals, slows the incoming load of new proteins. If stress is not relieved, it can also drive cell death (see [cell death](/lessons/cell-death/)). Budding yeast has one branch: the sensor Ire1 cuts an intron from *HAC1* mRNA so the Hac1 transcription factor can be made. Mammals have three overlapping branches:

- **IRE1** removes a 26-nucleotide intron from *XBP1* mRNA, as shown for mouse XBP1, producing the active transcription factor XBP1s.
- **PERK** phosphorylates eIF2α on serine 51, slowing most translation while permitting preferential translation of ATF4 (see [stress signals](/lessons/stress-signals/)).
- **ATF6** travels to the Golgi, where Site-1 and Site-2 proteases cut it to release a transcription factor.

## Selective autophagy: choosing a mitochondrion

The film treated a damaged mitochondrion as cargo ready to wrap; selective autophagy explains how that cargo is chosen. In **selective autophagy**, receptors link chosen cargo to the LC3 family. **Mitophagy** is selective autophagy of mitochondria.

One well-tested route begins when a mitochondrion loses its membrane potential, the voltage across its inner membrane. In cultured cells, the kinase PINK1 is broken down on healthy, polarized mitochondria but builds up on depolarized ones and recruits Parkin, a ubiquitin ligase. Parkin adds ubiquitin to outer-membrane proteins, and ubiquitin-binding receptors recruit the autophagy machinery that engulfs the mitochondrion.

### Design problem

The film's question of arrival versus clearance also helps distinguish a broken disposal route from a broken stress alarm.

A mutant yeast strain accumulates a misfolded ER membrane protein. Is ERAD blocked, or is the UPR broken? Design experiments that tell the two apart, and write down what each model predicts before you run them.

**Predict.** The substrate is stable in a cycloheximide chase, but tunicamycin, which blocks N-glycosylation, still triggers normal *HAC1* mRNA splicing, a yeast UPR readout. Which model is favoured, and why?

<details>
<summary>Check your prediction</summary>

An ERAD block is favoured. The stable substrate shows slow destruction, while normal *HAC1* splicing shows that the Ire1 step of the UPR can still signal. The pair of results is stronger than either alone.

</details>

<details>
<summary>One way to do it</summary>

An ERAD defect predicts normal synthesis, slow destruction and an intact UPR that may already be active. A UPR defect cannot raise folding and disposal capacity, so buildup may emerge mainly under stress while the alarm stays silent.

1. **Cycloheximide chase.** Stop synthesis and blot for the substrate at 0, 30, 60 and 90 minutes. A stable band in unstressed cells points to an ERAD block. Near-normal decay that slows only under stress points to the UPR. The chase alone cannot settle it: yeast need an intact UPR for efficient ERAD, so a UPR mutant can also degrade the substrate slowly. Step 3 decides.
2. **Ubiquitination.** Immunoprecipitate the substrate and blot for ubiquitin. No ubiquitin suggests a defect at or before tagging, such as loss of the E3 (Hrd1); ubiquitinated substrate stuck in membranes suggests a later step, such as extraction by Cdc48.
3. **UPR reporter.** Treat with tunicamycin and measure *HAC1* mRNA splicing by RT-PCR, or use a reporter that produces a measurable signal when a UPR regulatory element turns on. No splicing means the UPR is broken; normal splicing means at least the Ire1 step works.
4. **Complementation and rescue.** Transform the mutant with plasmids carrying candidate genes such as *HRD1* or *IRE1*. The gene that restores normal degradation (or normal splicing) is the lead candidate. Rescue alone is not proof, because extra copies of one gene can suppress a defect in another: overexpressed *APG1* rescues an *apg13* mutant. Sequencing the mutant allele, or remaking the mutation in a clean strain, shows that this mutation, not a second hit elsewhere, causes the phenotype.

If the chase, the ubiquitin blot and the rescue all point to *HRD1*, and splicing is normal, ERAD is blocked. If splicing fails and *IRE1* rescues, the UPR is broken.

</details>

## Going deeper

The film's route map and the main text use deliberately simple cases. These refinements show where that picture is client-dependent, where routes overlap and where an assay can mislead.

### UPR discovery and branch overlap

Jeffery Cox, Caroline Shamu and Peter Walter reported budding yeast Ire1 in 1993, in parallel with Kazutoshi Mori and colleagues. Ire1 spans the ER membrane and carries a kinase and an RNase on its cytosolic side; its cut in *HAC1* mRNA occurs outside the spliceosome.

Mammalian branches overlap but can be separated experimentally. In mouse fibroblasts lacking IRE1α, ATF6 was still cleaved, and tunicamycin, which blocks N-glycosylation, still induced BiP mRNA. One broken branch does not silence the response.

### The glycan cycle is client-dependent

The calnexin and calreticulin cycle has been followed in detail for particular clients. In HEK293-derived cells it was followed for the IGF-1 receptor, and the two human UGGT enzymes preferred different substrates. The retry loop is therefore a useful core model, not a claim that every client or UGGT behaves identically.

### Route overlap and flux limits

The proteasome cannot admit an intact organelle or large aggregate through its narrow pore; an individual substrate must be engaged and unfolded. Lysosomal routes can accept bulk cytoplasm and organelles, but cargo is not assigned by size alone, and a single protein does not automatically imply proteasomal degradation.

A lysosomal inhibitor can itself change carrier formation, for example by altering lysosomal mTORC1 signalling. That would break the worked problem's assumption that arrival remains unchanged during the block.

### The Hrd1 channel: reconstitution and structure

In 2016 Ryan Baldridge and Tom Rapoport put purified yeast Hrd1 and a membrane-anchored misfolded substrate into synthetic lipid vesicles. Hrd1 ubiquitinating itself triggered movement of the substrate across the membrane, and the results suggest that Hrd1 forms a ubiquitin-gated channel, with Cdc48 completing extraction. That is a reconstitution with yeast proteins. In 2020 a cryo-EM structure of the full yeast Hrd1 complex, from the same laboratory, refined the picture for luminal substrates: Hrd1 and its partner Der1 each form a half-channel, and the two face each other in a thinned patch of membrane. Whether every ERAD substrate in every cell crosses this way is not settled.

### ER-phagy receptors

ER-phagy, the selective digestion of parts of the ER, uses several receptors. FAM134B, reported in 2015, was the first found in mammals. Another receptor, CCPG1, binds mammalian ATG8-family proteins and the autophagy protein FIP200 through distinct motifs. Mice with reduced *Ccpg1* accumulate insoluble protein in the ER of pancreatic acinar cells.

### PINK1 and Parkin: amplification and limits

PINK1 phosphorylates ubiquitin, which activates Parkin so that more ubiquitin accumulates on a damaged mitochondrial surface. Receptors such as optineurin and NDP52 bind the tagged mitochondrion and recruit autophagy machinery.

Derek Narendra, Atsushi Tanaka, Der-Fen Suen and Richard Youle reported Parkin movement to depolarized mitochondria in cultured cells in 2008. In 2010 the same laboratory reported that PINK1 was necessary and sufficient for Parkin recruitment in the cells tested. These experiments used uncouplers such as CCCP, which depolarize every mitochondrion at once, and largely overexpressed Parkin. Yet in reporter mice lacking *Pink1*, basal mitophagy in the sampled tissues was mostly preserved, with pancreatic islets the exception.

### Counting mitochondria after Parkin

In HeLa cells exposed to CCCP for 48 hours, electron micrographs showed 0.62 mitochondrial profiles per µm² of cytoplasm without YFP-Parkin and 0.074 with it.

> reduction = (0.62 − 0.074) / 0.62 = 0.546 / 0.62 = 0.881, so 88.1% lower

That compares mean two-dimensional densities in two groups of cells at one time. It is not a rate.
