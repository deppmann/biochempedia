techniques:
  - name: "Dichotomous key"
    blurb: >-
      A key gives two choices at each step, such as "nucleus present" or "nucleus absent", and
      each answer leads to the next step. It can only sort the organisms and traits that were
      built into it.
  - name: "Ribosomal RNA sequence comparison"
    blurb: >-
      Scientists read the order of bases in ribosomal RNA, or in the gene that codes for it, from
      different organisms and compare them. Organisms with more similar sequences are judged more
      closely related, which is how Archaea were told apart from Bacteria.
  - name: "Taxonomy database lookup"
    blurb: >-
      Databases such as NCBI Taxonomy and ITIS store each organism's accepted scientific name and
      the larger groups it belongs to. A record gives the classification, but it does not show
      where any one specimen was collected.

## Explain: three domains of life

<WordBank terms={['domain', 'prokaryote', 'eukaryote', 'kingdom', 'multicellular', 'autotroph', 'heterotroph', 'decomposer', 'ingester', 'dichotomous key', 'binomial nomenclature', 'genus']} />

The largest group in classification is the **domain**, a rank above kingdom. There are three: Bacteria, Archaea and Eukarya.

The first question is about the nucleus. A **prokaryote** is an organism whose cells have no nucleus; its DNA sits in the cytoplasm. Most prokaryotes are single cells, though some grow as chains or filaments. A **eukaryote** is an organism whose cells have a nucleus, a compartment wrapped in membrane that holds the DNA. You met it in [that lesson](/lessons/organelles-as-a-system/). Bacteria and Archaea are prokaryotes. Eukarya are the eukaryotes.

Bacteria and archaea look alike under a light microscope, but they differ in three ways:

- **Nucleic acid sequences.** Their ribosomal RNA, a molecule every cell uses to build proteins, has a different sequence. In the 1970s, comparing it showed that methane-making microbes stood apart from typical bacteria. You compared sequences to build trees in [that lesson](/lessons/building-a-tree-of-life/).
- **Cell membrane.** In typical archaea, each fat-like molecule of the membrane has its tails attached by a different kind of chemical link, called an ether link, instead of the ester link in bacteria and eukaryotes.
- **Cell wall.** Most bacteria have a wall containing peptidoglycan, a mesh of sugars and short protein-like chains. Archaea walls have no peptidoglycan.

| Trait | Bacteria | Archaea | Eukarya |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nucleus | No | No | Yes |
| Ribosomal RNA sequence | Bacterial pattern | Archaeal pattern | Eukaryotic pattern |
| Membrane lipid links (typical) | Ester links | Ether links | Ester links |
| Cell wall | Most have one, with peptidoglycan | Many have one, never peptidoglycan | Plants and fungi have walls; animals do not |

Think of the domains as three branches of one tree, not three steps on a staircase. The branches are not equally related. Archaea and Eukarya share much of the machinery that copies and reads DNA, and they are more closely related to each other than to Bacteria. Many genome studies now place eukaryotes inside the archaea, so three domains is a simplified picture. The analogy also breaks because, unlike a real tree, this one cannot be seen directly; it is worked out from sequence comparisons and still tested.

<Support min={2}>**Key idea:** The three domains are separated by whether cells have a nucleus and, for the two prokaryote domains, by their RNA sequences, membrane lipids and cell walls.</Support>

## Explain: six kingdoms

A **kingdom** is the rank just below domain. The Virginia Biology standards use six. A **multicellular** organism is made of many cells. An **autotroph** makes its own food from carbon dioxide, usually using light. A **heterotroph** gets food from other living things or their remains. A **decomposer** is a heterotroph that breaks down dead material and absorbs the nutrients. An **ingester** is a heterotroph that takes food inside its body to digest it.

| Kingdom | Domain | Nucleus | Cells | Cell wall | Tissues | Food |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Archaebacteria | Archaea | No | Mostly one | Usually; no peptidoglycan | No | Autotroph or heterotroph |
| Eubacteria | Bacteria | No | Mostly one; some form filaments | Usually; peptidoglycan | No | Autotroph or heterotroph |
| Protists | Eukarya | Yes | Mostly one | Some do, some do not | Mostly no; some seaweeds, such as kelp, have several cell types | Autotroph, heterotroph, or both |
| Fungi | Eukarya | Yes | Most many; yeasts one | Yes; chitin | No | Heterotroph; absorbs food (many are decomposers) |
| Plants | Eukarya | Yes | Many | Yes; cellulose | Yes | Autotroph (most plants) |
| Animals | Eukarya | Yes | Many | None | Yes (sponges are a special case) | Heterotroph; ingester |

The protist kingdom holds every eukaryote that is not a plant, animal or fungus. Many biologists no longer treat protists as one natural group, which you will see in [that lesson](/lessons/classification-changes/).

This course uses four plant divisions, a simplified list. Botanists often say phylum instead of division.

- **Mosses** have no vascular tissue (tubes that carry water) and no seeds. They spread by spores.
- **Ferns** have vascular tissue but no seeds. They also spread by spores.
- **Conifers** have seeds in cones, as the loblolly pine does.
- **Flowering plants** have flowers and seeds inside fruits, as Virginia’s state tree, the flowering dogwood, does.

## Explain: keys and names

Below kingdom come smaller ranks. The main ranks are domain, kingdom, phylum, class, order, family, genus and species; scientists sometimes add ranks in between. The blue crab of the Chesapeake Bay belongs to domain Eukarya, kingdom Animalia, phylum Arthropoda, class Malacostraca, order Decapoda, family Portunidae and genus *Callinectes*.

**Binomial nomenclature** is the system of giving each species a two-part scientific name. The first part is the **genus**, a group of closely related species, written with a capital letter. The second part, the specific epithet, is written in lower case. Together the two words name the **species**. Both words are printed in italics: *Callinectes sapidus*.

A **dichotomous key** is a set of paired choices that leads you, step by step, to a name or a group. Here is a short one for this lesson:

1. Nucleus present: go to 3. Nucleus absent: go to 2.
2. Sequence or wall chemistry like Archaea: archaebacteria. Like Bacteria: eubacteria. No such evidence: stop; looks cannot decide.
3. Many cells, cellulose walls, makes food by photosynthesis, and its embryo grows inside the parent: plants. Seaweeds and pond algae fail this test. Otherwise: go to 4.
4. Chitin walls, digests food outside its cells and absorbs it: fungi. Otherwise: go to 5.
5. Many cells, no walls, ingests food: animals. Any other eukaryote, single-celled or a many-celled alga such as kelp: protists.

Among eukaryotes, chitin walls and decomposing mark the mushroom as a fungus, while no walls and ingesting mark the crab as an animal. Limit: the traits are textbook descriptions, not observations of these specimens."

### Design problem

Yeast is a one-celled fungus. A classmate says yeast is a tiny plant. Plan a fair test of whether yeast needs light or needs sugar to stay active. Name your variables, constants, control, trials and safety steps.

<details>
<summary>One way to do it</summary>

- **Independent variables:** sugar (sugar water or plain water) and light (a bright window or a dark cupboard), giving four groups.
- **Dependent variable:** the height of foam, or the size of a balloon over each bottle, after 30 minutes. Foam and gas show that yeast is breaking down food.
- **Constants:** amount of yeast, water volume and temperature, amount of sugar in the sugar-water groups, bottle type and timing.
- **Control:** plain water in the dark, where neither light nor food is supplied.
- **Trials:** three or more bottles per group; report the mean.
- **What results mean:** activity only with sugar points to a heterotroph. Activity only with light would point to an autotroph.
- **Limit:** one kind of yeast, one temperature.
- **Safety:** use baker’s yeast, do not taste anything, do not seal bottles tightly, and wash your hands afterward.

</details>

## Evaluate: check your thinking

To sort the pond drop, ask first whether each cell has a nucleus. Algae, amoebas and tiny animals do, so they are Eukarya; cell number, walls and food then separate protists from animals. The specks with no nucleus are prokaryotes, but only their RNA sequences or wall and membrane chemistry can tell you whether they are Bacteria or Archaea.

**Science practices you used:** using a classification key and naming its limits, organizing and reading a table of characteristics, comparing evidence from a microscope with evidence from DNA, and planning a fair test with a control and repeated trials.
