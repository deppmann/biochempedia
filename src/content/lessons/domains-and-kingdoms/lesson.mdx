    blurb: >-

      each answer leads to the next step. It can only sort the organisms and traits that were

  - name: "Ribosomal RNA sequence comparison"
    blurb: >-
      Scientists read the order of bases in ribosomal RNA, or in the gene that codes for it, from
      different organisms and compare them. Organisms with more similar sequences are judged more
      closely related, which is how Archaea were told apart from Bacteria.

## The first split: three domains

<WordBank terms={['domain', 'prokaryote', 'eukaryote', 'kingdom', 'multicellular', 'autotroph', 'heterotroph', 'decomposer', 'ingester', 'dichotomous key', 'binomial nomenclature', 'genus']} />

A **domain** is the largest classification rank. The first film split cells into **eukaryotes**, whose cells have a nucleus (domain Eukarya), and **prokaryotes**, whose cells have none (Bacteria and Archaea). Review the nucleus in [that lesson](/lessons/organelles-as-a-system/).

The table adds what the first film skipped: the membrane pattern is typical, not universal, and not every cell has a wall. To see how sequence evidence becomes a tree, return to [that lesson](/lessons/building-a-tree-of-life/).

| Trait | Bacteria | Archaea | Eukarya |
|---|---|---|---|
| Nucleus | No | No | Yes |
| Ribosomal RNA sequence | Bacterial pattern | Archaeal pattern | Eukaryotic pattern |
| Membrane lipid links (typical) | Ester links | Ether links | Ester links |
| Cell wall | Most have one, with peptidoglycan | Many have one, never peptidoglycan | Plants and fungi have walls; animals do not |

Peptidoglycan is a mesh of sugars and short protein-like chains. In a membrane lipid, the fatty tails are attached to the rest of the molecule by a chemical link: an ester link in bacteria and eukaryotes, an ether link in typical archaea.

**Predict.** Eukaryotic membrane lipids use ester links, like those of bacteria. Does that shared trait make Eukarya closer relatives of Bacteria?

<details>
<summary>Check your prediction</summary>

No. Sequence comparisons place Archaea and Eukarya closer to each other than either is to Bacteria. One shared trait can mislead.

</details>

## Finish the path: six kingdoms

The second film sorted Eukarya into four kingdoms. Virginia Biology adds two prokaryote kingdoms for six in all. A **multicellular** organism has many cells. An **autotroph** builds food from carbon dioxide, usually using light. A **heterotroph** gets food from other organisms or their remains. A **decomposer** breaks down dead material and absorbs nutrients. An **ingester** takes food inside to digest it.

| Kingdom | Domain | Nucleus | Cells | Cell wall | Tissues | Food |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Archaebacteria | Archaea | No | Mostly one | Usually; no peptidoglycan | No | Autotroph or heterotroph |
| Eubacteria | Bacteria | No | Mostly one; some form filaments | Usually; peptidoglycan | No | Autotroph or heterotroph |
| Protists | Eukarya | Yes | Mostly one | Some do, some do not | Mostly no; some seaweeds, such as kelp, have several cell types | Autotroph, heterotroph, or both |
| Fungi | Eukarya | Yes | Most many; yeasts one | Yes; chitin | No | Heterotroph; absorbs food (many are decomposers) |
| Plants | Eukarya | Yes | Many | Yes; cellulose | Yes | Autotroph (most plants) |
| Animals | Eukarya | Yes | Many | None | Yes (sponges are a special case) | Heterotroph; ingester |

**Predict.** A yeast is one cell with a nucleus and chitin walls, and it absorbs its food. Does being a single cell put it with the protists?

<details>
<summary>Check your prediction</summary>

No. The table places yeasts with the fungi: chitin walls and absorbing food outweigh cell number. That is why the key below asks the fungus question before it sends leftover eukaryotes to the protists.

</details>

In this six-kingdom scheme, protists are the eukaryotes outside plants, animals and fungi. Many biologists no longer treat protists as one natural group, which you will see in [that lesson](/lessons/classification-changes/).

Flowering plants also have flowers. In Virginia, the loblolly pine is a conifer, and the state tree, the flowering dogwood, is a flowering plant.

## Use the key and write the name

The lesson's decision path as a short key:

1. Nucleus present: go to 3. Nucleus absent: go to 2.
2. Sequence or wall chemistry like Archaea: archaebacteria. Like Bacteria: eubacteria. No such evidence: stop; looks cannot decide.
3. Many cells, cellulose walls, makes food by photosynthesis, and its embryo grows inside the parent: plants. Seaweeds and pond algae fail this test. Otherwise: go to 4.
4. Chitin walls, digests food outside its cells and absorbs it: fungi. Otherwise: go to 5.
5. Many cells, no walls, ingests food: animals. Any other eukaryote, single-celled or a many-celled alga such as kelp: protists.

The second film listed the main ranks; scientists sometimes add ranks in between. The blue crab of the Chesapeake Bay belongs to domain Eukarya, kingdom Animalia, phylum Arthropoda, class Malacostraca, order Decapoda, family Portunidae and genus *Callinectes*.

The second film gave the naming rule for **binomial nomenclature**, the two-part scientific name of each **species**. The first word is the **genus**, a group of closely related species. In print: *Callinectes sapidus*.

### Design problem

Yeast is a one-celled fungus. A classmate says yeast is a tiny plant. Plan a fair test of whether yeast needs light or sugar to stay active. Name your variables, constants, control, trials and safety steps.

**Predict.** If yeast is a heterotroph, should its activity follow sugar or light?

<details>
<summary>One way to do it</summary>

- **Independent variables:** sugar (sugar water or plain water) and light (a bright window or a dark cupboard), giving four groups.
- **Dependent variable:** the height of foam, or the size of a balloon over each bottle, after 30 minutes. Foam and gas show that yeast is breaking down food.
- **Constants:** amount of yeast, water volume and temperature, amount of sugar in the sugar-water groups, bottle type and timing.
- **Control:** plain water in the dark, where neither light nor food is supplied.
- **Trials:** three or more bottles per group; report the mean.
- **What results mean:** activity only with sugar points to a heterotroph. Activity only with light would point to an autotroph.
- **Limit:** one kind of yeast, one temperature.
- **Safety:** use baker’s yeast, do not taste anything, do not seal bottles tightly, and wash your hands afterward.

</details>

## Going deeper

### Archaea in ordinary seawater

Archaea were first studied in harsh places such as hot springs and oxygen-free mud. In 1992 Edward DeLong searched North American coastal water for archaeal ribosomal RNA genes without growing the microbes. He found Archaea in oxygen-rich surface water, where archaeal RNA made up as much as 2% of the extracted ribosomal RNA. A later year-long survey near Hawaii found one group reaching 39% of the tiny cells detected in deep water below the sunlit layer. These numbers hold only for those samples but show that Archaea can be common outside extreme places.

### Why the course tree is a model

In the 1970s, Woese and Fox used ribosomal RNA to show that methane-making microbes stood apart from typical bacteria despite both lacking a nucleus. Woese, Kandler and Wheelis later proposed the domains Bacteria, Archaea and Eucarya; this lesson uses Eukarya. The three branches are not equally related: Archaea and Eukarya share much of the machinery that copies and reads DNA. Many genome studies now place eukaryotes inside Archaea, so the course tree is a model, not the last word.
