techniques:
  - name: "Reconstitution in lipid vesicles"
    blurb: >-
      Purify each protein, then rebuild the pathway from its parts: the receptor is inserted into
      phospholipid vesicles, and the G protein, the kinase and any candidate protein are added one
      at a time. If an effect appears only when a given part is present, that part is required.
      Here the readout was the receptor's ability to activate Gs.
  - name: "Activity-guided purification"
    blurb: >-
      Pass a tissue extract through a series of columns, and after each one measure how much
      protein is left and how much activity. Fold purification is activity per milligram compared
      with the starting extract. When one activity survives and another is lost as purity rises,
      the columns have separated two components.
  - name: "A mutant cell line as a negative control"
    blurb: >-
      The kin⁻ line of S49 lymphoma cells has no working protein kinase A. If an event still
      happens in an extract of these cells, protein kinase A cannot be required for it. A genetic
      control of this kind tests one suspect without relying on a drug, which may block its target only partly or hit other kinases too.
  - name: "Low-stringency library screening"
    blurb: >-
      Probe a library of cloned DNA with a labeled piece of a known gene, under conditions that
      tolerate mismatches. Clones that bind the probe imperfectly are candidate relatives of the known
      protein. Beta-arrestin was found this way, with retinal arrestin as the probe.

      - "0.875 µM"
      - "0.5 µM"
      - "0.125 µM"
      - "2 µM"
    answer: 3
    rationale: >-
      At steady state, synthesis equals breakdown, so the level is the rate of synthesis divided
      by the rate constant. Before the drug that is 2 divided by 4, or 0.5 µM. With 75% of the
      enzyme inhibited the rate constant is 1 per second, and the level is 2 divided by 1, or

      nothing. The value 0.125 µM comes from multiplying the starting level by 0.25, as if the
      drug cut synthesis. The value 0.875 µM comes from adding 75% to the starting level, which
      confuses percent inhibition with percent rise.

**Adrenaline is still bound to its receptor, yet within minutes the response fades. What removed the signal while its source remained?**

A response that fades while the stimulus continues is **desensitization**. The first film gave the answer at the receptor, a kinase mark read by arrestin; here is the evidence it skipped, and what happens below the receptor.

> **The one idea.** During a GPCR response, each active layer fades when removal outruns production.
>
> *Scope:* the rule covers the active forms of a G-protein-coupled receptor response: signaling receptors, GTP-bound G proteins, cyclic nucleotides and phosphorylated targets. Desensitization works from the production side: arrestin uncoupling or a loss of IP3 receptors cuts the active material made per unit of stimulus, so removal wins at an unchanged stimulus. A pathway need not use every removal process.

You met the “on” pathway in biochemistry: adrenaline, receptor, G protein, adenylyl cyclase, cyclic AMP, protein kinase A, phosphorylated targets. From the outside in, six removal steps can end it: the ligand leaves; the receptor loses access to its G protein; the receptor is taken inside in a vesicle; G-alpha hydrolyzes its GTP; a phosphodiesterase destroys cyclic AMP; and phosphatases reset the targets.

## The experiment: the mark is necessary but not sufficient

Robert Lefkowitz's laboratory at Duke University knew that phosphate appeared on the beta-adrenergic receptor as its response faded. Protein kinase A, activated by cyclic AMP, was the obvious suspect.

Jeffrey Benovic tested it with the kin⁻ line of S49 mouse lymphoma cells, which has no working protein kinase A. He mixed an extract with hamster lung receptor rebuilt into lipid vesicles and radioactive ATP.

The first film showed the purified kinase marking the receptor without silencing it; the missing reader came from vision. Hermann Kühn, at Jülich in West Germany, had described an abundant retinal protein, then called the 48K protein, that works with rhodopsin kinase to shut down rhodopsin, the rod cell's light receptor. It was later named **arrestin** because it arrested rhodopsin's function. The writer, a **G-protein-coupled receptor kinase** (**GRK**), preferentially marks the active receptor.

The retinal protein was a stand-in. Three years later Martin Lohse, a fellow in the same laboratory, found the non-retinal version, now called beta-arrestin. It reduced signaling by the kinase-marked receptor by more than 75% and did not act on rhodopsin. Arrestin can also bind only the marked receptor tail, leaving the core accessible to a G protein; that refinement matters when receptors signal inside the cell.

## Arrestin carries the receptor into the cell

Clathrin cages a patch of surface membrane and pulls it inward as a coated pit. In 1996 Benovic's laboratory, then at Thomas Jefferson University in Philadelphia, reported with James Keen and Vsevolod Gurevich that beta-arrestin 1 and beta-arrestin 2 bind clathrin directly and tightly. In intact cells, agonist brought the beta-2 adrenergic receptor, beta-arrestin and clathrin together.

**Predict.** A hybrid arrestin still binds the marked receptor but cannot bind clathrin. What happens first to G-protein coupling, and what happens to receptor uptake?

<details>
<summary>Check your prediction</summary>

It should still uncouple the receptor if it covers the core, but carry the receptor into the cell less well. In the 1996 study, hybrids lacking receptor binding or clathrin binding supported less uptake, so uptake needs both contacts: the protein that blocks the G protein's site also pulls the receptor into a coated pit.

</details>

<LessonFigure src="/figures/turning-signals-off/arrestin-to-coated-pit.webp" width={1200} height={611} alt="AI-generated illustration: three frames of a cell membrane in which a receptor carrying a phosphate mark binds a gold arrestin that blocks the G protein, then a clathrin cage gathers over a pit, then a coated vesicle pinches inside the cell." caption="Core-bound arrestin can block G-protein coupling and recruit clathrin, but tail-bound or internalized receptors need not be silent." />

GPCRs can be unfaithful to trimeric G proteins. Once arrestin replaces the G protein, the receptor's new partner has contacts of its own: besides clathrin and the adaptor AP-2, it can bind Src and parts of the ERK and JNK kinase cascades. How much signaling this supports without a G protein remains debated.

An **endosome** receives incoming vesicles and was long assumed to take receptors out of service. In 2013 Roshanak Irannejad, in Marc von Zastrow's laboratory, found active receptor and G protein at the early endosome membrane, which a later lesson takes up.

The receptor rule, then: an occupied receptor loses its G protein only when a dedicated protein acts on it, and where it sits does not tell you whether it is still signaling. Two findings would break the rule: an occupied receptor that uncouples with nothing acting on it (no mark, reader or other bound protein), or a receptor that stops signaling every time it leaves the surface, in every cell tested.

So measure the active forms themselves; the second film follows them below the receptor.

## Three downstream removal processes

Which G proteins count as “compatible” for the second film's RGS protein? **Regulators of G-protein signaling**, or **RGS proteins**, from the lesson on GTPase switches, are selective. RGS4 and GAIP accelerated GTP hydrolysis by Gi alpha-1 at least 40-fold and acted on the other Gi-family subunits tested. They did not act on Gs, the alpha subunit in the beta-adrenergic example. A 1997 study from the same group showed that RGS4 and GAIP also act on Gq alpha, so making more of a compatible RGS protein can shorten a response.

**Predict.** In otherwise identical tubes, add RGS4 to active Gi alpha-1 or active Gs alpha. Which GTP-bound pool should disappear faster?

<details>
<summary>Check your prediction</summary>

The Gi alpha-1 pool. “RGS protein” names a family, not a guarantee that every member acts on every G-alpha subunit.

</details>

A phosphodiesterase turns cyclic AMP into AMP, which does not activate protein kinase A. To quantify the pool, assume synthesis is constant and breakdown is first-order, meaning proportional to the amount present. At steady state synthesis equals breakdown, so:

> **messenger level at steady state = rate of synthesis ÷ rate constant of breakdown**

**Predict.** If synthesis doubles while the breakdown rate constant stays fixed, what happens to the steady messenger level?

<details>
<summary>Check your prediction</summary>

It doubles. Slowing breakdown at fixed synthesis also raises the level, and the messenger lingers.

</details>

Two drugs show the second film's limit in practice. Caffeine inhibits phosphodiesterases in a test tube, but at the concentrations a few cups produce, most of its effect is thought to come from blocking adenosine receptors. Sildenafil, the cleaner example, inhibits phosphodiesterase 5, which breaks down cyclic GMP, a close relative of cyclic AMP. Nitric oxide starts cyclic GMP synthesis in the smooth muscle of some blood vessels, and the muscle relaxes; sildenafil makes that existing signal larger and longer.

At the last layer, a **protein phosphatase** removes the phosphate protein kinase A added, as in the lesson on the phosphorylation switch. At each layer, ask whether active material is being made faster or removed faster.

## Constant GnRH shuts the pituitary down

**Gonadotropin-releasing hormone**, or **GnRH**, is a hypothalamic peptide that tells the pituitary to release gonadotropins, the hormones that drive the ovaries and testes. In 1978 Belchetz and colleagues studied rhesus monkeys whose hypothalamic lesions had abolished gonadotropin release. GnRH given once an hour restored sustained secretion; a constant infusion did not. Clinics use sustained GnRH agonists to suppress gonadotropin output.

The second film's adaptive brake, which reverses in the gaps, is a general possibility, not a location. The mammalian type I GnRH receptor lacks a C-terminal tail, resists agonist-dependent phosphorylation, and resists rapid desensitization. A GRK mark and arrestin therefore cannot be the whole explanation.

**Predict.** If adding a suitable C-terminal tail restores phosphorylation sites, what should happen to rapid agonist-dependent desensitization?

<details>
<summary>Check your prediction</summary>

It should increase. Tail-addition experiments support this, tying the missing tail to resistance against the rapid route, but they do not show that the tail explains long-term pituitary shutdown.

</details>

To locate the longer-term block, count surface receptors, measure second messenger made per dose, and bypass the receptor. A receptor-level failure lowers receptor number or messenger output; a downstream failure can leave both relatively intact while secretion falls. A bypass stimulus then places the defect relative to where the bypass enters: rescue implicates a bypassed step, and continued failure puts the block farther downstream. In gonadotrope cell lines, evidence points mainly downstream: sustained GnRH causes a fast loss of the IP3 receptors that release calcium, so the calcium response fades. This is the best-studied change; how much it explains in a patient receiving a GnRH agonist is less settled.

### Design problem

Design a scheme to down-regulate a receptor using PKC and PKA. Say which signals each kinase reports, why this differs from the kinase that marks mainly the occupied receptor, and what the cell gains by having both.

<details>
<summary>One way to do it</summary>

Protein kinase A, switched on by cyclic AMP, reports Gs-coupled receptor activity somewhere in the cell. Conventional and novel protein kinase C forms, switched on by diacylglycerol (conventional forms also need calcium), report phospholipase C branch activity. Neither kinase knows which receptor produced the messenger.

Give the receptor cytoplasmic serines matching both kinases' target sequences; when either messenger rises, its kinase phosphorylates them and weakens coupling. That is negative feedback, the motif from the lesson on signaling circuits.

A GRK reads receptor shape, so it marks mainly agonist-occupied receptors. Kinases A and C read messengers, so they can mark any receptor carrying their sites, occupied or empty, including receptors for a different hormone. The first is **homologous desensitization**; the second is **heterologous desensitization**.

The two controls differ in reach: messenger-reading kinases act cell-wide when messenger is high, whereas a GRK and arrestin silence mainly the receptors being hit, leaving the cell able to answer a different hormone.

</details>

## Going deeper

### The reconstitution numbers

Benovic purified the kinase from cow brain cortex 20,000-fold. A batch purified only 350 times had cut the receptor's ability to activate its G protein by up to 80%, and the pure enzyme cut it by only 10 to 15%. With the pure kinase alone, the receptors carried an average of about 8 phosphates each and could still switch on Gs. With kinase and arrestin together, activity fell to about 59% of the untreated receptor's, a loss of about 40%. Arrestin added to a receptor the kinase had never marked left activity at 98.1% of the value without arrestin, across 13 measurements. Neither bovine serum albumin, an unrelated protein, nor heat-denatured arrestin could substitute for arrestin.

### Where GAIP sits

GAIP is held on a membrane by palmitate, a fatty acid bonded to the protein. In 1998 Marilyn Farquhar's laboratory reported it on clathrin-coated vesicles, some budding from the surface and some from the Golgi: the first GTPase-activating protein found on such vesicles.

### The GnRH receptor has no tail

The finding that the missing C-terminal tail makes the GnRH receptor resist agonist-dependent phosphorylation and rapid desensitization comes from Willars and colleagues in 1999. The receptor works mainly through Gq and phospholipase C, so cyclic AMP is not the main messenger here.
