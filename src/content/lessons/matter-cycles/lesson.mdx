## Explain: carbon moves, and it sets the temperature

<WordBank terms={['photosynthesis', 'respiration', 'combustion', 'decomposition', 'ocean absorption', 'transpiration', 'nitrogen fixation', 'nitrification', 'assimilation', 'ammonification', 'denitrification', 'greenhouse effect']} />

Carbon is in every sugar, fat, protein and piece of DNA in your body. The first film showed four of these five processes; combustion is new.

- **Photosynthesis:** plants, algae and some bacteria use light energy to build sugar from carbon dioxide and water, pulling carbon out of the air or water.
- **Respiration** (cellular respiration): cells break down sugar with oxygen and release carbon dioxide. Plants, animals and microbes all do it.
- **Combustion:** burning wood, coal, oil or natural gas joins carbon with oxygen and releases carbon dioxide quickly.
- **Decomposition:** fungi and bacteria break down dead organisms and wastes. Their respiration returns much of the carbon to the air, and some stays in the soil for years.
- **Ocean absorption:** carbon dioxide dissolves into seawater at the surface (it can also come back out).

**Predict.** After dark, photosynthesis stops but a plant’s cells keep respiring. Does the plant move carbon dioxide into its surroundings or out of them?

<details>
<summary>Check your prediction</summary>

Into its surroundings. Cellular respiration continues without light, so the plant releases carbon dioxide.

</details>

> carbon dioxide + water + light energy → glucose + oxygen
> 6CO2 + 6H2O → C6H12O6 + 6O2

Overall, aerobic respiration has the reverse inputs and outputs, and it releases stored energy for cells. You met both processes in [Capturing Sunlight](/lessons/photosynthesis-energy-capture/) and [Respiration and ATP](/lessons/respiration-and-atp/).

Coal, oil and natural gas formed from ancient organisms; burning them adds carbon locked underground for millions of years to today’s air.

Carbon dioxide also helps set Earth’s temperature. Sunlight warms the ground, which gives off heat as infrared light. Carbon dioxide absorbs some of that light and gives off heat in all directions, including toward the surface. This warming by gases that absorb outgoing heat is the **greenhouse effect**. Water vapor and methane trap heat too. Without the greenhouse effect, Earth would be far colder. Adding carbon dioxide strengthens the effect.

## Explain: nitrogen needs microbial handoffs

Your body uses nitrogen to build proteins and DNA. Plants also need it for chlorophyll, the green pigment of photosynthesis. Air is about 78 percent nitrogen gas, but the two atoms in each molecule are held by a very strong bond. Most organisms cannot break it. The first film showed five handoffs; here is who does each one.

1. **Nitrogen fixation** converts nitrogen gas into ammonia. In nature, certain microbes, mostly bacteria, do most of it. Some live freely in soil; others live in nodules on roots of beans, peas and other legumes. Lightning fixes a small amount too.
2. **Nitrification** changes ammonia into nitrite and then nitrate. Soil bacteria and archaea do this work.
3. **Assimilation** is a plant or alga taking up nitrate or ammonium from the soil or water and building it into proteins and DNA.
4. **Ammonification** is decomposers turning the nitrogen in wastes and dead bodies back into ammonium.
5. **Denitrification** is bacteria turning nitrate back into nitrogen gas, usually in wet soil or mud with little oxygen. The gas returns to the air.

Animals get nitrogen by eating plants or other animals. A crab eats clams, worms, small animals and dead material, and much of that nitrogen traces back to algae and plants that took it up from the water.

**Predict.** Nitrogen also ties to carbon. A plant receives nitrate but no light for a long time. Is nitrate alone enough for the plant to keep building amino acids?

<details>
<summary>Check your prediction</summary>

No. The plant also needs carbon compounds made by photosynthesis. Nitrogen supply alone cannot build the carbon part of an amino acid.

</details>

<summary>One way to do it</summary>

- **Independent variable:** the amount of liquid plant fertilizer added: none, a small dose and a large dose.
- **Dependent variable:** how green the water becomes after 10 days, judged against a color chart or measured with a light sensor.
- **Constants:** the same pond water, jar size, water volume, light, temperature and number of days.
- **Control:** the jars with no fertilizer.
- **Repeated trials:** at least three jars at each dose, so one odd jar does not decide the result.
- **What results would mean:** if the jars get greener as the dose rises, the data support the idea that added nutrients feed algae.
- **Safety and ethics:** wear gloves, wash your hands, never drink the water, and pour it down a drain rather than into a stream, so you do not add nutrients to real water.

## Going deeper: routes and rates

- Ocean carbon exchange runs both ways, but today the ocean takes in more carbon dioxide than it gives off.
- Factories fix nitrogen to make fertilizer. People now add about as much new usable nitrogen each year as all natural fixation does.
- In many soils, archaea are the more common microbes carrying out the first step of nitrification.
