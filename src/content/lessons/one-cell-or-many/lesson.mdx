In the model you get five life jobs: take in food, move, sense the surroundings, reproduce and remove extra water. You can give every job to one cell, or build a group of cells and give each cell one or two jobs. Each cell has a limited energy supply, and you can make the organism bigger and see which arrangement keeps up.

## Explain: one cell that does it all

<WordBank terms={['unicellular', 'multicellular', 'cilia', 'contractile vacuole', 'binary fission', 'specialized cell', 'tissue', 'organ', 'organ system', 'gene expression']} />

The first film followed one Paramecium through its four jobs. Here are its terms. A **unicellular** organism is made of one cell, and a **multicellular** organism is made of many. **Cilia** are short hair-like structures that beat together like oars. Food such as bacteria is swept into a groove and digested in food vacuoles. Pond water has fewer dissolved particles than the cell, so water keeps moving in by osmosis (see [Crossing the Membrane](/lessons/crossing-the-membrane/)). A **contractile vacuole** is a sac that fills with this extra water and squeezes it out. **Binary fission** is reproducing without a partner: one cell divides into two.

## Explain: specialized cells, from tissues to systems

The first film built the levels of organization with a heart. In words: a **specialized cell** is a cell with a structure that suits it to a particular job; a **tissue** is a group of similar cells working together; an **organ** is several tissues working together; an **organ system** is a set of related organs.

## Explain: same DNA, different genes

As the second film showed, cells with the same DNA differ in **gene expression**: the use of a gene's instructions to make a product such as a protein. Many genes stay unused in each cell.

The film did not say where the shared copy comes from. It was copied from the first cell you began as. See how that copying works in [The Cell Cycle and Mitosis](/lessons/cell-cycle-and-mitosis/), and how genes are read in [From DNA to Protein](/lessons/protein-synthesis/). Exceptions besides the mature red blood cell are in Going deeper.

<Support min={2}>**Key idea:** Nearly all body cells with a nucleus carry essentially the same DNA, but each kind of cell uses a different set of genes, which gives it its own structure and job.</Support>

### Design problem

Do radish seedlings grown on drier paper towels grow longer root hairs than seedlings grown on wetter towels? Plan an investigation using a hand lens or a microscope with a scale. Name your variables, constants, control, sample size and safety steps.

<details>
<summary>One way to do it</summary>

- **Independent variable:** how wet the paper towel is. Add a set volume of water per towel, such as 5 mL for drier and 15 mL for wetter.
- **Dependent variable:** length of root hairs, measured on the same part of each root with a scale in view.
- **Constants:** seed batch, number of seeds per dish, temperature, light, towel type and the day you measure.
- **Control:** the dishes given the usual amount of water used for sprouting seeds.
- **Sample size and trials:** several dishes per group, ten or more seedlings per dish, and at least ten hairs per seedling. Report the mean and the spread.
- **Fairness:** a partner codes the dishes so the person measuring does not know the group.
- **Limit:** this tests one plant species at one age.
- **Safety:** handle slides and cover slips with care, wash hands after handling seeds, and do not eat them.

</details>

## Going deeper

### A bridge between one cell and many specialists

Some multicellular organisms have only a few kinds of cells. Volvox, a green alga, lives as a hollow ball of many cells. In Volvox carteri, about 1,000 to 2,000 small cells swim and make food but can no longer divide, while up to 16 large cells are set aside for reproduction. Even with just two kinds of cells, Volvox shows a clear division of labor.

### How many cells and cell types?

A 70-kilogram reference adult man holds about 30 to 36 trillion cells, according to two careful estimates (2016 and 2023); the 2023 model gave about 28 trillion for a reference woman and about 17 trillion for a 10-year-old child. Textbooks often say there are about 200 kinds of cells. A 2022 atlas that sorted cells by the genes they use counted more than 400 types in the tissues it sampled, so the true number depends on how finely you sort.

### Exceptions to the shared-DNA model

Some immune cells cut and rejoin pieces of their DNA as they mature, to build the genes for their antibodies or receptors. A maturing red blood cell pushes out its nucleus and also gets rid of its mitochondria, which hold a little DNA of their own, so a mature one has no DNA left. Egg and sperm cells carry one set of chromosomes, while most body cells carry two.
