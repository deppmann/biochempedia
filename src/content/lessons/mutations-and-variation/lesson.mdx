## Explain: same features, slightly different

<WordBank terms={['trait', 'population', 'variation', 'mutation', 'body cells', 'sex cells', 'random']} />

A **trait** is a feature of a living thing, such as colour, shape, body covering or height. A **population** is a group of the same kind of living thing in one area. All white oaks have leaves with rounded lobes. But each leaf, and each tree, differs a little. **Variation** is these small differences among members of a population.

Where does variation come from? Some comes from genes. Each child gets a new mix of the parents' alleles when sex cells form and join, as you saw in [Reproduction and Meiosis](/lessons/reproduction-and-meiosis/). Some variation comes from the environment.

Height shows both. Many genes add a little to height. Food and health during childhood matter too. So two children with very similar genes can grow to different heights. Many traits work this way: inheritance and the environment act together. You can sort traits into these kinds in [Inherited or Acquired?](/lessons/inherited-or-acquired/).

<Support min={2}>**Key idea:** Members of a population share the same basic features but vary a little in each trait, and many traits depend on both genes and the environment.</Support>

## Explain: what a mutation is and where it goes

A **mutation** is a change in the DNA sequence, the order of the bases. Recall from [DNA: The Code of Life](/lessons/dna-the-code-of-life/) that a gene carries the code for a protein. A mutation can change that protein. It can also change how much of it a cell makes. Sometimes the protein works the same as before.

Mutations can be caused by:
- mistakes when a cell copies its DNA before dividing
- radiation, such as ultraviolet light from the Sun
- some chemicals

Mutations are **random**. Here, random means they happen by chance, not because a living thing needs them. A mutation does not "know" whether it will help.

Where the mutation happens matters. **Body cells** are all the cells of the body except eggs and sperm, such as skin, muscle and bone cells. **Sex cells** are eggs and sperm. A mutation in a body cell stays in that person. A mutation in a sex cell can be passed to offspring.

<Support min={2}>**Key idea:** A mutation is a chance change in DNA, and only mutations in sex cells can be passed to offspring.</Support>

## Explain: helpful, harmful or neither

Does a mutation help? That depends on the living thing and where it lives. Here is a comparison: a mutation is like a misprint in a recipe. Most misprints change little, some ruin the dish, and a rare one makes it better. The comparison breaks here: a recipe has a writer, but no one chooses a mutation.

The variation that mutations create may or may not help an individual survive and reproduce. Individuals that happen to carry a helpful variation are more likely to survive and pass it on. You will see how this changes a population in [Natural Selection in Action](/lessons/natural-selection-in-action/). Over many generations, heredity keeps most traits steady. Mutations add the new variation that leads to the diversity of life.

<Support min={2}>**Key idea:** A mutation can help, harm or make no difference, and its effect depends on the environment.</Support>

### Design problem

Plan a study of how much white oak leaves vary. Do leaves from the sunny side of one tree differ in length from leaves on the shady side?

<details>
<summary>One way to do it</summary>

- **Question:** Are oak leaves from the sunny side of a tree longer or shorter than leaves from the shady side?
- **Independent variable:** the side of the tree, sunny or shady.
- **Dependent variable:** leaf length in centimetres, measured from the base of the blade to the tip.
- **Constants:** one tree, leaves collected on the same day, the same ruler and the same start and end points.
- **Control:** comparing the two sides of the same tree, so the tree's genes are the same.
- **Repeated trials:** pick 20 leaves from low branches on each side, then repeat with a second tree.
- **Safety:** pick leaves only from branches you can reach from the ground, wash hands afterwards, and never climb the tree.

If one side's leaves are clearly longer, the environment affects leaf length. The spread within each side shows variation even with the same genes.

</details>

## Evaluate: check your thinking

No two oak leaves or students are exactly alike, because genes and the environment both shape each trait. New genetic variation starts with mutations, which arise by chance. Some help, some harm and many make little difference, and only those in sex cells reach offspring.

**Science practices you used:** making predictions with a model, reading and comparing data in tables, calculating a difference, planning a fair test with constants and repeated trials, and naming the limits of data.
