    story: >-
      Rui Chang and colleagues in Stephen Liberles's laboratory asked whether the vagus nerve is a single channel or a bundle of separate lines. They put the light-gated channel channelrhodopsin-2 into mouse vagal neurons that expressed one of two receptor genes, P2ry1 or Npy2r, using Cre-dependent genetics. Then they exposed the vagus in mice under deep isoflurane anaesthesia and shone light on it. Activating the P2ry1-labelled afferents suppressed breathing. Activating the Npy2r-labelled afferents produced rapid shallow breathing. Light-evoked recordings from the nerve showed that the P2ry1 axons were largely fast A fibres and the Npy2r axons largely slow C fibres. In fixed adult mouse lungs, labelled P2ry1 terminals contacted pulmonary neuroepithelial bodies, which are clusters of secretory neuroendocrine cells within the airway epithelium. The work showed what each population can do when driven. It did not say what stimulus drives each one in life, and that question set the agenda for the studies that followed.

    story: >-
      Wei-Zheng Zeng and colleagues, in a paper whose last two authors are Stephen Liberles and Ardem Patapoutian, asked which mechanically gated channel lets arterial stretch drive the baroreflex, the reflex by which a rise in blood pressure slows the heart. They used a Phox2b-Cre mouse line to delete Piezo1, Piezo2 or both from nodose and petrosal sensory neurons. In anaesthetized mice they raised blood pressure with a drug and measured heart rate against pressure. Neither single deletion produced a detectable change against littermate controls. The double deletion abolished the reflex. In conscious freely moving mice fitted with telemetry, the double mutants had higher mean arterial pressure in the active period than controls. The result showed that the two channels are required collectively in the targeted population. It did not show that every neuron carries both. Several candidate baroreceptor channels had already been proposed, and what this paper added was a combined requirement for Piezo1 and Piezo2 in the targeted population.

techniques:
  - name: "Single-cell RNA sequencing of sensory ganglia"
    blurb: >-
      Neurons from acutely dissociated ganglia are sequenced one cell at a time and grouped by their expression. The groups are hypotheses about cell types; a function is assigned only when imaging, genetic access or behaviour ties a group to it.
  - name: "Cre-dependent optogenetics and ablation"
    blurb: >-
      A Cre driver restricts a light-gated channel, or a cell-removal tool, to a labelled population. Driving the cells tests sufficiency for the output measured; removing them tests necessity, in that assay, at that age and with that driver, since a Cre line marks cells that expressed the driver at some time.
  - name: "In vivo ganglion and brainstem calcium imaging"
    blurb: >-
      A fluorescent calcium indicator reports activity in identified neurons while a stimulus is delivered to an organ in an anaesthetized mouse. It maps who responds to what, and it cannot show perception or behaviour.
  - name: "Conditional channel deletion"
    blurb: >-
      A floxed allele, a gene copy marked for conditional deletion, is removed in a chosen population, here sensory neurons or nodose and petrosal neurons. Comparing the single and double deletions with littermate controls shows which channels a reflex needs in that population.

**The vagus carries a bundle of specialised sensory lines, not one cable: some report lung inflation and airway irritants, some report arterial stretch, and others report gut stretch and food, and activating labelled populations of each can trigger a reflex or change feeding.** The sharpest evidence is a pair of opposite results in anaesthetized mice. Light on one set of labelled vagal axons suppresses breathing, and light on another set makes the animal breathe fast and shallow, which a single cable reporting that the body is stretched could not do. This lesson asks what each population reports, which channel turns force into current, and where the signal lands first. It is mouse work unless a sentence says human, and it starts where [hypothalamus and autonomic control](/lessons/hypothalamus-autonomic/) and [sensory coding](/lessons/sensory-coding/) stop: at the peripheral afferent.

## One nerve, many labelled lines: genetic access to vagal afferents

Vagal sensory cell bodies sit in two ganglia at the base of the skull, the nodose and the jugular, and single-cell atlases of them sort the neurons into many types. A cluster is a hypothesis about function until genetic access, physiology or behaviour ties it to one, and the next paragraphs show how that tie is made. The ganglion origins and the atlas details are under Going deeper.

Chang and colleagues (Cell, 2015) used a Cre driver to put the light-gated channel channelrhodopsin-2 into P2ry1-labelled or Npy2r-labelled vagal neurons, exposed the vagus in mice under deep isoflurane, and shone light on it. P2ry1 activation suppressed breathing. Npy2r activation produced rapid shallow breathing. Light-evoked nerve recordings showed P2ry1 axons to be largely fast A fibres and Npy2r axons largely slow C fibres. This is sufficiency in anaesthetized mice: it says what the cells can do when driven, not what drives them in life.

Williams and colleagues (Cell, 2016) imaged labelled vagal neurons in mice with the gut still connected. GLP1R-labelled neurons responded to stomach and intestinal stretch. Bai and colleagues later found that in adult mice the Glp1r-Cre line labels gastric endings and not intestinal ones, and put the difference down to developmental Glp1r expression being broader than adult expression. That is the marker-versus-gene problem again. GPR65-labelled neurons innervated villi near the junction of stomach and small intestine and responded to food delivered into the intestinal lumen, and activating them suppressed gastric contractions. The authors suggested that most villous nutrient detection in their preparation was independent of GLP1R. GLP1R and GPR65 are labels. Neither paper shows that the receptor named is the molecule that senses stretch or food.

## Two ways to stop breathing: protection afferents and stretch afferents

Prescott and colleagues activated P2ry1-labelled afferents and recruited a coordinated airway defence: apnoea, closure of the vocal folds, swallowing and expiratory reflexes. In the Chang study, labelled P2ry1 terminals contacted pulmonary neuroepithelial bodies in fixed adult lungs, clusters of secretory neuroendocrine cells within the airway epithelium that themselves express Piezo2. Ablating the P2ry1 cells genetically impaired swallowing responses to water and acid at the larynx, which is a necessity test in that assay. One transcriptomic type, NP19, was a candidate for mediating these reflexes, and "candidate" is the right word.

A step before the nerve involves the epithelium. Loss of the ATP receptors P2X2 and P2X3 impaired some swallowing responses to laryngeal stimuli, which fits ATP passing from epithelium to afferent. The receptors deleted there were P2X2 and P2X3, not P2RY1.

The second way to stop breathing is by inflation. The Hering-Breuer inflation reflex is the interruption of further inspiration when the lungs fill. In Prescott's ganglion imaging, with central connections severed under isoflurane and the airway inflated in vivo, most stretch-responsive neurons were PIEZO2-positive and P2RY1-negative. So the protection neurons and the stretch neurons are different populations that both end in a halt to inspiration.

## What transduces force in the viscera, lung and artery

Nonomura and colleagues (Nature, 2017) deleted Piezo2 conditionally in sensory neurons of adult mice, which impaired the inflation reflex, and driving Piezo2-positive vagal neurons with light caused apnoea. Newborn lethality after earlier or broader Piezo2 deletion is a separate experiment, described under Going deeper. The adult result shows the channel is needed for the reflex. For the pore-level evidence, see [Piezo channels and the physics of force](/lessons/mechanotransduction-channels/).

Arteries add a twist. Zeng and colleagues (Science, 2018) used Phox2b-Cre to delete channels from nodose and petrosal neurons and raised blood pressure with a drug in anaesthetized mice. Only the Piezo1 and Piezo2 double knockout lost the baroreflex; neither single knockout showed a detectable change, and in conscious double knockouts active-period blood pressure was higher. The channels are required collectively in the targeted population. The result does not show that each neuron carries both. The numbers are under Going deeper.

## A map of the organs in the brainstem, sharpened by inhibition (2022)

Vagal sensory axons primarily target the nucleus of the solitary tract (NTS) in the medulla, and some gut afferents also reach the area postrema. Jugular afferents are the exception: they target the paratrigeminal nucleus, in the spinal trigeminal tract, so the crest-derived arm of the nerve has its own central target. Ran and colleagues (Nature, 2022) imaged the caudal NTS with a nuclear calcium indicator in anaesthetized mice and reported spatially ordered, organ-biased ensembles. Cutting the vagus below the diaphragm on both sides removed the gastric distension response, which confirmed the route. Blocking GABA-A receptors with bicuculline broadened tuning across organs, so that many cells answered both stomach and duodenum stretch, or both stomach stretch and laryngeal water. Within one organ, tuning was already broad: most larynx-responsive cells answered water, high salt and citric acid alike. The map is processed, not a set of fixed lines. The worked arithmetic on this data set is under Going deeper.

## From afferent to behaviour: stretch, satiety and what the trial evidence covers

Bai and colleagues (Cell, 2019) worked in awake mice at least six weeks old at surgery. Activating Oxtr-positive intestinal stretch afferents, which end in intraganglionic laminar endings between the muscle layers, strongly suppressed feeding. Activating Glp1r-positive afferents, whose endings Bai found in the stomach rather than the intestine, suppressed it more weakly, and activating Gpr65-positive afferents did not measurably change feeding in the assays tested. Loading the intestine with a non-nutritive hyperosmolar solution (mannitol) inhibited AgRP neuron activity and food intake, so nutrients were not required, though the load was not a pure volume change. Nutrients also reach the vagus by a chemical route these experiments did not test: enteroendocrine cells in the gut lining release cholecystokinin and other hormones that act on afferent terminals, and both of Bai's stretch populations express the cholecystokinin receptor Cckar. For central feeding circuits see [motivation and reward](/lessons/motivation-and-reward/). Evidence on ascending anatomy agrees across species: rat tracing and mouse tracing support parabrachial relays toward thalamus and insular cortex, and mouse insular activity tracked recovery of physiological state (Livneh and colleagues, Neuron, 2020). No study here proves one serial chain.

The human evidence is narrow. The VNS-REHAB trial (Dawson and colleagues, Lancet, 2021) tested implanted vagus-nerve stimulation paired with rehabilitation after ischaemic stroke and favoured stimulation over sham, with the effect size given under Going deeper. That covers one device and one pairing. It says nothing about "vagal tone", ear devices or any selected cell type.

### Design problem

Activating GLP1R-labelled afferents, which end mainly in the stomach in Bai's adult labelling, weakly suppresses mouse feeding, activating Oxtr-labelled intestinal stretch afferents strongly suppresses it, and Gpr65-afferent activation did not measurably change it. Design experiments that test whether volume or nutrient content ends a meal.

<details>
<summary>One way to do it</summary>

Dissociate the stimuli. Give an equal-volume, non-nutritive load in one group and an iso-volumetric nutrient load in another, to awake animals with intake measured directly. Use activation for sufficiency, and use generic reversible silencing or loss of the labelled population for necessity. Allow for an indirect nutrient route as well, since gut hormones such as cholecystokinin released from enteroendocrine cells act on vagal terminals, so a nutrient signal need not be sensed by the afferent itself. Add a route control, such as interrupting the subdiaphragmatic vagus. Read the outcomes in a fixed order. If the non-nutritive load cuts intake as much as the nutrient load, a nutrient-independent signal is sufficient in this assay. Crediting volume itself would need osmolality-matched loads and measured distension, because a hyperosmolar load pulls fluid into the gut. If silencing the stretch population raises intake of a large meal, that population is needed there. If neither moves intake in the way predicted, report the result and name which control would be run next. State at the end that mouse intake does not quantify a human meal.

</details>

## Going deeper

### Ganglia, atlases and the 18 versus 37 counts

The two ganglia have different origins: nodose neurons derive from epibranchial placodes (thickenings of embryonic head ectoderm), and jugular neurons from [neural crest](/lessons/neural-crest/). In the mouse atlas of Kupari and colleagues (Cell Reports, 2019), Phox2b marked the nodose population and Prdm12 the jugular one. Their preparation was acutely dissociated ganglia from mice about 4.5 to 5 weeks old, and they described the dissected tissue as merged jugular and nodose ganglia that could include neurons of the petrosal ganglion, which belongs to the glossopharyngeal nerve. A mouse "nodose" sample can therefore hold three kinds of cell.

That atlas came from Patrik Ernfors's laboratory. It classified 18 nodose types by single-cell RNA sequencing, and several functional assignments were proposed from expression signatures rather than shown for every type. A later atlas from Stephen Liberles's laboratory (Prescott and colleagues, Cell, 2020) reported 37 types from a combined vagal and glossopharyngeal sample of eight-week-old male mice. The two counts differ in scope and are not interchangeable.

Prescott and colleagues used mice aged 8 to 24 weeks for the P2ry1 activation work.

### Piezo2 in newborns and the baroreflex numbers

A separate experiment deleted Piezo2 in the whole mouse, or from development in neural crest-derived sensory neurons with Wnt1-Cre, which caused respiratory distress and death in newborns. Deleting it from nodose neurons alone with Phox2b-Cre did not: those pups survived with normal blood oxygen, so the neonatal deaths were not caused by loss in the airway-innervating nodose population. The authors propose Piezo2 as an airway stretch sensor at birth as well, but that phenotype is a different experiment from the adult reflex defect.

Control mice had a baroreflex sensitivity of -5.0 ± 0.5 bpm/mmHg (mean ± SEM), the change in heart rate per unit rise in pressure. Piezo1 and Piezo2 double knockouts had -0.6 ± 0.4 bpm/mmHg.

In conscious mice with telemetry, active-period mean arterial pressure was 112 ± 0.4 mmHg in double knockouts and 95 ± 0.5 mmHg in controls, a difference of 17 mmHg.

### The stroke trial in detail

The VNS-REHAB trial (Dawson and colleagues, Lancet, 2021) randomized 108 adults at least 9 months after an ischaemic stroke, with moderate-to-severe arm weakness, to implanted vagus-nerve stimulation or sham stimulation, each paired with rehabilitation.

The difference in Fugl-Meyer upper-extremity improvement, measured on the first day after six weeks of in-clinic therapy, was 2.6 points (95% confidence interval 1.0 to 4.2).

The trial also reported Wolf Motor Function Test scores and Fugl-Meyer scores 90 days later, both favouring stimulation.

### Worked problem: reading the NTS map

Ran and colleagues (Nature, 2022) imaged the caudal NTS with a nuclear calcium indicator in anaesthetized mice, recording about 2,800 neurons at once. Stimuli included gastric balloon volumes of 150, 300, 600 and 900 microlitres and laryngeal citric acid at 25 mM, pH 2.6.

In Ran's six-mouse experiment, 1,018 of 1,133 responsive neurons answered to only one tested organ.

> **single-organ fraction = 100 × (single-organ neurons) / (all responsive neurons)**

(a) 100 × 1,018 / 1,133 = 89.8%. (b) 1,133 − 1,018 = 115 neurons responded to more than one tested organ. (c) 100 × 115 / 1,133 = 10.2%. (d) The denominator is neurons responsive to this panel in an anaesthetized mouse, not all NTS neurons and not awake humans. The 1,133 cells come from six mice, so they are not 1,133 independent units.

(e) In a different experiment in the same paper, 42.2% of glucose-responsive cells also responded to duodenal distension, while 31.0% of distension-responsive cells also responded to glucose. Call the overlap I, the glucose population G and the stretch population S. Then I = 0.422 G = 0.310 S, so S / G = 0.422 / 0.310 = 1.36. The two percentages are consistent: the stretch-responsive population is about 36 percent larger, which gives the smaller conditional fraction. Do not merge this with the 1,133-cell data set. The published percentages are rounded, so 1.36 is approximate.
