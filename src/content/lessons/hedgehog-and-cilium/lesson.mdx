techniques:
  - name: "Saturation mutagenesis screen"
    blurb: >-
      Damage genes at random with a chemical mutagen, breed thousands of separate lines so that
      each mutation can be seen in two copies, and inspect every line for a chosen defect. When
      the same genes keep turning up, the list is close to complete, and it was made with no prior
      idea of what the genes encode.
  - name: "Complementation test"
    blurb: >-
      Cross two mutants that have similar defects. Normal offspring mean each parent supplied a
      working copy of the gene the other lacked, so the mutations lie in different genes. A repeat
      of the defect means they lie in the same gene. The test turns a pile of mutations into a
      count of genes.
  - name: "Epistasis analysis with double mutants"
    blurb: >-
      Combine two mutations that push a pathway in opposite directions and see which outcome wins.
      In a chain of switches like this one, the double mutant looks like the mutant in the gene
      that acts later, which gives the order of the parts. It placed Smoothened after Patched, and
      the cilium-building genes between Patched and the target genes.
  - name: "Fluorescent tagging and imaging of the cilium"
    blurb: >-
      Fuse a protein to a fluorescent tag, or stain it with an antibody, and photograph cells
      together with a marker for the cilium. Comparing cells with and without Hedgehog shows where
      each pathway protein sits and how the signal moves it.

It is not limited to cells that touch the midline, because it reaches cells many cell diameters away. It is a secreted signal and not a kinase. And it comes from the midline, not from every cell in equal amounts."

    rationale: >-
      Patched restrains Smoothened without holding it: one Patched can keep many Smoothened
      molecules off, probably by keeping a sterol away from them. Binding Smoothened and plugging

      Carrying Hedgehog to the surface for release describes Dispatched, which works in the cell
      that sends the signal. Phosphorylating Gli is the work of PKA, CK1 and GSK3, and Patched is
      not a kinase.

  - stem: "A cell that is receiving no Hedgehog is treated with a drug that blocks PKA. What is the most likely effect on Gli?"
    choices:
      - "More Gli is cut to the repressor form"
      - "Less Gli is cut to the repressor form"
      - "Gli is destroyed whole by the proteasome"
      - "Gli is unchanged, as PKA acts on Patched"
    answer: 1
    rationale: >-
      PKA puts the first phosphates on Gli, and those phosphates lead to ubiquitin tagging and
      partial cutting, so blocking PKA means less repressor is made. More cutting is what you
      would predict if you carried over the activating role PKA has in the cAMP cascade, but here
      PKA is a brake. Destruction of the whole protein is not the answer either, because blocking
      a kinase that marks Gli for the proteasome would not send more Gli there. PKA does not act
      on Patched. It acts on Gli, after Smoothened, so Gli does change.

## A lipid-anchored protein becomes a morphogen

The pathway's odd names come from Christiane Nüsslein-Volhard and Eric Wieschaus's 1979 fruit-fly screen at EMBL Heidelberg. Breaking embryonic genes at random, they found a larva lacking smooth cuticle—spiny from end to end—and named its gene *hedgehog*. Grouped with it in their 1980 paper were *patched*, *cubitus interruptus* and *wingless* (fly Wnt), before anyone knew which encoded the signal, receptor or transcription factor. Going deeper outlines the screen's scale.

The newly made Hedgehog precursor cuts itself in two: its back part acts once as an enzyme, breaking the chain and joining **cholesterol** to the front part's new end. A second enzyme adds **palmitate**, a 16-carbon fatty acid, to the other end. That dual-lipidated front part is the signal.

<LessonFigure src="/figures/hedgehog-and-cilium/hedgehog-two-lipids-and-release.webp" width={1200} height={423} alt="AI-generated illustration: a three-step strip showing the Hedgehog chain as two blocks, the back part cutting the chain and joining cholesterol to the front part, then a second enzyme adding palmitate so the front part carries a lipid at each end, with an inset showing the membrane protein Dispatched letting the lipid-anchored signal go." caption="Self-cleavage attaches cholesterol and a second enzyme adds palmitate, anchoring Hedgehog to membranes until Dispatched releases it." />

As with Ras in [the lesson on lipid anchors](/lessons/lipid-anchors-and-addresses/), both lipids tether Hedgehog to a membrane, so sending cells need a membrane protein, **Dispatched**, to release it. How freed Hedgehog then crosses tissue is unsettled—lipoprotein particles, multimeric clusters and thin cell extensions have all been proposed—but it clearly reaches many cell diameters away in a declining gradient.

That makes Hedgehog a **morphogen**: a signal spreading from a source that draws different responses at different concentrations. Mammals have three Hedgehog genes; the best studied is **Sonic hedgehog**, named after the video-game character. Made along the limb bud's future little-finger edge and the neural tube's ventral midline, it specifies digits and spinal neurons as transcription-factor networks read both dose and exposure time.

**Predict.** In a simplified exponential gradient, a morphogen starts at 8 arbitrary units and halves every 10 micrometres; cells above 2 units adopt fate A (to 20 micrometres). If source output doubles to 16 units with the same halving distance, does fate A reach 40 or 30 micrometres?

<summary>Check your prediction</summary>

**30 micrometres.** From 16 units, levels fall to 8 at 10 micrometres, 4 at 20, and 2 at 30. Under exponential decay, doubling source output shifts every boundary outward by one halving distance, not twofold.

</details>

## A catalytic brake and a transcription factor cut in two

The first film animated the double-negative switch; here are its protein parts and biochemical mechanism.

**Patched** crosses the membrane twelve times and resembles a transporter, related to bacterial pumps and **NPC1** (which moves cholesterol out of human lysosomes). **Smoothened** crosses seven times, like Unit 3 G-protein-coupled receptors. In logic, Hedgehog releases a car's handbrake rather than pressing an accelerator—which is why losing Patched mimics constant Hedgehog.

Patched acts as a brake in logic, not by clamping Smoothened: one Patched keeps many Smoothened molecules off. The working model is that Smoothened needs a sterol, most likely cholesterol itself, to switch on, and that Patched uses its transporter-like body to keep that sterol out of reach. Cryo-electron microscopy shows sterol-like densities in both proteins and Hedgehog's palmitate plugging into Patched. What Patched moves and what powers it remain unsettled.

**Predict.** Compare a 1:1 physical clamp with catalytic sterol removal. If a cell makes fivefold more Smoothened while Patched stays constant, in which model is Smoothened more likely to activate without Hedgehog?

<details>
<summary>Check your prediction</summary>

The **1:1 clamp** model. Excess Smoothened would outnumber Patched and escape restraint, whereas depleting a shared sterol pool lets one Patched silence many Smoothened molecules.

</details>

### What Smoothened controls: one protein, two opposite outputs

The first film animated Gli trimming; here are the kinases behind it and how vertebrates split the work.

The pathway ends at **Gli** in vertebrates and **Cubitus interruptus (Ci)** in flies. Flies use Ci for both roles; mammals divide them across **Gli3** (mostly repressor), **Gli2** (mostly activator) and **Gli1** (a direct target gene).

When Smoothened is off, [PKA](/lessons/camp-and-pka/) adds priming phosphates to full-length Gli, letting **CK1** and **GSK3** add more. An E3 ligase ubiquitin-tags the phosphorylated cluster for the [proteasome](/lessons/ubiquitin-and-proteolysis/), which degrades only the back activating domain and releases the front DNA-binding fragment as a repressor silencing target genes.

**Predict.** In Unit 3, PKA drove hormone responses forward, and in Wnt, CK1 and GSK3 target β-catenin for complete destruction. Without Hedgehog, how does blocking PKA change repression—and what would differ if the proteasome destroyed Gli completely?

<details>
<summary>Check your prediction</summary>

Blocking PKA prevents priming phosphorylation, so *less* Gli repressor forms and repression weakens: resting PKA acts as a brake here. Complete destruction (as with β-catenin) would leave no repressor fragment to actively silence target genes.

</details>

With Smoothened on, priming stops. Newly made Gli escapes trimming and enters the nucleus whole as an activator. Even then a downstream brake, **SUFU**, binds full-length Gli and restrains it. Unlike Notch, which cuts the receptor to free an activator, Hedgehog prevents a cut on the transcription factor.

How vertebrate Smoothened silences PKA leads straight to the organelle where these proteins meet—and to why downstream steps decide what mutations and drugs do.

## In vertebrates the pathway runs inside an antenna

The second film showed Patched exiting and Smoothened entering the **primary cilium**—a single non-motile microtubule-based projection with its own membrane, built by **intraflagellar transport (IFT)** motor shuttles. Here is the evidence placing Hedgehog there, and the tissue paradox that proves both Gli outputs require it.

In 2003, Kathryn Anderson's laboratory at Sloan-Kettering identified two mutagenized mouse lines with neural-tube and limb defects mimicking broken Sonic hedgehog signalling. Both carried damaged IFT genes, and crosses placed them after Patched and before target genes—without revealing where in the cell they acted.

In 2005, Kevin Corbit in Jeremy Reiter's laboratory at UCSF tracked tagged Smoothened into the primary cilium upon pathway activation. Mutating Smoothened's ciliary targeting stretch kept it in the cell body, and it could no longer pass the signal on.

Inside the cilium, two mechanisms link Smoothened to PKA: without Hedgehog, ciliary **GPR161** keeps cAMP and PKA high until Hedgehog clears it; active Smoothened also uses an internal decoy sequence to block PKA's catalytic site. How these two steps fit together is still being worked out.

**Predict.** Both Gli activator maturation and Gli3 repressor trimming require the primary cilium. Predict how a cilia-less mouse embryo fares in the ventral neural tube (activator-dependent) versus the limb bud (where Gli3 repressor restricts digit number).

<details>
<summary>Check your prediction</summary>

It shows opposite defects: *too little* Hedgehog response in the ventral neural tube (ventral cell types lost without activator) and signs of *too much* in the limb bud (extra digits without Gli3 repressor).

</details>

## Blocking Smoothened deforms an embryo and treats a tumour

As before, switching off Patched lets Smoothened keep Gli whole and active—and because downstream steps decide, a drug's target determines which faults it can override.

In the 1950s, Idaho ranchers reported lambs born with one central eye. US Department of Agriculture investigators traced it to corn lily (*Veratrum californicum*) eaten around day 14 of pregnancy and purified **cyclopamine**. Mouse embryos lacking Sonic hedgehog show the same single eye: cyclopamine binds Smoothened and blocks the midline Sonic hedgehog signal that splits the early forebrain and its single eye field into left and right. In humans, failure of that split is **holoprosencephaly**, and inheriting one faulty *SHH* copy is one known cause.

The opposite fault—constitutive activation—drives **basal cell carcinoma**, the most common human cancer. The large majority of these tumours run the pathway with no Hedgehog present: most carry mutations inactivating *PTCH1* (human Patched), while a smaller share carry Patched-resistant Smoothened. Most people with **Gorlin syndrome** inherit one faulty *PTCH1* copy in every cell and develop many basal cell carcinomas starting young.

**Predict.** In Gorlin syndrome, every skin cell inherits one broken *PTCH1* copy, yet tumours appear in scattered patches rather than across all skin at once. Why—and once a tumour forms, why can a Smoothened blocker shut its pathway down?

<details>
<summary>Check your prediction</summary>

The remaining normal *PTCH1* copy still supplies enough Patched brake until a skin cell loses that second copy too. Because Smoothened lies downstream of Patched, nothing upstream matters once Smoothened is blocked, so the drug silences the tumour.

</details>

**Vismodegib** binds among Smoothened's seven helices and holds it off. Approved in the United States in 2012, it treats adults with metastatic basal cell carcinoma, or locally advanced tumours that recurred after surgery or cannot be treated by surgery when radiation is not an option either. Tumours can escape through a Smoothened mutation blocking drug binding or a downstream fault—and the label repeats the lambs' warning against pregnancy.

### Design problem

Hedgehog switches off Patched, and Patched switches off Smoothened. Predict whether the pathway is on or off, with and without Hedgehog, in four cells: no Patched, no Smoothened, neither, and a Patched-resistant Smoothened. Then say which tumour a Smoothened blocker treats: one that lost Patched, or one that lost a brake acting after Smoothened.

<details>
<summary>One way to do it</summary>

Ask one question per cell: what state is Smoothened in?

- **No Patched.** Nothing restrains Smoothened: on with or without Hedgehog.
- **No Smoothened.** Nothing passes the signal on, so Gli is cut to repressor: off both ways.
- **Neither.** Off both ways. Without Smoothened, missing its brake changes nothing—the later step decides (**epistasis**).
- **Patched-resistant Smoothened.** On both ways.

A Smoothened blocker shuts down the Patched-loss tumour because Smoothened is free and downstream of Patched. A tumour lacking a later brake—such as **SUFU**, which holds full-length Gli in the cytoplasm—stays on regardless.

</details>

## Going deeper

### How the fly screen worked and its size

In 1979, Nüsslein-Volhard and Wieschaus fed male flies a DNA-damaging chemical and bred nearly 27,000 lines, each carrying one damaged chromosome, until embryos carried two broken copies of the same gene. Healthy larvae have segmental belts of tiny spines separated by smooth cuticle, so missing, doubled or reversed belts revealed lost patterning genes. In his Nobel lecture Wieschaus reported 580 describable mutations falling into 139 genes; their 1980 paper described fifteen.

### A cilium-based branch in flies

A cilium-based branch of Hedgehog signalling was reported in 2014 in ciliated fly olfactory neurons. Why vertebrates route the same brake and switch through this compartment is not known.

### How cyclopamine was tied to Smoothened

In 1996, mouse embryos lacking Sonic hedgehog were found to have the same single eye seen in affected lambs. In 1998, Philip Beachy's and Henk Roelink's laboratories showed cyclopamine blocks Sonic hedgehog responses, and in 2002 Beachy's group showed it binds Smoothened directly.
