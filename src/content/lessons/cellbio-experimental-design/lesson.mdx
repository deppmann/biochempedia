    story: >-
      In 2018 Stanley Lazic, Charlie Clarke-Williams and Marcus Munafò published a paper in PLoS
      Biology that asks what exactly N is in cell culture and animal experiments. Their answer
      rests on three kinds of unit. The biological unit is the entity the conclusion is about. The experimental unit is the entity randomly and independently
      assigned to a condition. The observational unit is the entity on which measurements are
      taken. The sample size, N, is the number of experimental units. Measuring more cells adds
      observational units but no experimental units, so when a drug is added to a whole well, the
      well is the unit and its cells are subsamples of it. Genuine replication, they argued,
      needs the treatment to be applied independently to each unit, and repeating a procedure on
      separate days gives information that extra wells on a single day cannot. The underlying
      ideas are older than the paper. What it supplied was the vocabulary of the dish, the well
      and the culture day, so that a cell biologist can hold a figure legend up against it and
      ask whether the N reported is the N that matters.

    blurb: >-
      Cells carry two fluorescent reporters: red mKO2-Cdt1 in G0 and G1, green mAG-Geminin from
      S phase until mitosis. Filming the same cells and following each one separates entry into
      S and G2 from completion of a cycle with an observed division.
  - name: "EdU incorporation"
    blurb: >-
      EdU, a thymidine analogue, is built into DNA by cells replicating during the labelling
      window and is then detected in fixed cells. It reports DNA replication, a distinct endpoint
      from reporter colour or division.
  - name: "TOPdGFP transcriptional reporter"
    blurb: >-
      A destabilized GFP is placed under control of TCF-binding sites, so that its level reports
      beta-catenin-dependent transcription. Comparing it across conditions asks whether a
      treatment engaged the Wnt pathway at the level of gene expression.
  - name: "Experimental-unit analysis and SuperPlots"
    blurb: >-
      Identify the thing independently assigned to treatment, summarize each unit, and do the
      statistics on those summaries. A SuperPlot shows the cells as dots and each run's mean as a
      large symbol, so the true N is visible in the figure.

      Each run supplies one independently treated dish per condition, so N is three per

  - stem: "After stretch, a monolayer has more green Fucci cells. Which further measurement would best show that more cells completed division?"
    choices:
      - "Measuring the fraction of red cells at one late time point"
      - "Tracking single cells from red through green to a division"
      - "Measuring the total area covered by the monolayer"
      - "Measuring the mean green intensity of each cell"
    answer: 1
    rationale: >-
      Tracking each cell through the reporter sequence to an observed division directly shows
      completion here. Mean green intensity reports how much Geminin reporter a cell holds, not
      whether it divided. A single late red fraction cannot tell cells that divided and returned
      to G1 from cells that never left it. Monolayer area can change through spreading or
      migration without any division.
  - stem: "A student treats cells with one kinase inhibitor and sees a phenotype. Which added experiment most directly tests whether inhibiting the drug's intended target causes the phenotype?"
    choices:
      - "Use a second dish of the same drug on the same day"
      - "Image more cells per dish to tighten the error bars"
      - "Repeat the drug at a higher dose to strengthen the effect"
      - "Deplete the target genetically, then restore it with rescue"
    answer: 3
    rationale: >-
      An independent genetic perturbation that removes the target, followed by rescue with a
      version the perturbation cannot touch, tests the target rather than the drug. A higher

      for example the fraction of initially red cells tracked to an observed division, and
      include the region of the result space that discriminates neither. Use a factorial design

The film's opening dish was one experiment per condition, not 600: every cell shared its dish, so the reported p-value—a measure of incompatibility with a specified no-effect model—used the wrong units. The running case below uses MDCK cells—Madin-Darby canine kidney cells grown as an epithelial sheet—to ask whether stretch makes cells proliferate. All findings below are from those cultured cells.

The film gave the three-lock map. The rest of the page tests where two models disagree, then asks whether the endpoint, independent runs and controls support the claim; the final design brings those decisions back together.

## Make the models disagree

The film's Model A said stretch alone is sufficient for division; Model B said stretch starts the cycle but a Wnt signal is needed to finish. Now turn that disagreement into a numerical test. **Quiescent** cells are not actively cycling.

Wnt proteins are secreted signals. Here, Wnt3A stabilizes beta-catenin, which switches on target genes; the [lesson on Wnt signaling](/lessons/wnt-signaling/) covers the pathway. Because the models agree at S-phase entry and disagree at division, completion is the primary endpoint.

Before collecting an image, write each model's numerical prediction for the chosen endpoint and the range that would favour neither. An experiment that can only confirm is not a test. The [lesson on experiments that distinguish](/lessons/experiments-that-distinguish/) develops the same logic.

**Predict.** In a two-by-two design, Wnt3A alone raises completed divisions as much as stretch plus Wnt3A. What happens to Model B's claim that stretch and Wnt cooperate?

<details>
<summary>Check your prediction</summary>

It fails Model B's prediction. If Wnt3A alone matches stretch plus Wnt3A, stretch adds nothing in Wnt3A medium. Model B instead predicts that stretch adds at least 10 percentage points more in Wnt3A medium than in control medium. The result points to Wnt3A adding divisions on its own, not to stretch starting the cycle and Wnt finishing it. This is why the Wnt3A-alone arm is required.

</details>

## Read the endpoint before interpreting it

The film's Fucci picture was the simple case; here is the fuller one. **Fucci** means fluorescent ubiquitination-based cell-cycle indicator. In these MDCK cells, red mKO2-Cdt1 marks G0 and G1, while green mAG-Geminin marks cells from S phase, when DNA is copied, until mitosis (see [cell-cycle control](/lessons/cell-cycle-control/)). The green signal can switch on a little before or after DNA replication starts: a green cell has roughly entered S, while only the path to an observed division directly shows completion here.

<LessonFigure src="/figures/cellbio-experimental-design/fucci-entry-vs-completion.webp" width={1200} height={640} alt="AI-generated illustration: a left-to-right strip of one epithelial cell followed over time, first with a red nucleus, then a green nucleus, then rounding up to divide, then two pale daughter cells, with a gold arrow beneath marking the path a tracked cell follows and a small inset contrasting a single snapshot with tracking." caption="A tracked Fucci record keeps one cell's identity attached to its color changes and eventual outcome." />

Benham-Pyle and colleagues found in 2016 that about 10% of cells in unstretched, quiescent MDCK-II monolayers were Geminin-positive. Strain alone increased cells in S and G2—the interval before mitosis—without increasing cells tracked from G1 through S to division. Wnt3A alone did not move quiescent cells into S phase in this preparation. Strain plus Wnt3A raised mitotic entry above either input alone. Thus more green meant more cells in S and G2, not more divisions within the 24 hours watched.

EdU supplies a separate endpoint: it is a thymidine analogue that cells build into DNA during the labelling window. Because the Fucci transition is approximate, agreement between green Fucci and EdU supports entry into S without turning either measure into a division count.

**Predict.** Green cells start to rise only after about 8 hours and keep rising through the 24 hours watched. Can that 24-hour window rule out extra divisions later?

<details>
<summary>Check your prediction</summary>

No. A cell that enters S at 20 hours cannot finish S, G2 and mitosis by 24 hours. A negative division result holds only for the period watched; the window must cover the delay plus the time S, G2 and mitosis take.

</details>

Area creates a second endpoint trap. Suppose two circular MDCK tissues, 1.7 and 3.4 mm across—the sizes Heinrich and colleagues used in 2020—each have an edge moving outward at a constant 30 micrometres per hour for an idealized 10 hours. Their radii move from 850 to 1,150 micrometres and from 1,700 to 2,000.

**Predict.** With the same edge speed, which tissue has the larger percentage area increase? Does that prove faster proliferation?

<details>
<summary>Check your prediction</summary>

The smaller tissue. Its area ratio is (1150/850)² = 1.83, an 83% increase; the larger tissue gives (2000/1700)² = 1.38, a 38% increase. No division rate appears in the calculation. Geometry alone creates the difference, so percent area growth does not prove proliferation.

</details>

Count nuclei, as [microscopy and images](/lessons/microscopy-and-images/) describes, and track divisions. Heinrich and colleagues measured tissue area and cell number and found different changes: over 46 hours, small tissues grew about 6.4-fold in area but about 9.2-fold in cell number.

## Read repeatability at the independent-unit level

The film collapsed hundreds of cell dots into the dish that received treatment. Formally, the **experimental unit** is the thing independently assigned to a condition: a dish, stretch device or mouse. The **observational unit** is what is measured: a cell or field. N is the number of experimental units (Lazic and colleagues, 2018). Treating cells that share a dish as independent replicates is **pseudoreplication**.

Repeating the whole procedure on separate days supplies information that extra cells from one dish cannot. Lazic and colleagues call choosing days rather than wells a scientific judgement because it asks whether an effect survives a fresh run. They also distinguish the **biological unit**, the entity the conclusion concerns; with one cell line, the conclusion is about that line.

### Worked problem: six hundred cells, three runs

Here is a made-up data set, invented for teaching. Each of three independent days has one control dish and one treated dish, with 200 cells scored per dish for EdU.

| Run | Control (% EdU-positive) | Treated (% EdU-positive) | Treated − control |
| --- | ---: | ---: | ---: |
| 1 | 20 | 34 | +14 |
| 2 | 25 | 27 | +2 |
| 3 | 15 | 28 | +13 |

Pooling erases the run labels. Runs 1 and 3 show large differences; run 2 barely moves. Summarizing each dish preserves that disagreement, and the dish-level interval spans a small decrease to a large increase; the calculation is in Going deeper.

**Predict.** The paired analysis gives p = 0.13. Which statement is justified: “there is no effect,” or “these runs leave the effect uncertain”?

<details>
<summary>Check your prediction</summary>

These runs leave the effect uncertain. A non-significant result does not show no effect, and the wide interval makes the uncertainty visible. Scoring 2,000 cells per dish would shrink some counting noise, but N would remain three and run-to-run variation would remain. More independent runs address that uncertainty.

## Controls that rule out the boring explanations

The film's three locks are necessary, not sufficient: controls test whether a dull alternative could produce the result.

- **Handling:** assemble, mount and image unstretched devices identically, or “stretch” includes every handling difference.
- **Medium:** match how the medium was prepared; a pathway readout then tests whether the intended signal was engaged.
- **Drug:** match the vehicle—the drug's solvent without drug—at the same concentration. A concentration copied from a paper is a condition, not a measure of potency.
- **Observer:** fix positivity thresholds before analysis, score blind to condition and keep thresholds the same across conditions, as the 2016 authors did.
- **Stretch:** measure the strain cells actually feel and analyze only the strained region. Do not merge devices that impose different strains.

A pathway readout asks whether the intended signal was engaged. TOPdGFP is a destabilized GFP driven by beta-catenin-responsive sites; it reports beta-catenin-dependent transcription ([hacking the cell](/lessons/hacking-the-cell/) covers reporters).

**Predict.** One inhibitor produces the expected phenotype. What addition best tests whether inhibiting its intended target caused that phenotype?

<details>
<summary>Check your prediction</summary>

Use an independent genetic perturbation of the target, then restore the target with a **rescue** version the perturbation cannot touch. A second dish of the same drug or a higher dose still carries the same off-target concern. See [CRISPR and functional genomics](/lessons/crispr-and-functional-genomics/) and [drugs against a part](/lessons/drugs-against-a-part/).

</details>

## Putting it together

Now the prediction, endpoint and independent unit return as one design, with controls added to test alternative explanations.

### Design problem

A student claims "stretch makes epithelial cells proliferate" from more green Fucci cells after stretching an MDCK monolayer. Redesign the experiment.

<details>
<summary>One way to do it</summary>

**Design.** A 2 × 2 factorial—every combination of no stretch or stretch with control (parental L-cell) or Wnt3A-conditioned medium. Run all four arms on each day, assigning devices to arms at random within the day. N is the number of independent days, chosen from a pilot's run-to-run variation.

**Primary endpoint, fixed in advance.** The fraction of cells red at the start that are tracked through green (S/G2) to an observed division within a set window, long enough to cover the delay before S entry plus the time S, G2 and M take. Call it Q.

**Predictions, with invented thresholds (they are teaching choices, not values from any paper).**
- Model A: Q(stretch) - Q(control) is at least 10 percentage points.
- Model B: Q(stretch) - Q(control) is at most 2 points, and the stretch effect in Wnt3A medium, Q(stretch + Wnt3A) - Q(Wnt3A), is at least 10 points larger than the stretch effect in control medium. The Wnt3A-alone arm is what separates "Wnt3A is needed to finish" from "Wnt3A adds divisions on its own"; in the 2016 study, Wnt3A alone briefly raised mitoses in unstretched monolayers.
- Inconclusive: a stretch-alone difference between 2 and 10 points, or confidence intervals wide enough to fit both models.

**Secondary endpoints.** Nuclear counts and density, EdU, TOPdGFP, and cell loss, each reported separately.

**Controls.** Identically handled unstretched devices; L-cell medium; vehicle for any drug; fixed thresholds; blinded scoring; measured strain. For molecular claims, a genetic perturbation of beta-catenin signaling with rescue alongside any inhibitor.

**Analysis.** Day-paired device means, shown as a SuperPlot.

**Compare with the published result.** The evidence above fits Model B's pattern in quiescent canine MDCK-II monolayers held under static strain in culture. Whether the same holds with another starting state, another kind of stretch, another epithelium or in tissue is a separate experiment.

</details>

## Going deeper

The film and the first pass used the simple case. These details show where reporter timing, arithmetic, pathway tools and biological context limit that picture.

### Reporter limits and biological scope

Red Fucci does not distinguish G0 from G1, so red alone cannot prove that a cell is quiescent. The construct also has a dark period after mitosis, when neither color is bright, so a snapshot undercounts cells that have just divided. These limits are why reporter fractions are not rates and why the main design tracks individual cells.

Other work in MDCK cells points the other way: stretch alone pushed cells paused in G2 into mitosis within about an hour through the channel Piezo1 (Gudipaty and colleagues, 2017). The answer depends on the cells' starting state and the kind of stretch, so a design must state both.

### The paired calculation

Pooling the invented data gives 120 of 600 control cells and 178 of 600 treated cells: 20.0% and 29.7%, a 9.7-point difference. If all cells are wrongly treated as independent, the standard error is

> **SE = sqrt(0.20 × 0.80 / 600 + 0.297 × 0.703 / 600) = 0.0248, about 2.5 points**

and z is about 3.9, which looks decisive. With the dish as the unit, the last column of the table has mean 29 / 3 = 9.67 points. Its deviations from the mean are 4.33, −7.67 and 3.33; their squared sum is 88.7. Dividing by n − 1 gives variance 44.3, SD 6.66 and SE 6.66 / sqrt(3) = 3.84. Thus t = 2.51 with 2 degrees of freedom, and the two-sided p is about 0.13. Using the critical t value 4.303, the 95% interval is 9.67 ± 16.5, or about −6.9 to 26.2 points.

### Pathway controls and device details

Wnt3A-conditioned medium comes from mouse L cells engineered to make Wnt3A. Parental L-cell medium matches its preparation, but cannot guarantee that Wnt3A is the only difference; a Wnt-pathway readout or blocker tests whether the pathway did the work.

Benham-Pyle and colleagues compared SU6656 at 10 µM, which inhibits Src-family kinases, with a DMSO vehicle control and found that it blocked strain-induced EdU incorporation. A casein kinase I inhibitor, which allows beta-catenin to build up, raised mitotic entry with strain; iCRT3, which blocks beta-catenin-dependent transcription, prevented that rise. The authors flagged possible off-target effects of the casein kinase I inhibitor, so one inhibitor does not prove its nominal target.

The two devices in the 2016 study reached different strain maxima, about 8.5% and 15%. Their results should not be merged.
