## Explain: what rocks and fossils record

<WordBank terms={['fossil', 'fossil record', 'relative dating', 'radioactive dating', 'comparative genomics', 'common ancestor', 'homologous structure', 'vestigial structure', 'embryology', 'theory']} />

A **fossil** is the preserved remains or traces of a living thing from the past, such as a bone, shell, tooth or footprint. The **fossil record** is all the fossils found so far, arranged by age. It shows that kinds of life appeared, changed and often went extinct over time.

The record is incomplete. Most dead plants and animals rot or are eaten before they can fossilize. Fossils form more often when a body is buried quickly in mud or sand under water, so sea animals are better recorded than forest animals. Life has existed for about 3.5 billion years or more, and most of it left no fossils.

**Relative dating** puts rocks and fossils in order from older to younger, without an age in years. Sedimentary rock builds up in layers, like a stack of mail on a table. In an undisturbed stack, the bottom layer was laid down first. So a fossil in a lower layer is older than one higher up. The analogy breaks where Earth movements have folded layers over, flipped them or broken them along faults, so geologists check for that first. Tilting alone does not change the order of the layers.

**Radioactive dating** uses radioactive atoms in certain minerals to estimate a rock’s age in years. These atoms change into other atoms at a steady rate. Measuring how much starting atom remains and how much product has built up gives the time since the mineral formed. Sometimes dated volcanic layers lie above and below a fossil, so the fossil’s age falls between the two dates. More often there is no volcanic layer nearby. Then scientists match the fossil’s layer to dated rocks elsewhere using index fossils, the remains of species that lived for a known span of time.

Different atoms suit different spans of time. Carbon-14 works only for remains up to about 50,000 years old. Fossil whales millions of years old are far too old for it.

## Explain: what DNA comparisons show

DNA is passed from parents to offspring. When it is copied, small changes sometimes happen, and some are passed on. **Comparative genomics** is the comparison of the whole DNA, or genome, of different species. A **common ancestor** is an ancestral population from which two or more species descended. Two species that split from a common ancestor recently have had less time to collect differences. So, for matching stretches of DNA, more similar sequences usually mean a more recent common ancestor.

In 2005, a team compared the genome of a chimpanzee named Clint with the human genome. Across about 2.4 billion lined-up bases, 1.23 of every 100 differed.

> percent matching = 100% − percent different = 100% − 1.23% = 98.77%, or about 98.8%

That number counts single-base differences in matching regions only. It leaves out other kinds of difference, so a “percent similar” depends on what was compared.

DNA also tested where whales belong. In 1999, a team compared pieces of DNA that had been inserted at particular spots in the genomes of whales and hoofed mammals. Species sharing an insertion at the same spot likely inherited it from one ancestor. The pattern placed whales among the even-toed hoofed mammals, grouped with hippos. This does not mean hippos were whale ancestors. It means whales and hippos share a common ancestor.

Humans did not come from chimpanzees; both descend from a common ape ancestor.

<Support min={2}>**Key idea:** Species with more similar DNA in matching regions usually share a more recent common ancestor.</Support>

## Explain: many fields, one history

Each field below could have contradicted the others. For whales, they agree.

| Field | Whale evidence |
|---|---|
| Paleontology and geology | Legged whale fossils from Pakistan and Egypt, in layers dated tens of millions of years old |
| Anatomy | Small hip bones inside living whales |
| Embryology | Pantropical spotted dolphin embryos grow hind-limb buds that disappear |
| Genetics | DNA insertions group whales with hippos |
| Biochemistry | Whales share the nearly universal genetic code (the same three-base codes for amino acids) with other life. This supports the common ancestry of all life, but it cannot show which animals are the closest relatives of whales |

A **homologous structure** is a body part in different species that comes from the same structure in a common ancestor. A whale flipper, a human arm and a bat wing hold the same set of bones, arranged differently.

A **vestigial structure** is a reduced body part that has lost all or most of the job it did in ancestors. Vestigial does not mean useless. In male whales, these bones anchor muscles used in mating, as anatomists described long ago. A 2014 study found that, across whales, dolphins and porpoises, the hip bones were larger in species where males have larger testes for their body size.

<LessonFigure src="/figures/evidence-for-evolution/whale-hip-bones.webp" width={1200} height={800} alt="AI-generated illustration: a living whale in side view with a small pair of hip bones shown deep inside its lower body and no legs attached. An inset zooms in on one hip bone with a muscle band attached." caption="A vestigial structure: living whales carry small hip bones that hold up no legs, yet in males they still anchor muscles used in mating." />

**Embryology** is the study of how an organism develops before birth or hatching. Pantropical spotted dolphin embryos grow hind-limb buds that stop growing and break down around the fifth week. The embryo is not replaying its ancestors; it starts a limb and then stops.

<LessonFigure src="/figures/evidence-for-evolution/dolphin-limb-buds.webp" width={1200} height={800} alt="AI-generated illustration: two simple outlines of a dolphin embryo. A **theory** in science is a well-tested explanation supported by many lines of evidence. The theory of evolution explains fossils, anatomy, embryos and DNA with one history of common descent. For natural selection, one of the main ways that change happens, and for how new species form, see [Natural Selection and New Species](/lessons/natural-selection-and-speciation/).

### Design problem

Some people say a whale’s small hip bones are leftovers with no job. Plan a study, using museum skeletons, to test whether the hip bones are linked to mating in male whales.

<details>
<summary>One way to do it</summary>

- **Question:** In male whales, do species with relatively larger testes also have relatively larger hip bones?
- **Independent variable:** relative testes size of each species, from published records.
- **Dependent variable:** hip bone size and shape, measured the same way on every skeleton.
- **Constants:** adult males only, the same measuring tool and method, and every size divided by body length, so large and small species can be compared.
- **Control:** measure a rib from each skeleton too. If ribs also track testes size, the link is not special to the hip bones and may come from overall body size instead of mating.
- **Sample size and repeated trials:** many species and several skeletons per species; measure each bone more than once. The 2014 study used 130 whales, dolphins and porpoises from 29 species.
- **Ethics:** museum skeletons harm no living animal.

If hip bones track testes size and ribs do not, the bones likely still serve mating. This is a pattern, not proof of cause.

## Going deeper

### How the two whale skeletons were dated

The ages of the *Maiacetus* and *Basilosaurus* skeletons in this lesson come from tiny fossils of sea plankton in the same rock layers. Those plankton layers were matched to rocks elsewhere that other radioactive atoms had dated.

### What the 1.23 percent leaves out

The same study also found about five million places where short pieces of DNA had been added or lost, which the 1.23% leaves out. A 2025 study of complete ape genomes compared regions that earlier work had skipped.

### The 2014 hip-bone study in numbers

The 2014 study measured hip bones from 130 whales, dolphins and porpoises of 29 species. In species where males have larger testes for their body size, the hip bones were larger too, but a rib measured as a check was not.
